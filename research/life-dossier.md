# Life Cycle — research dossier

Researched 22 November 2026. Scope: the twelve requested exhibits and two further topics. Existing exhibit ids are preserved; `life.development` is the proposed id for Growing Up. Only this dossier is changed. The SOURCES block is a proposal for the registry, not an edit to it.

## Reading this dossier

- Bracketed keys refer to `content/sources.yaml` or the new entries below. All 170 existing keys were checked, including titles and identifiers, before choosing new keys.
- **established** = directly documented and reasonably secure as qualified; **supported** = a particular experiment, limited sample, or inference; **debated** = an unresolved explanation. Confidence never licenses extrapolation to another species.
- FIGURES are candidate numerical fact cards, not illustrations. Counts, ranges, maxima, experimental treatments and sample sizes are identified explicitly. A laboratory maximum is not life expectancy.
- Hatching means leaving the egg envelope; emergence means leaving the silk sac. Neither is automatically the beginning of an author's developmental clock.
- Instar numbering differs among authors. Downes calls the stage before the first true moult the **postembryo**, and the following stage the **first instar**; other accounts call emerging spiderlings second instars. Keep each source's convention.
- Scientific names identify every biological observation. TERMS marked **GENERAL SPIDER** are definitions, not evidence of a particular widow's performance.
- No measured *Latrodectus* ballooning-distance record, universal egg-to-dispersal survival percentage, or genus-wide senescence curve was verified. Those gaps are retained rather than filled with general-spider numbers.

### Priority qualifications for the current exhibits

1. Preserve `life.egg-sac`, `life.ballooning`, `life.molting`, and `life.courtship`. Proposed new names below are editorial suggestions.
2. Replace an unqualified “Being eaten pays” with the demonstrated *L. hasselti* paternity benefits; distinguish fatal first-copulation cannibalism from surviving to inseminate both sides. [andrade1996; snow2005]
3. Do not convert the current egg-count bands into universal averages: measured means differ among samples. [kaston1970; ebeling1975; vetter2012]
4. Do not claim automatic fertility decline with sac order: the redback study specifically found no consistent trend. [downes1985]
5. Do not claim that lost widow legs always regenerate, or that adult females never moult. Northern widow experiments and Crimean Mediterranean widows provide important exceptions. [randall1981; nadolny2019]
6. Do not explain redback monogyny as cessation of sperm production: adult redbacks and western widows can replenish their palps. [modanu2013]

## 1. life.egg-sac — Many Broods from Stored Sperm

**Taxa studied:** *L. hesperus*, *L. mactans*, *L. variolus*, *L. geometricus*, *L. hasselti*. [kaston1970; vetter2012; downes1985]

### FIGURES

| 276.1 ± 97.2 / 143.7 ± 61.6 | eggs per sac | A California comparison | *L. hesperus* / *L. geometricus*; reported means ± dispersion, n=10 / 30 field sacs; p. 212 | [vetter2012] |

| 3.77 ± 0.40 / 1.10 ± 0.10 | sacs per female | Feeding changes sac production | *L. hasselti*; fed / starved for six weeks, laboratory means ± SE | [stoltz2010b] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- *L. hesperus* and *L. geometricus* differed in both egg number and egg diameter in the California comparison. **supported** [vetter2012]
- *L. mactans*, *L. hesperus* and *L. variolus* eggs are usually cream to yellow. **established** [kaston1970; ebeling1975]
- *L. hasselti* females can use sperm from one mating to produce successive fertile sacs. **established** [mowery2022b]
- *L. hasselti* sac-production intervals depend on the female's food intake. **supported** [mowery2022b]
- Across 216 sacs, *L. hasselti* egg inviability varied without consistently increasing or decreasing across a female's successive sacs. **supported** [downes1985]
- *L. hasselti* fecundity likewise showed no consistent sac-order trend in that study. **supported** [downes1985]
- Inviable *L. hasselti* eggs were proposed as an early food resource; adaptive provisioning was not demonstrated. **debated** [downes1985]
- *L. geometricus* and *L. hesperus* sacs can contain unhatched eggs whose failure cannot be assigned to infertility alone. **supported** [vetter2012]
- Fed *L. hasselti* produced fewer spiderlings in successive sacs during another six-week experiment; offspring output does not isolate sperm viability. **supported** [stoltz2010b]
- Mated *L. hasselti* largely stopped producing sacs during food restriction and resumed production after feeding restarted. **supported** [stoltz2010b]

### TERMS

- **Fecundity:** egg production; distinct from the fraction developing successfully. [downes1985]
- **Fertility / viability:** fertilization and subsequent developmental success; a failed egg does not by itself distinguish them. [downes1985; vetter2012]
- **Sperm storage:** retention of received sperm for later fertilization; demonstrated across successive *L. hasselti* broods. [mowery2022b]

### Claims to avoid / quantitative gaps

- “Every widow sac contains 250 eggs,” “all eggs hatch,” or “the last sac is always infertile.” [vetter2012; downes1985]
- Do not infer egg colour from the colour of the silk covering. [kaston1970; ebeling1975]
- The existing 919-egg *L. mactans* record and ≥499-day *L. hesperus* storage anecdote need their original Kaston page checked before reuse: the registry's large scan could not be fully retrieved here. They are not newly verified cards in this dossier.
- Keep protein chemistry and construction details in `web.egg-sac-silk`; cross-reference storage anatomy in `body.epigynum`.

## 2. life.hatching — An Egg Opens Before the Nursery Does

**Taxa studied:** principally Townsville, Queensland *L. hasselti*; Californian *L. hesperus* and *L. geometricus* for sac contents. [downes1987; vetter2012]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 / 10 | days | Oviposition to hatching | *L. hasselti*, 25 / 30°C; combined means, 30 / 7 sacs; Table 1 | [downes1987] |
| 10 / 5 | days | Hatching to first moult | *L. hasselti*, 25 / 30°C; combined means, 12 / 7 sacs; Table 1 | [downes1987] |
| 33 / 21 | days | Oviposition to emergence | *L. hasselti*, 25 / 30°C; means, 47 / 19 intact sacs | [downes1987] |
| 26–43 / 17–24 | days | Emergence-time ranges | Same *L. hasselti* treatments, respectively; not embryonic duration | [downes1987] |
| 10–20 | spiderlings per sac | Reduced emergence | *L. hasselti*; four sacs at 30°C; another sac had no emergence | [downes1987] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- *L. hasselti* hatches inside the sac and undergoes its first true moult before emergence. **established** [downes1987]
- *L. hasselti* developed faster to emergence at 30°C than at 25°C in the Queensland experiment. **supported** [downes1987]
- Intact-sac hatching estimates for *L. hasselti* relied on changes in egg-mass movement and were less direct than observations of exposed eggs. **supported** [downes1987]
- Some *L. hasselti* sacs at 30°C yielded few spiderlings despite relatively rapid development. **supported** [downes1987]
- *L. geometricus* and *L. hesperus* sacs can contain live, mobile spiderlings before departure. **established** [vetter2012]
- Dead older spiderlings inside some *L. geometricus* and *L. hesperus* sacs suggested failure to open an exit, rather than failure to hatch. **supported** [vetter2012]

### TERMS

- **Hatching / eclosion:** shedding the egg envelope; Downes's endpoint of embryonic development. [downes1987]
- **Postembryo:** Downes's *L. hasselti* stage between hatching and the first true integument moult. [downes1987]
- **Emergence:** departure from the silk egg sac. [downes1987]

### Claims to avoid

- “Hatching takes 33 days”: that figure measures oviposition to emergence at 25°C. [downes1987]
- “More warmth always means more surviving young” or a universal 30°C developmental ceiling; Downes discusses conflicting redback results from other localities. [downes1987]
- An observed sac containing dead young does not establish starvation, cannibalism or infertility as its cause. [vetter2012]

## 3. life.siblings — A Brief, Uneasy Community

**Taxa studied:** *L. hasselti* and *L. hesperus* spiderlings; *L. geometricus* in field-sac surveys. [modanu2014; johnson2016; vetter2012]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 55 ± 3.5 / 56 ± 2.7 | % cannibalized | High / low density | *L. hasselti*; mean ± SE among cages with cannibalism, seven-day laboratory assay | [modanu2014] |
| 15–83 / 30–80 | % cannibalized | Cage-to-cage variation | Same high / low density treatments; not natural mortality rates | [modanu2014] |
| >3 | days sooner | Asynchrony hastened cannibalism | *L. hesperus*; mixed developmental treatment relative to synchronous controls | [johnson2016] |
| 2.5 | days earlier | First moult advanced | *L. hesperus*; 28 versus 24°C egg/spiderling treatment | [johnson2016] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Newly emerged *L. hasselti* siblings can share a communal web before dispersal. **established** [modanu2014]
- *L. hasselti* sibling cannibalism occurred in all but one of the experimental cages. **supported** [modanu2014]
- The tested density difference did not significantly alter the proportion of *L. hasselti* siblings cannibalized. **supported** [modanu2014]
- Family of origin predicted *L. hasselti* cannibalistic tendencies across treatments. **supported** [modanu2014]
- Experimentally creating developmental differences accelerated sibling cannibalism in *L. hesperus*. **supported** [johnson2016]
- *L. hesperus* families differed in their response to experimentally imposed developmental asynchrony. **supported** [johnson2016]
- Larger maternal egg investment delayed *L. hesperus* cannibalism in cool synchronous controls, but was a poor predictor in warm treatments. **supported** [johnson2016]
- Dead young inside *L. hesperus* and *L. geometricus* sacs had multiple possible causes in the California survey. **supported** [vetter2012]

### TERMS

- **Sibling cannibalism / siblicide:** consuming / killing siblings; killing and consumption need not be synonymous. [modanu2014; johnson2016]
- **Developmental synchrony:** similarity in siblings' developmental timing. [johnson2016]
- **Family effect:** differences among families; shared environment and maternal effects can accompany genetic differences. [modanu2014]

### Claims to avoid / quantitative gap

- Do not present cage percentages as survival from egg to dispersal, or apply them to all widow species. [modanu2014]
- “Only 1–12 of every brood survive”: no suitably qualified primary field estimate was verified here.
- Do not describe every unhatched egg or missing spiderling as an in-sac cannibalism victim; the cited experiments chiefly quantify post-hatching social encounters. [johnson2016; vetter2012]
- Group living during dispersal preparation does not establish a permanently social adult life. [modanu2014]

## 4. life.ballooning — Several Ways to Leave

**Taxa studied:** *L. hesperus* in laboratory assays; invasive *L. geometricus* tested outdoors in Israel; *L. hasselti* natural history. [johnson2015; mowery2022; forster1995]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14.3 / 1.8 | % ballooning | Day / night departures | *L. geometricus*; 21/147 versus 3/171 spiderlings, outdoor trials | [mowery2022] |
| 0.2–4.4 | m s⁻¹ | Dispersal wind speeds | *L. geometricus*; observed **rappelling and ballooning together**, not ballooning alone | [mowery2022] |
| 16.5–42.9 | °C | Dispersal temperatures | Same combined behaviours; ambient trial conditions, not long-term thermal tolerance | [mowery2022] |
| 10 | minutes | Observation window | *L. geometricus*; each outdoor trial, not flight duration | [mowery2022] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- *L. geometricus* spiderlings ballooned during both day and night, more frequently during daylight. **supported** [mowery2022]
- *L. geometricus* also dispersed by rappelling to nearby objects on silk. **established** [mowery2022]
- Night-time *L. geometricus* aerial dispersal was associated with higher wind speeds, lower temperatures and higher humidity. **supported** [mowery2022]
- Daytime *L. geometricus* ballooning was not significantly predicted by those measured weather variables. **supported** [mowery2022]
- *L. geometricus* families differed in the proportions displaying dispersal and preparatory behaviours. **supported** [mowery2022]
- Isolated *L. hesperus* spiderlings dispersed more slowly than siblings reared together. **supported** [johnson2015]
- *L. hesperus* family differences in ballooning behaviour were repeatable across time. **supported** [johnson2015]
- Young *L. hasselti* climb toward light and can build shared silk routes before dispersal. **supported** [forster1995]
- Aerial dispersal may contribute to *L. geometricus* range expansion alongside transport by people. **supported** [mowery2022]

### TERMS

- **Ballooning:** silk-supported aerial departure, observed in the named widow species. [johnson2015; mowery2022]
- **Rappelling:** silk-supported short-distance departure toward nearby structures, as scored for *L. geometricus*. [mowery2022]
- **Bridging:** crossing a gap on an anchored silk connection; **GENERAL SPIDER** definition, not a measured widow flight distance. [foelix2011]
- **Tiptoeing:** raising the abdomen and extending the legs before potential aerial departure. [mowery2022]

### Claims to avoid / quantitative gap

- “Widows fly hundreds of kilometres”: neither cited dispersal study measured completed flight distances. [johnson2015; mowery2022]
- Do not merge rappelling, bridging and ballooning into one movement, or turn the combined weather range into a ballooning threshold. [mowery2022]
- Family differences alone do not demonstrate particular dispersal genes; human-mediated transport remains relevant to invasion. [mowery2022]
- Keep juvenile web architecture in `web.growing-up` and mature male silk expression in `web.male-silk`.

## 5. life.molting — Growing by Escape

**Taxa studied:** *L. mactans* as identified by Hagstrum; *L. hesperus*, *L. hasselti*, *L. variolus*, and Crimean *L. tredecimguttatus*. [hagstrum1968; kaston1970; forster1995; randall1981; nadolny2019]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ≥2 | hours before ecdysis | Premoult twitches | *L. mactans* sensu Hagstrum; coxal movements, not total moult duration | [hagstrum1968] |
| 4 | leg pairs | Hanging attachments | Same study; legs and spinnerets attached beneath the web at the start | [hagstrum1968] |
| 4–7 / 6–9 | instars to maturity | Western males / females | *L. hesperus*; Kaston's convention, as summarized by Ebeling | [kaston1970; ebeling1975] |
| 4–7 / 6–8 | instars to maturity | Southern males / females | *L. mactans*; usual categories in the same series; one male reached eight and one female nine | [kaston1970; ebeling1975] |
| 5 / 7–8 | maturity instar | Redback males / females | *L. hasselti*; usual stages in Forster's convention; not universal moult counts | [forster1995] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Hagstrum's *L. mactans* moulted while suspended beneath the web. **established** [hagstrum1968]
- In *L. mactans*, pressing movements opened a lateral split above the leg bases. **supported** [hagstrum1968]
- *L. mactans* then pulled its legs from the old cuticle before reaching for the web. **supported** [hagstrum1968]
- Female *L. hesperus* generally reached maturity after more instars than males in Kaston's rearing series. **supported** [kaston1970; ebeling1975]
- *L. variolus* regenerated experimentally amputated legs only when damage was sufficiently distal, at the femoral midpoint or beyond. **supported** [randall1981]
- *L. variolus* shed injured limbs at the coxa–trochanter joint, but those autotomized limbs did not regenerate. **supported** [randall1981]
- Adult female *L. tredecimguttatus* from Crimea were documented moulting after sexual maturity. **supported** [nadolny2019]
- Some Crimean *L. tredecimguttatus* females may mature earlier and continue growing through later moults. **debated** [nadolny2019]

### TERMS

- **Ecdysis:** shedding the old cuticle; **GENERAL SPIDER**. [foelix2011]
- **Exuvia:** the shed cuticle; **GENERAL SPIDER**. [foelix2011]
- **Instar:** developmental stage separated from the next by a moult; numbering requires the author's convention. [downes1987]
- **Autotomy:** controlled shedding of an appendage; in the *L. variolus* experiments, distinct from distal amputation. [randall1981]

### Claims to avoid / quantitative gaps

- “Every black widow has exactly eight moults,” “adults never moult,” and “any lost leg grows back.” [kaston1970; nadolny2019; randall1981]
- Do not relabel Hagstrum's historical *L. mactans* as *L. hesperus* solely because the author worked in California; specimen provenance would need checking.
- A defensible total ecdysis-duration card was not verified from Hagstrum's accessible abstract. The two-hour figure describes preparation.
- Cross-reference `body.exoskeleton`, `body.legs`, and `web.growing-up` for cuticle, leg mechanics and juvenile webs.

## 6. life.development — Two Routes to Adulthood

**Taxa studied:** *L. hesperus*, *L. mactans*, *L. hasselti*. [kaston1970; forster1995; moen2022; kasumovic2009]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 62–151 / 137–242 | days | Western males / females | *L. hesperus*; range of mean times associated with different maturity instars, not all individual extremes | [kaston1970; ebeling1975] |
| 54–88 / 112–140 | days | Southern males / females | *L. mactans*; usual maturity-instar categories; rare extra-instar exceptions excluded | [kaston1970; ebeling1975] |
| 28–45 / 45–74 | days | Redback males / females | *L. hasselti*; 25°C, 12:12 light:dark; review passage does not explicitly identify clock origin, so do not label egg-to-adult | [forster1995] |
| 27 / 30 / 33 | °C | Experimental temperatures | *L. hesperus* juveniles; desert, intermediate and urban-heat treatments; not three optimal temperatures | [moen2022] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Male *L. hesperus* matured earlier than females in Kaston's laboratory series. **supported** [kaston1970; ebeling1975]
- Male *L. mactans* likewise generally matured sooner than females in that series. **supported** [kaston1970; ebeling1975]
- *L. hasselti* females added more developmental stages while attaining a much larger adult size than males. **supported** [forster1995]
- Early *L. hasselti* female instars could be shorter than male instars despite females' longer total development. **supported** [forster1995]
- Elevated-temperature *L. hesperus* juveniles showed delayed development and altered ecdysteroid patterns. **supported** [moen2022]
- *L. hesperus* heat exposure delayed and reduced the ecdysteroid peak preceding the second moult. **supported** [moen2022]
- Male *L. hasselti* developmental responses to nearby females involved a trade-off between rapid maturity and adult condition. **supported** [kasumovic2009]
- Better juvenile feeding generally extended subsequent adult male *L. hasselti* lifespan in the diet experiment. **supported** [kasumovic2009]

### TERMS

- **Protandry:** males reaching reproductive maturity earlier than females; demonstrated in field *L. hesperus* populations too. [scott2019]
- **Sexual size dimorphism:** size differences between sexes; body mass and leg length are different measures. [baruffaldi2024]
- **Ecdysteroid:** a steroid hormone class associated with moulting; measured during *L. hesperus* development. [moen2022]
- **Developmental plasticity:** development changing with environmental or social conditions. [kasumovic2009]

### Claims to avoid

- Treating the table as a common-garden comparison of all three species, or silently converting every duration to “days since egg laying.” [kaston1970; forster1995]
- “Warmer always means faster”: stressful heat can delay *L. hesperus* development. [moen2022]
- “Females just grow more slowly”: *L. hasselti* females can have shorter early instars while adding more instars overall. [forster1995]
- Cross-reference `body.size` for adult measurements and `venom.sex-and-age` for toxin expression; do not repeat those exhibits here.

## 7. life.mate-search — Finding a Web Is the First Hazard

**Taxa studied:** Australian *L. hasselti*; Vancouver Island *L. hesperus*, plus laboratory conspecifics. [andrade2003; scott2019; modanu2013]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| >80 | % estimated mortality | Search without finding a mate | *L. hasselti*; observational field studies and release experiment, not cannibalism mortality | [andrade2003] |
| ≈88 | % estimated mortality | Between-web search risk | *L. hesperus*; mark/recapture inference, Vancouver Island; disappearance is not an observed death | [scott2019] |
| >200 | m | Some male movements | *L. hesperus*; displacement between detections; most were within 60 m; not a flight distance | [scott2019] |
| 62 / 26 | % finding females | Two release experiments | *L. hesperus*; 2016 / 2017 nights with different wind conditions; not species survival rates | [scott2019] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Adult male *L. hasselti* leave their capture webs and search for females. **established** [andrade2003]
- *L. hasselti* males have a low expectation of encountering multiple females in nature. **supported** [andrade2003]
- Male *L. hesperus* use female airborne pheromones during field mate searching. **established** [scott2019]
- Male *L. hesperus* detect and follow rival males' silk trails in laboratory choices. **supported** [scott2019]
- Rival silk can provide *L. hesperus* males with routes and information leading toward females. **supported** [scott2019]
- *L. hesperus* searching success differed strongly between release nights with contrasting winds. **supported** [scott2019]
- Adult male *L. hasselti* and *L. hesperus* can transfer sperm into their palps repeatedly. **established** [modanu2013]
- *L. hasselti* rival competition includes sneaking tactics as well as direct interactions. **supported** [stoltz2008]

### TERMS

- **Sperm induction:** loading the palps from sperm deposited on silk; **GENERAL SPIDER** mechanism. [michalik2011]
- **Scramble competition:** competition through finding and reaching mates quickly. [scott2019]
- **Monogyny:** a male mating with one female; distinct from an inability to make more sperm. [andrade2003; modanu2013]

### Claims to avoid

- “Eighty per cent are eaten by females”: the redback search estimate concerns failure before finding a potential mate. [andrade2003]
- Do not treat every missing marked *L. hesperus* as a confirmed death; emigration and imperfect detection qualify mortality estimates. [scott2019]
- “Palps make sperm,” “all adult widows stop spermatogenesis,” or “all males cease silk production.” [michalik2011; modanu2013; correagarhwal2017]
- Cross-reference `web.messages` for pheromone identities and web reduction, `web.male-silk` for expression, and `body.pedipalps` for loading anatomy.

## 8. life.courtship — The Somersault

**Taxa studied:** principally *L. hasselti*; *L. geometricus* for the independently studied immature-mating route. [forster1992; andrade1996; biaggio2016; sentenska2021]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 180 | degrees | Redback somersault | *L. hasselti* male's turn after palp insertion; Forster's description | [forster1992] |
| ≈100 | minutes of courtship | Female response threshold | *L. hasselti*; inferred cumulative stimulation threshold in the studied context, not a universal minimum | [stoltz2010] |
| 2 | reproductive tracts | Paired insemination targets | *L. hasselti*; independent left/right spermathecae, normally accessed with opposite palps | [snow2005] |
| 2–6 | days before maturity moult | Immature-mating window | *L. hasselti*, *L. geometricus*, *L. hesperus* literature summarized by Sentenská; individual studies differ | [sentenska2021] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- After palp insertion, a male *L. hasselti* turns his abdomen toward the female's mouthparts. **established** [forster1992]
- A surviving male *L. hasselti* can withdraw, groom and repeat copulation using the other palp. **established** [forster1992]
- Cannibalized *L. hasselti* males copulated longer and achieved greater paternity in Andrade's comparison. **supported** [andrade1996]
- Female *L. hasselti* that consumed a mate were less receptive to another male in that study. **supported** [andrade1996]
- *L. hasselti* intruders can benefit from courtship stimulation already supplied by a rival. **supported** [stoltz2010]
- Rapidly mating *L. hasselti* sneakers risk cannibalism before completing both copulations. **supported** [stoltz2008]
- Male *L. hasselti* and *L. geometricus* can open the cuticle covering late-subadult female genitalia and inseminate them. **established** [biaggio2016]
- Immature-mated *L. hasselti* and *L. geometricus* females retain functional sperm through their maturity moult. **established** [biaggio2016]
- *L. hasselti* and *L. geometricus* immature matings rarely ended in cannibalism, allowing some males to reproduce again. **supported** [biaggio2016]
- Female *L. hasselti* can limit paternity by ending mating before both reproductive tracts are inseminated. **supported** [snow2005]

### TERMS

- **Copulation:** genital coupling and sperm transfer; separate from the preceding courtship. [forster1992]
- **Late subadult:** the portion of the penultimate female stage with developing functional genital structures. [sentenska2021]
- **Premature cannibalism:** redback killing during the first copulation, preventing the second. [snow2005; stoltz2008]

### Claims to avoid

- “Every widow somersaults,” “every redback male is eaten,” or “the female must eat him for eggs to develop.” [andrade1996; baruffaldi2024]
- Do not label immature widow mating haemocoelic sperm injection: *L. geometricus* morphology shows transfer through the genital ducts. [sentenska2021]
- Do not equate a breached covering cuticle with mating with an arbitrary early juvenile. [biaggio2016; sentenska2021]
- Cross-reference `life.mating-plugs`, `body.epigynum`, `body.pedipalps`, and `web.messages`; this exhibit keeps the somersault as its focus.

## 9. life.cannibalism — How Much Widow Is in the Name?

**Taxa studied:** *L. hasselti*, *L. geometricus*, *L. mirabilis*, *L. hesperus*, *L. mactans*. [andrade1996; baruffaldi2024; breene1985]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ≈65 | % matings | Redback field cannibalism | *L. hasselti*; unmanipulated natural matings reported in the original research, not a genus average | [andrade1996; andrade2003] |
| 92 / 50 / 30 | % matings | Laboratory species contrast | *L. hasselti* / *L. geometricus* / *L. mirabilis*; 2024 comparison, cannibalism across mating | [baruffaldi2024] |
| 11 | % trials | Western pre-mating cannibalism | *L. hesperus*; 2024 study, during initial courtship; none during copulation | [baruffaldi2024] |
| 0 | observed cases | Southern cannibalism | *L. mactans*; 2024 laboratory trials; an observed zero, not biological impossibility | [baruffaldi2024] |
| 2/18 | males consumed | An earlier southern experiment | *L. mactans*; laboratory serial-mating study; both deaths at second mating attempts | [breene1985] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- *L. hasselti* field matings included males that survived rather than being cannibalized. **established** [andrade1996]
- *L. hasselti* cannibalism was more frequent in the 2024 laboratory comparison than in the earlier field sample. **supported** [baruffaldi2024; andrade1996]
- *L. geometricus* and *L. mirabilis* differed from *L. hasselti* in cannibalism frequency under comparable laboratory conditions. **supported** [baruffaldi2024]
- *L. hesperus* males in that comparison usually survived mating; recorded cannibalism preceded copulation. **supported** [baruffaldi2024]
- *L. mactans* males survived all interactions in the 2024 comparison. **supported** [baruffaldi2024]
- Two male *L. mactans* each inseminated three different females in the earlier experiment. **supported** [breene1985]
- *L. hasselti* cannibalism during the first copulation can deny a male access to the second sperm store. **supported** [snow2005]
- Longer courtship was not consistently predicted by cannibalism-based male mating-system categories across the five species. **supported** [baruffaldi2024]

### TERMS

- **Sexual cannibalism:** consuming a potential or actual mating partner; record whether it precedes, accompanies or follows copulation. [baruffaldi2024]
- **Male self-sacrifice:** a male behaviour facilitating his consumption, as in the redback somersault. [andrade1996]
- **Polygyny:** one male reproducing with multiple females, experimentally demonstrated in *L. mactans*. [breene1985]

### Claims to avoid

- “Black widows always eat their husbands” and its reverse, “sexual cannibalism is entirely a laboratory myth.” [andrade1996; breene1985]
- Do not compare percentages without their denominator: mating males, all pairings, and serially tested individual males differ. [baruffaldi2024; breene1985]
- Zero observed *L. mactans* cases in one study does not establish a zero field rate. Neither western nor southern widows have a verified universal rate here.
- Nutritional benefit to a female and paternity benefit to a male are different hypotheses; neither follows merely from observing consumption. [andrade1996]

## 10. life.mating-plugs — A Broken Tip in the Right Place

**Placement:** a standalone exhibit or `life.courtship.plugs`; use one id, not duplicate exhibits.

**Taxa studied:** *L. hasselti* for paternity experiments; *L. geometricus* for morphology; *L. mactans* for repeat insemination after tip loss. [snow2005; snow2006; sentenska2021; breene1985]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | spermathecae | Separate stores | *L. hasselti*; the paternity experiment manipulated which store each rival inseminated | [snow2005] |
| 79.8 | % offspring | First-male paternity, same side | *L. hasselti*; mean when rivals inseminated one spermatheca; not an isolated plug-effect estimate | [snow2005] |
| 49.3 | % offspring | First-male paternity, opposite sides | *L. hasselti*; mean when rivals inseminated different spermathecae | [snow2005] |
| 3 | females per male | Repeat insemination despite tip loss | *L. mactans*; both males assigned three females produced offspring with all three | [breene1985] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- *L. hasselti* males can leave broken embolus tips in the female reproductive tract. **established** [snow2006]
- *L. hasselti* tips at the entrance to a sperm store protected paternity better than tips lodged elsewhere. **supported** [snow2006]
- Broken tips are therefore not automatically effective plugs in *L. hasselti*. **supported** [snow2006]
- Experimental removal of the *L. hasselti* tip did not prevent subsequent copulation and insemination. **supported** [snow2006]
- Rival *L. hasselti* males tended to avoid previously inseminated reproductive tracts. **supported** [snow2005]
- Late-subadult *L. geometricus* females more often had plugs in both spermathecae than adult-mated females in the morphology study. **supported** [sentenska2021]
- *L. mactans* embolus-tip loss did not necessarily prevent successful insemination of additional females. **established** [breene1985]

### TERMS

- **Embolus:** the male palpal structure inserted into a female's genital opening; **GENERAL SPIDER**. [michalik2011]
- **Apical sclerite:** the detachable embolus tip in these widow studies. [snow2006]
- **Sperm precedence:** a mating-order bias in offspring paternity. [snow2005]
- **Mating plug:** a physical obstruction that reduces subsequent insemination; effectiveness requires evidence. [snow2006]

### Claims to avoid

- “The whole palp snaps off,” “a plug seals the entire female,” or “broken genitalia always sterilize the male.” [snow2006; breene1985]
- Do not attribute the 79.8 versus 49.3% comparison solely to plugs: Snow 2005 manipulated sperm-storage allocation. Snow 2006 tested tip location and function.
- Do not promise complete lifetime paternity: placement and access to both stores matter. [snow2005; snow2006]
- Keep detailed duct and palp anatomy in `body.epigynum` and `body.pedipalps`.

## 11. life.lifespan — A Long-Lived Female, a Different Male Clock

**Taxa studied:** *L. hesperus*, *L. mactans*, *L. variolus*, *L. hasselti*. [kaston1970; kasumovic2009; stoltz2010b]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 952 / 196 | adult days | Western female / male maxima | *L. hesperus*; Kaston's captive post-maturity records; not egg-to-death ages | [kaston1970; ebeling1975] |
| 849 / 127 | adult days | Southern female / male maxima | *L. mactans*; same historical series and endpoint | [kaston1970; ebeling1975] |
| 822 / 155 | adult days | Northern female / male maxima | *L. variolus*; same historical series and endpoint | [kaston1970; ebeling1975] |
| 11.72 ± 0.59 / 8.88 ± 0.59 | days | Survival after control / courtship | *L. hasselti* males; means ± SE, n=25/group; three-hour courtship experiment, no subsequent feeding | [kasumovic2009] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Captive female *L. hesperus* substantially outlived males in Kaston's maximum adult-longevity records. **supported** [kaston1970; ebeling1975]
- Captive *L. mactans* and *L. variolus* showed the same sex difference in recorded adult maxima. **supported** [kaston1970; ebeling1975]
- Adult male *L. hasselti* that courted experimentally had shorter subsequent survival than controls. **supported** [kasumovic2009]
- Better-conditioned adult male *L. hasselti* survived longer in that experiment. **supported** [kasumovic2009]
- Restricting juvenile food did not extend adult male *L. hasselti* lifespan in the tested treatments. **supported** [kasumovic2009]
- Mated female *L. hasselti* outlived unmated females under dietary restriction in a separate laboratory experiment. **supported** [stoltz2010b]
- Field *L. hesperus* females in the Vancouver Island study system can persist across multiple summer mating seasons. **supported** [fischer2025]

### TERMS

- **Adult longevity:** time after the maturity moult; distinct from total lifespan. [kaston1970; ebeling1975]
- **Senescence:** age-related deterioration; a maximum lifespan alone does not measure its rate. **GENERAL LIFE-HISTORY** [stoltz2010b]
- **Capital breeding:** reproduction using resources accumulated previously; applied experimentally to male *L. hasselti*. [kasumovic2009]
- **Iteroparity:** repeated reproductive bouts; applicable to female *L. hasselti*. [stoltz2010b]

### Claims to avoid / quantitative gaps

- “Females live three years in nature” derived from a captive maximum, or “males die immediately after any mating.” [kaston1970; breene1985]
- Do not call a post-courtship survival interval an entire adult lifespan. [kasumovic2009]
- No verified genus-wide ageing curve, maximum-age record across all widow species, or fixed number of overwinterings is proposed.
- Reproductive status, diet, search hazards and temperature complicate attribution of mortality to intrinsic ageing. [kasumovic2009; stoltz2010b]

## 12. life.seasons — A Calendar with a Place Attached

**Taxa studied:** Vancouver Island *L. hesperus*; Queensland and introduced Japanese *L. hasselti*; Japanese invasive *L. geometricus*. [scott2019; salomon2011; fischer2025; downes1987; mowery2022b; hayasaka2021]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| August | calendar month | Peak mating activity | *L. hesperus*, Vancouver Island, 2016 surveys; not a worldwide widow calendar | [scott2019] |
| ≥5:1 | adult males : receptive females | Peak-season competition | Same *L. hesperus* population during August; operational, not hatchling sex ratio | [scott2019] |
| 15 / 25 | °C | Cold-range comparison | *L. hasselti* native Australian versus invasive Japanese populations; experimental development temperatures | [mowery2022b] |
| 45.9 / 45.4 | °C | Estimated 50% hatch reduction | *L. hasselti* / *L. geometricus*; ten-minute dry-heat exposures, Table 2; not seasonal ambient limits | [hayasaka2021] |
| 50.9 / 52.8 | °C | Estimated 90% hatch reduction | Same species/order, exposure and endpoint | [hayasaka2021] |
| May–October | calendar months | Oviposition and juvenile growth | *L. hesperus*, coastal Vancouver Island; seasonal context reported with 2005 year-round field sampling | [salomon2011] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Male *L. hesperus* matured earlier than females in the Vancouver Island field study. **supported** [scott2019]
- *L. hesperus* sexual activity there concentrated in late summer rather than uniformly across the survey season. **supported** [scott2019]
- *L. hesperus* web pheromone abundance peaked during summer in the year-long sampling study. **supported** [fischer2025]
- Northern Queensland *L. hasselti* populations included all life stages throughout the year. **supported** [downes1987]
- *L. hasselti* can overwinter in sacs containing eggs or young, with development slowed by cold. **supported** [mowery2022b]
- Invasive Japanese *L. hasselti* sacs had better hatching success under cold experimental conditions than native Australian sacs. **supported** [mowery2022b]
- Short dry-heat exposure sharply reduced subsequent hatching in Japanese *L. hasselti* and *L. geometricus* sacs. **supported** [hayasaka2021]
- *L. hasselti* and *L. geometricus* differed in the estimated temperature causing a 90% reduction in hatching. **supported** [hayasaka2021]
- Coastal Vancouver Island *L. hesperus* produced sacs during the warm-season period when emerging juveniles grew and matured. **supported** [salomon2011]

### TERMS

- **Phenology:** timing of seasonal biological events. [scott2019]
- **Operational sex ratio:** reproductive males relative to currently receptive females. [scott2019]
- **Thermal reaction norm:** how a measured trait changes with temperature. [mowery2022b]
- **LT50 / LT90:** estimated treatment temperatures for specified reductions in hatch success, at a stated exposure duration. [hayasaka2021]

### Claims to avoid / calendar gaps

- Do not publish one calendar for every latitude, hemisphere or widow species. In particular, Vancouver Island August is not an Australian redback schedule. [scott2019; downes1987]
- No well-supported month-by-month schedule of sac laying and spiderling emergence for all five familiar widow species was verified here; use regional evidence before adding one.
- Do not identify cold-induced developmental delay as obligate diapause without evidence, or declare all adults winter casualties. [mowery2022b; fischer2025]
- **Source inconsistency:** the Hayasaka abstract says complete failure at ≥55°C for both species, but Table 1 reports 1.6 ± 1.0% redback hatch at 55°C and zero at 57.5°C. Use the LT estimates above, not the abstract's blanket cutoff.

## 13. life.guarding — A Mother Between the Sac and the Wasp

**Further topic.** Emphasize active maternal behaviour; leave the sac's materials and spike structure in `web.egg-sac-silk` and its defence sub-exhibit.

**Taxa studied:** *L. geometricus* and *L. pallidus*; parasitoid *Philolema latrodecti*. [arabesky2025]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5/18 / 0/17 | females capturing wasps | Brown / white widow defence | *L. geometricus* / *L. pallidus* laboratory exposure trials | [arabesky2025] |
| 19 / 64 | % sacs parasitized | Brown / white widow outcomes | Same comparison; 3/16 / 9/14 assessed sacs, a different denominator from behavioural trials | [arabesky2025] |
| 3/5 | wasp-catching females | Wrapped, then discarded | *L. geometricus* females that captured wasps; remaining two fed on them | [arabesky2025] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Female *L. geometricus* and *L. pallidus* increased activity when exposed to parasitoid wasps. **supported** [arabesky2025]
- Both *L. geometricus* and *L. pallidus* circled and tapped sacs more during wasp exposure. **supported** [arabesky2025]
- *L. geometricus* captured and silk-wrapped wasps; no *L. pallidus* did so in these trials. **supported** [arabesky2025]
- Some *L. geometricus* discarded captured wasps without feeding. **supported** [arabesky2025]
- *L. geometricus* sacs were parasitized less frequently than *L. pallidus* sacs after experimental wasp exposure. **supported** [arabesky2025]
- Defensive care may contribute to invasive *L. geometricus* success, but its population-level contribution remains unmeasured. **debated** [arabesky2025]

### TERMS

- **Maternal care:** maternal behaviour improving offspring prospects, here including sac defence. [arabesky2025]
- **Parasitoid:** an organism whose immature development consumes and kills its host; distinct from an incidental sac occupant. [arabesky2025]

### Claims to avoid

- “All widows abandon their eggs” or “brown widow mothers prevent all parasitism.” [arabesky2025]
- Do not confuse the behavioural sample with the smaller set of sacs scored for later parasitism. [arabesky2025]
- Killing a wasp without feeding supports defence, but does not prove that all such attacks lack a feeding function. [arabesky2025]

## 14. life.hybridisation — Inheriting the Somersault

**Further topic.** A genetics-and-reproduction extension of `life.courtship`, rather than a claim about routine hybridisation in nature.

**Taxa studied:** laboratory *L. hasselti* × *L. katipo* hybrids and backcrosses. [uludag2026]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 104 | backcross males | Scored mating phenotypes | *L. hasselti*–*L. katipo* crosses; 55 redback-directed / 49 katipō-directed males | [uludag2026] |
| 31/55 / 18/49 | males somersaulting | Behaviour segregates | Respective backcross groups, tested with *L. katipo* females; Fig. 3 | [uludag2026] |
| 49/55 / 10/49 | males constricting abdomen | A different inheritance pattern | Same backcross groups and trials; observed counts, not Mendelian predictions | [uludag2026] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- *L. katipo* females and *L. hasselti* males produced hybrid offspring used for backcrossing. **established** [uludag2026]
- Hybrid females were crossed with males of either parental species to produce the tested generations. **established** [uludag2026]
- *L. hasselti*–*L. katipo* backcross males could express somersaulting without abdominal constriction, or the reverse. **supported** [uludag2026]
- The *L. hasselti*–*L. katipo* results rejected tight co-inheritance of those two male traits. **supported** [uludag2026]
- Somersault frequencies in these backcrosses supported a simple X-linked inheritance model. **supported** [uludag2026]
- Abdominal-constriction frequencies in these backcrosses required a more complex explanation. **supported** [uludag2026]

### TERMS

- **Hybrid:** offspring of parents from different species, here the two named widows. [uludag2026]
- **Backcross:** crossing a hybrid with a parental species. [uludag2026]
- **X-linked:** associated with a locus on an X chromosome; the study inferred inheritance without identifying the causal gene. [uludag2026]

### Claims to avoid

- “A suicide gene has been found”: the experiment supported an inheritance model, not a sequenced causal locus. [uludag2026]
- Do not turn successful laboratory crosses into a measured rate of natural hybridisation, or extend compatibility to all widows. [uludag2026]
- Do not repeat the paper's historical species-count statements as current taxonomy; use the museum's `wsc2026` and `miller2026` registry entries.
