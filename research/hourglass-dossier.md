# The Hourglass — research dossier

Researched 23 November 2026. Scope: BWM-01.01, body.hourglass, proposed as a parent case containing ten halls. Existing parent id and accession are preserved; hall ids, titles, kickers and summaries are suggestions. This is the single-file research hand-off; neither exhibit YAML nor the source registry is edited.

## Reading this dossier

- Bracketed citation keys refer to content/sources.yaml or the NEW entries in SOURCES below. The Senses dossier, registry, hourglass case and body.colour case were read before drafting. Registry presence alone was not treated as verification.
- **established** = directly documented and secure as qualified; **supported** = a particular experiment, limited sample or interpretation; **debated** = an unresolved mechanism or explanation. Confidence applies to the qualified sentence, never automatically to the genus.
- Evidence classes are **LATRODECTUS**, **OTHER THERIDIID**, **OTHER SPIDER**, and **GENERAL SPIDER**. Species and experimental context are specified separately; a lizard injected with widow venom supplies LATRODECTUS venom evidence, not an hourglass-avoidance experiment.
- FIGURES are candidate fact cards, not illustrations. Sample sizes, instrumental measurements, developmental descriptions and experimental effects are distinguished. Qualitative cards are preferable to invented averages.
- Colour is separated into location, shape, reflectance, pigment chemistry, receiver perception and behavioural effect. None of those measurements substitutes for another.
- Primary papers, their author-hosted copies and taxonomic treatments were preferred; institutional identification accounts supplement them. A thesis is labelled as such. Indexed primary-text passages are identified in the verification notes where a complete file could not be retrieved.
- Names follow the checked [World Spider Catalog genus listing](https://wsc.nmbe.ch/species-list/3681/Latrodectus), with the very recent [2026 atlas](https://doi.org/10.1093/zoolinnean/zlag145) considered separately where the catalog has not yet incorporated changes. No fixed contemporary species-total card is proposed. The redback is styled *L. hasselti*, although some papers print *hasseltii*. [wsc2026; miller2026]
- All biological plates must distinguish dorsal from ventral views. A widow's hourglass belongs on the abdominal venter; its web is a tangle cobweb, with ground-attached gumfoot capture lines where shown, never an orb.

### Priority qualifications for the current exhibits

1. Retain the warning interpretation as supported, not a settled account of learning or of the signal's evolutionary origin.
2. Replace “birds can see it, insects cannot” with receiver-dependent contrast; visibility is not an all-or-nothing property.
3. Anatomical underside and direction in space are different: inversion can make the venter face upward, but concealment and viewing angle still matter.
4. Revise body.colour's genus-wide “hatch pale and striped” formulation: hatching, emergence and later instars are different events, and the documented western-widow sequence is not a universal widow timetable.
5. Revise “males keep the patterns of youth” to a species-qualified description; male widows can possess ventral hourglass marks.
6. Do not equate hourglass area, redness or brightness with venom quantity, potency, unpleasant taste or human bite severity.
7. Preserve “tentatively assigned” for western-widow red pigments; a Raman fingerprint is not isolation of a named molecule.
8. A missing red mark does not exclude *Latrodectus*, and a recognizable hourglass alone does not resolve every species.

**Suggested summary:** A small patch links development, chemistry, posture and the eyes of other animals. These halls follow the evidence from a visible shape to the experiments that can establish what it does.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: *L. hesperus*, *L. hasselti* and *L. pallidus* for the five additional overview items below.

## 1. body.hourglass.warning — A Warning Put to the Test

**Suggested kicker:** A red patch can change an attack.

**Suggested summary:** Widow defence includes biting and silk-flicking, both observed experimentally. Whether a particular receiver responds to colour must be tested separately from the spider's ability to defend itself. [nelsen2014]

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: widow-shaped models, live *L. hesperus*, *L. revivensis* nests and *L. hesperus* venom; birds and lizards are the receivers or assay animals.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.9 | reported attack ratio | Unmarked models attacked more | Wild birds; species-controlled analysis | [brandley2016] |
| 24 | gecko–widow pairings | A different receiver | *Hemidactylus turcicus*; thesis, three paint treatments | [gburek2014] |
| >50 | % interactions | Dry bites occurred | *L. hesperus*, artificial-target experiment; not a field bite rate | [nelsen2014] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Feeder visitors included chickadees, cardinals, house finches, titmice, white-breasted nuthatches, red-headed woodpeckers and pileated woodpeckers. **supported — LATRODECTUS, model experiment** [brandley2016]
- Birds encountered single belly-up models, painted black or black with red. **established — LATRODECTUS, model experiment** [brandley2016]
- Beak or foot contact counted as attack. **established — LATRODECTUS, operational definition** [brandley2016]
- Pinching female western widows elicited more biting than prodding. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [nelsen2014]
- Western widows changed venom expenditure with the location and timing of experimental threats. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [nelsen2014]
- Hourglass painting did not alter house-gecko activity in the thesis trials. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus, thesis** [gburek2014]
- No geckos ate widows during the initial trials, and only two predation events occurred during subsequent cohabitation. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus, thesis** [gburek2014]
- Natural-history observations identified great grey shrikes as predators of desert widows. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. revivensis** [konigswald1990]
- Southern alligator lizards maintained sprint performance after experimental western-widow venom injection. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus venom; Elgaria multicarinata receiver** [thill2022]
- A later injection study found different whole-animal and tissue responses among additional lizard species. **supported — LATRODECTUS venom assays; Coleonyx variegatus, Takydromus sexlineatus and Iguana iguana** [thill2025]

### TERMS

- **Aposematism:** signalling a defence to potential attackers.
- **Neophobia:** reluctance to engage an unfamiliar object.
- **Qualitative honesty:** a signal associated with possessing a defence.
- **Quantitative honesty:** variation in a signal predicting variation in the strength of that defence.
- **Venom resistance:** reduced impairment at a specified exposure, not universal immunity.

### Claims to avoid

- The model experiment cannot distinguish learned recognition, generalized colour aversion and neophobia, or isolate hourglass geometry from the presence of red.
- Do not call feeder visitors proven natural widow predators merely because they touched a model.
- No verified mark-manipulation study here measures avoidance learning, retention or generalization in experimentally naive birds.
- No verified widow experiment here correlates individual mark colour with venom potency or quantifies how unpleasant the body tastes.
- Gecko non-results with very few predation events are weak evidence against a colour effect; they do not invalidate the bird result.
- Venom-injection studies in 2022 and 2025 extend the defence story, not the colour-deterrence result. [thill2022; thill2025]

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- Illustrate the feeder experiment with two alternative, belly-up plastic models shown in separate trial panels, clearly labelled as models and without silk.
- Show a southern alligator lizard beside a widow in a tangle web, with a small laboratory-assay inset that distinguishes venom tolerance from visual avoidance.

## 2. body.hourglass.receivers — Red Through Other Eyes

**Suggested kicker:** A colour belongs to its viewer.

**Suggested summary:** Spectral measurements describe returned light; a visual model estimates how a specified eye receives it. Comparative spider optics also show why human-visible whiteness and ultraviolet reflectance must be considered separately. [gawryszewski2015]

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: adult female *L. mactans* and *L. variolus*; OTHER SPIDER: crab-spider optical comparators.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9 / 5 | females | Reflectance sample | *L. mactans* / *L. variolus* | [brandley2016] |
| 520 / 607 | nm | Model receptor peaks | Insect green / blue-tit long-wave | [brandley2016] |
| 0.67 / 0.21 | Michelson contrast | Bird / insect model | Within-body red–black contrast | [brandley2016] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Measured hourglasses reflected little below approximately 550 nm. **supported — LATRODECTUS, sampled females** [brandley2016]
- The sampled adult females showed no significant ultraviolet reflectance. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. mactans and L. variolus** [brandley2016]
- Crab-spider guanine reflects ultraviolet light that overlying tissues partly filter. **established — OTHER SPIDER, Thomisidae** [gawryszewski2015]
- Female southern and northern widows hide in dense silk by day and commonly wait inverted at night. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. mactans and L. variolus, institutional account** [hiskes2013]
- Night collections in the Phoenix study sampled western widows active on their webs. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus, collection protocol** [johnson2017]

Five observations are retained: receiver physiology and natural illumination remain less well tested than the adult reflectance comparison.

### TERMS

- **Reflectance:** the fraction of incident light returned at each wavelength.
- **Photon catch:** light absorbed by a specified receptor under a specified illuminant.
- **Chromatic contrast:** separation in colour signals.
- **Achromatic contrast:** a brightness-related difference.
- **Michelson contrast:** difference divided by sum of the two measured or modelled intensities.
- **UV:** ultraviolet wavelengths, outside human visible colour.

### Claims to avoid

- A receptor peak is not the wavelength reflected most strongly by the spider.
- Do not say the hourglass is invisible to insects, equally conspicuous to every bird, or “three times redder.”
- Contrast values do not establish detection distance, visual acuity, prey behaviour or an absolute perceptual threshold.
- The receiver models use assumed receptor sensitivities and daylight illumination, not measured nocturnal performance; their colour-space distances are not just-noticeable differences.
- No dedicated UV survey of juveniles, males, brown widows or all populations was verified; adult measurements do not exclude stage-specific UV properties.
- Do not label a digitally desaturated human photograph “exactly what an insect sees.”
- Night activity does not prove either that the mark is useless at night or that colour vision remains effective in darkness.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- Build a plate with measured red and black reflectance curves above labelled receptor-sensitivity schematics, identifying simulations explicitly and avoiding invented detection thresholds.
- Show a daylight refuge and a dim nocturnal tangle web as alternative lighting contexts, with the ventral mark anatomically constant and no luminous or fluorescent glow.

## 3. body.hourglass.inverted — The Side Facing the Sky

**Suggested kicker:** Underside need not mean hidden.

**Suggested summary:** Inversion changes the direction from which a ventral patch is visible. Web geometry, ground height and the retreat determine which surfaces are exposed; a dorsal stripe remains a separate character.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: *L. hesperus*, *L. mactans*, *L. variolus*, *L. hasselti*, *L. katipo* and *L. revivensis*.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upward | ventral orientation | Anatomy meets posture | Inverted southern/northern widow; not every moment or viewpoint | [hiskes2013] |
| 2 | abdominal surfaces bearing red | Redback's two displays | Dorsal stripe plus ventral hourglass; distinct markings | [australianmuseum2026] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- An inverted widow presents its anatomical venter upward. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. mactans and L. variolus posture** [hiskes2013]
- Western-widow cobwebs contain gumfoot lines that attach to the substrate below. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [blackledge2005b]
- Gumfoot lines transmit and help capture ground-moving prey. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [blackledge2005b]
- Desert widows sit beneath their capture platform at night. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. revivensis** [konigswald1990]
- Northern widows settled higher than southern widows in laboratory habitats. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. variolus versus L. mactans** [brandley2016]
- Female redbacks bear both a dorsal stripe and a ventral mark. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. hasselti** [australianmuseum2026]
- Red katipō females have a median dorsal red stripe. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. katipo, red form** [vink2008]
- A desert widow's debris-covered retreat can conceal the animal from view. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. revivensis** [konigswald1990]

### TERMS

- **Dorsal versus ventral:** anatomical coordinates that do not change when the spider turns.
- **Viewing angle:** the receiver's direction relative to the displayed surface.
- **Gumfoot line:** a capture line with adhesive near its substrate attachment. [blackledge2005b]
- **Retreat:** the sheltered part of the spider's web-site.

### Claims to avoid

- Posture supports a presentation mechanism; it does not establish why the first ventral patch evolved.
- “Always upside down,” “always visible from above,” and “insects only approach from below” are too absolute.
- The high-web/dorsal-red association motivates a viewing-angle hypothesis, not experimental proof that prey avoidance caused dorsal colour loss.
- Do not redraw a redback's dorsal stripe as an hourglass or put a ventral hourglass on its back.
- Do not infer signal function from a horizontal platform in isolation: the widow occupies a three-dimensional cobweb.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- Draw an inverted female in a side elevation of a tangle web with gumfoot lines, labelling anatomical dorsal/ventral surfaces separately from ground/sky.
- Place paired dorsal and ventral redback and red-katipō specimens beside the western widow, using species-verified markings and no implied movement of a mark between surfaces.

## 4. body.hourglass.growing — Before the Red Hourglass

**Suggested kicker:** The adult pattern has a history.

**Suggested summary:** Juvenile patterning changes through development, with substantial differences among individuals. A developmental series should follow one named species and preserve the distinction between dorsal pattern loss and ventral mark formation. [kaston1970]

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: principally *L. hesperus*, with *L. variolus*, *L. mactans* and *L. hasselti* comparisons.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First | instar, author's terminology | A pale precursor | *L. hesperus*: yellow ventral area, scarcely narrowed | [kaston1970] |
| Sixth | instar, author's terminology | Redder and narrower | *L. hesperus* developmental description, not universal onset | [kaston1970] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Newly emerged western widows have a white abdomen with dark spots. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [vetter2012c]
- Their legs and carapace are initially tan. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [vetter2012c]
- Western-widow dorsal stripes fragment as the background darkens. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [vetter2012c]
- Some western-widow juveniles carry orange-red within the pale dorsal stripe. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [vetter2012c]
- Kaston described increasing ventral red and central constriction in sixth-instar western widows. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [kaston1970]
- Young northern widows may have a more connected hourglass than later stages. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. variolus** [kaston1970]
- Juvenile redbacks have additional white abdominal markings. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. hasselti** [australianmuseum2026]
- Some adult western-widow females retain pale anterior abdominal markings. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [vetter2017]

### TERMS

- **Ontogeny:** an individual's development.
- **Instar:** a developmental stage between successive moults.
- **Penultimate:** the stage immediately before the final moult to maturity.
- **Emergence:** leaving the egg sac, distinct from hatching.
- **Pattern element:** one stripe, spot or patch considered separately from background colour.

### Claims to avoid

- Do not turn the first conspicuous red mark into the moment venom first becomes present.
- Do not claim all widows acquire a finished red hourglass only at the final moult.
- Instar counts depend on author convention and development; do not convert Kaston's ordinal stages into days or a universal moult count.
- A pale juvenile, a darker juvenile and an adult are not interchangeable illustrations of the same stage.
- No verified longitudinal spectrometric series or molecular map specifies the colour of every patch at every widow moult.
- Avoid an inevitable uniform-black endpoint: adult patterns vary by species and individual.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- Make a western-widow growth plate with separately labelled dorsal and ventral rows, showing the pale yellow precursor and progressive pattern changes without assigning unverified ages.
- Show a northern-widow ventral comparison in which a connected juvenile mark becomes separated later, explicitly marking this as a documented possibility rather than the fate of every individual.

### TERMS

- **Sexual dichromatism:** differences in colour between sexes.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** sex differences in morphology more generally.
- **Juvenile-like:** descriptive resemblance, not proof of developmental arrest.
- **Pedipalp:** a paired appendage modified into a sperm-transfer organ in mature males.

### Claims to avoid

- Do not use colour alone to distinguish a juvenile from an adult male.
- “Only females have hourglasses” is false.
- Retained patterning does not by itself demonstrate neoteny, female mimicry, sexual selection or a different warning function.
- No verified experiment here tests whether male versus female colour changes predator attack rate.
- A non-result from changing female marks does not prove male widows lack colour vision.
- Do not use medical-danger generalizations to explain male coloration.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- Place a mature male western widow, a juvenile female and a mature female in a comparison plate with adult male palps visible and species-appropriate dorsal and ventral insets.

### TERMS

- **Cline:** a geographic gradient in a character.
- **Synonym:** a different scientific name applied to the same taxon.
- **Ground colour:** the background against which pattern elements appear.
- **Heritability:** population-specific genetic contribution to measured trait variation, not mere resemblance between relatives.

### Claims to avoid

- Do not give an exact “percentage without marks” without a defined population and adequate census.
- Do not turn the temperature correlation in katipō into an experimental temperature-induced colour switch or an established thermal mechanism.
- *L. atritus* is not an additional accepted species in the checked catalog. [wsc2026]
- “White widow” is not a synonym for an unusually pale brown widow.
- The classic thirteen-spot description of *L. tredecimguttatus* concerns the dorsal abdomen; it is not thirteen hourglasses or a dependable count on every adult.
- Fully black Mediterranean specimens and the historical tredecimguttatus/lugubris material require taxonomic care; the recent atlas flags past identification problems. Do not make a range-wide spot-frequency card. [miller2026]
- No widow hourglass heritability estimate, Mendelian segregation ratio, QTL or causative pattern gene was verified in this pass.
- “Always complete” in a species description refers to the examined material, not every future specimen.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- Create a ventral specimen atlas using verified examples of connected, split, reduced, orange and cream-white marks, with species labels and separate dorsal thumbnails.
- Show red and black katipō forms within a single species frame and a pale *L. pallidus* in its own frame, avoiding an invented continuous evolutionary transformation.

## 7. body.hourglass.condition — A Mark That Can Vary

**Suggested kicker:** Area and colour need different explanations.

**Suggested summary:** Field measurements link hourglass area to body condition more clearly than they link hue to condition. Laboratory observations add plasticity evidence, but neither establishes a measure of venom strength. [johnson2017; gburek2014]

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: adult female *L. hesperus* in metropolitan Phoenix and surrounding Sonoran desert, plus laboratory descendants; *L. mactans* in a small dye-ingestion comparison.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 59 / 30 | adult females | Urban / desert field sample | Nine / seven sites; 2013 collections | [johnson2017] |
| 35 | females | Repeated laboratory measurements | Thesis; 12 families, 13 weeks | [gburek2014] |
| 2 | southern widows | Dye-ingestion sample | *L. mactans*; tiny comparative sample | [gillespie1989] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Hourglass area, hue and brightness varied among collection sites. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [johnson2017]
- The published nested analysis found no urban–desert difference in hourglass measurements. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [johnson2017]
- Urban females had larger cephalothoraxes than desert females. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [johnson2017]
- Body condition predicted hourglass area better than the measured colour variables. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [johnson2017]
- Colour estimates came from calibrated photographs rather than a receiver-specific visual model. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus, methods** [johnson2017]
- In the thesis, individuals' higher-condition observations had larger hourglasses. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus, repeated measurements** [gburek2014]
- The planned feeding treatments did not significantly separate average body condition. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus, thesis** [gburek2014]
- Ingested dyes gave southern widows a faint abdominal tinge rather than uniformly recolouring them. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. mactans, two individuals** [gillespie1989]

### TERMS

- **Body condition:** a mass–size index used to estimate physiological state.
- **Plasticity:** variation associated with environmental or physiological circumstances.
- **Residual index:** deviation from a fitted mass–size relationship.
- **Hue / saturation / brightness:** different colour attributes, not synonyms for red-pigment amount.
- **Correlation:** covariation that does not by itself identify a cause.

### Claims to avoid

- The thesis's laboratory analysis selected each individual's low, middle and high condition observations; do not describe it as a clean dose-response experiment of assigned diets.
- Enlargement as an abdomen expands need not mean more pigment was synthesized; do not convert projected area into pigment concentration.
- The published field paper and the thesis's related field chapter are not independent replications; retain the published analysis for urban–desert inference.
- The small dye comparison concerns visible digestive contents, not demonstrated replacement of the hourglass pigment.
- Do not call colour “an honest venom gauge” or assume better-fed animals are redder.
- No significant family effect in a small experiment would not establish zero heritability.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- Present a clearly labelled association plot of body condition and mark area with no venom axis, using the published data only if obtained rather than fabricating points.
- Show an abdomen expanding schematically beside separate labels for projected area and pigment concentration, leaving the latter unmeasured.

## 8. body.hourglass.pigment — What Makes the Red?

**Suggested kicker:** A colour can leave a molecular fingerprint.

**Suggested summary:** Western-widow Raman data support an ommochrome interpretation of the red patch, with explicit uncertainty about chemical identity. Black pigment, white reflectors and glossy surfaces require separate evidence.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: *L. hesperus* red patches and black hairs, *L. hasselti* guanine observations; OTHER SPIDER: salticid scales, crab-spider optics and *Austracantha minax* microscopy/transcriptomics.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ≈595, 1255, 1430, 1490 | cm⁻¹ Raman shift | Red-patch fingerprint | *L. hesperus*, Fig. 5/Table S4; tentative assignment | [hsiung2017] |
| 532 | nm excitation wavelength | The measuring laser | Raman method; not spider reflectance peak | [hsiung2017] |
| 14 / 6 | species / families | Broad black-pigment sample | Includes *L. hesperus* black hairs; not 14 widows | [hsiung2015] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Four western-widow red-patch Raman peaks were tentatively assigned to ommochromes. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [hsiung2017]
- The assignment lacked a commercially available ommochrome reference standard. **established — LATRODECTUS, study limitation** [hsiung2017]
- Similar peaks occurred in sampled orange-red jumping-spider patches. **supported — OTHER SPIDER, Salticidae** [hsiung2017]
- Raman signals consistent with eumelanin occurred in western-widow black hairs. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [hsiung2015]
- Classic spider chemistry distinguished ommatin-like orange-brown pigments from darker ommin-like pigments. **supported — GENERAL SPIDER, chemical survey** [seligy1972]
- The redback behavioural account identifies guanine beneath the male's dorsal abdominal surface. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hasselti** [forster1995]
- Guanine crystals provide reflective material beneath crab-spider hypodermis. **established — OTHER SPIDER, Thomisidae** [gawryszewski2015]
- Christmas-spider microscopy found crystal-like deposits in white regions that were absent from black morphs. **supported — OTHER SPIDER, Austracantha minax** [salgadoroa2026]
- Christmas-spider colour morphs differed especially in expression associated with cuticle metabolism. **supported — OTHER SPIDER, Austracantha minax** [salgadoroa2026]
- Red coloration was lost under ethanol in the *L. occidentalis* description. **established — LATRODECTUS, preservation observation** [valdezmondragon2023]

### TERMS

- **Ommochromes:** a group of tryptophan-derived pigments including ommatins and ommins.
- **Raman shift:** the wavenumber difference between incident and scattered light; cm⁻¹ is not nm.
- **Eumelanin:** a dark, broadly absorbing melanin pigment.
- **Guanine reflector:** crystalline purine material that scatters or reflects light rather than producing red by selective absorption.
- **Hypodermis:** the cellular layer beneath the cuticle.
- **Structural colour:** colour generated by how physical structures interact with light.

### Claims to avoid

- “The red is chemically proved xanthommatin” exceeds the widow evidence.
- Do not assign a purified molecular mixture, concentration, biosynthetic enzyme or precise pigment-cell anatomy to the hourglass without direct analysis.
- Black-hair Raman evidence does not show that eumelanin is the sole source of the entire glossy abdominal black.
- The 2017 paper's pigment–structure interaction and melanosome microscopy in jumping spiders are not demonstrations of structural red or melanosome arrays in a widow hourglass.
- Guanine evidence from other spiders does not chemically identify every white widow stripe.
- The 2026 Christmas-spider gene-expression study is not a widow colour-genetics study.
- Gloss is surface reflection; it is not itself proof of a separate black pigment.
- No verified experiment here shows background matching, a rapid stress-driven hourglass switch, or prey carotenoids becoming the widow's red pigment.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- Pair a ventral western-widow close-up with a labelled four-peak Raman trace, printing “tentative ommochrome assignment” and keeping spectral axes distinct.
- Use separate, taxon-labelled panels for widow red patch, widow black hairs and comparative guanine crystals, rather than inventing one fully resolved widow skin cross-section.

## 9. body.hourglass.evolution — Ancestry Written on Two Surfaces

**Suggested kicker:** Similar colours can have different histories.

**Suggested summary:** Phylogenies make a history of colour testable, but the history of a dorsal patch is not automatically the origin story of a ventral hourglass. Similar-looking markings in relatives require anatomical and functional comparison.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: the genus across molecular phylogenies; OTHER THERIDIID: *Steatoda* and related latrodectines.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | major sampled clades | An early molecular framework | 2004: geometricus group and mactans group; historical result | [garb2004] |
| ≥5 | % dorsal red | A reconstruction category | Adult females; operational coding threshold | [brandley2016] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- The 2004 molecular analysis supported *Latrodectus* monophyly. **supported — LATRODECTUS, sampled mitochondrial phylogeny** [garb2004]
- Its geometricus clade paired brown widows with African *L. rhodesiensis*. **supported — LATRODECTUS, sampled phylogeny** [garb2004]
- Brandley's reconstruction inferred ancestral dorsal red and subsequent loss in some North American lineages. **supported — LATRODECTUS, ancestral-state inference** [brandley2016]
- The 2026 atlas again strongly supported *Latrodectus* monophyly. **supported — LATRODECTUS, updated phylogeny** [miller2026]
- The updated atlas found *Steatoda* non-monophyletic in its sampling. **supported — OTHER THERIDIID** [miller2026]
- *L. umbukwane* changed placement among the atlas's phylogenetic analyses. **supported — LATRODECTUS, method-dependent result** [miller2026]
- A Washington State account describes an hourglass-like ventral outline in *Steatoda* without the widow's reddish-orange fill. **supported — OTHER THERIDIID, genus-level extension description** [johnson2003]
- The katipō colour forms interbred and lacked the diagnostic separation expected of two species. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. katipo** [vink2008]

### TERMS

- **Monophyletic group:** an ancestor and all descendants included in the group.
- **Ancestral-state reconstruction:** inference of earlier character states from a tree and its sampled tips.
- **Homology:** similarity inherited from a common ancestor.
- **Convergence:** independently evolved resemblance.
- **Character coding:** the categories used to convert observations into a comparative dataset.

### Claims to avoid

- No verified family-wide reconstruction establishes the first origin, number of origins or ancestral shape of the ventral hourglass.
- Do not say a red dorsal stripe “moved underneath”; dorsal and ventral markings can coexist.
- An inferred ancestral state is not a fossil observation or a dated origin.
- The older reconstruction should retain its original tree and taxon identities; several historical identifications have since been reconsidered.
- An hourglass-like outline in *Steatoda* is not proof of homologous pigment development, independent warning convergence, Batesian mimicry or equal venom danger.
- Do not use the Barrantes–Eberhard “ontogeny repeats phylogeny” title as colour evidence: that study concerns web construction. [barrantes2010]
- Do not import dorsal aposematism results from unrelated spiders as the origin of widow patterning.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- Draw a simplified, explicitly source-dated widow tree with dorsal and ventral character columns kept separate, displaying uncertainty at inferred ancestors.
- Place a verified *Steatoda* ventral view beside a widow ventral view and label the comparison “resemblance under examination,” without arrows asserting evolutionary direction.

## 10. body.hourglass.identification — A Clue, Not a Verdict

**Suggested kicker:** Identify the spider around the mark.

**Suggested summary:** A ventral hourglass is a valuable field clue, especially in familiar adult females. Reliable identification also considers life stage, geography and additional morphology, with specialist characters where needed. [valdezmondragon2023; miller2026]

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: particularly North American widows, *L. occidentalis*, *L. pallidus* and *L. lucacha*; OTHER THERIDIID: *Steatoda grossa* and other false widows.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spiked | egg-sac surface | A useful accompanying clue | *L. geometricus*; assess alongside the animal | [vetter2012] |
| Dorsal + ventral | documented views | A stronger record | Editorial collecting standard, not an experimental accuracy score | [valdezmondragon2023] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- *Steatoda grossa* shares a rounded abdominal form and cobweb habit with widows. **established — OTHER THERIDIID** [vetter2017]
- The UC account distinguishes *S. grossa* by absence of red ventral coloration. **established — OTHER THERIDIID, species-qualified** [vetter2017]
- A pale ventral hourglass can occur in *L. pallidus*. **established — LATRODECTUS** [lotz1994]
- Immature western widows can resemble adult brown widows. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus versus L. geometricus** [vetter2012c]
- Brown-widow egg sacs bear conspicuous silk spicules. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. geometricus** [vetter2012]
- The Mexican species description combines colour with genital and body-measurement characters. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. occidentalis** [valdezmondragon2023]
- The 2026 atlas integrates molecular clusters, reproductive morphology, abdominal setae and egg-case attributes. **established — LATRODECTUS, taxonomic method** [miller2026]
- Described *L. lucacha* adult females vary in dorsal pattern and can overlap *L. thoracicus*. **established — LATRODECTUS, examined material and field photographs** [miller2026b]
- The *L. lucacha* study uses combined colour, egg-case and geographic evidence when assessing photographs. **established — LATRODECTUS, identification protocol** [miller2026b]

### TERMS

- **Field character:** an accessible clue useful during observation.
- **Diagnosis:** the combination of characters distinguishing a taxon.
- **Voucher:** a specimen or documented record retained so an identification can be checked.
- **Epigynum / palp:** female / male reproductive structures used in identification.
- **Look-alike:** a practical resemblance, without an assumed evolutionary mechanism.

### Claims to avoid

- Do not offer “red hourglass = southern widow” or “no hourglass = harmless.”
- Joined versus split is useful typical patterning, not a reliable stand-alone key to all North American specimens.
- Do not identify sex, maturity, species or venom potency from redness alone.
- A dorsal red patch on a false widow is not a ventral hourglass; *Steatoda* should not be treated as a uniform colour pattern.
- Do not infer a venom diagnosis from a photograph of a skin lesion or an unidentified mark.
- A specimen faded in preservative should not be compared as though its colours were measured alive.
- Institutional guides are regional aids; their simplified language must not override an identified exception or newer taxonomic treatment.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- Design a regional comparison plate of verified adult female and juvenile widows plus *S. grossa*, pairing dorsal/ventral views with locality and life-stage labels.
- Show a brown widow with its spiked egg sac in a tangle web beside a western widow with a smooth sac, avoiding a stand-alone colour swatch as the identification key.
