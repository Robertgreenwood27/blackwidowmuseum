## Reading this dossier

- Bracketed citation keys refer to content/sources.yaml or the NEW entries in SOURCES below. Structure follows research/legs-dossier.md and research/hourglass-dossier.md. CLAUDE.md, the registry, current parent and requested neighbours were consulted; existing exhibit copy is an editorial boundary, not independent verification.
- **established** = securely documented as qualified; **supported** = a particular experiment, limited sample or anatomical interpretation; **debated** = an unresolved explanation. Confidence belongs to the qualified sentence, not automatically to the genus.
- Evidence classes are **LATRODECTUS**, **OTHER THERIDIID**, **OTHER SPIDER**, and **GENERAL SPIDER**. Direct widow microscopy, a comparative spider survey and a general anatomical convention are different evidence. Sources containing several taxa are cited only for the specified animal or comparison.
- FIGURES are candidate fact cards, including qualitative cards where measurements are unavailable. A missing measurement is not zero. Species, sex, stage, preparation, sample and measurement convention travel with each number. HOLD items are research leads, not launch copy.
- Left and right belong to each spider, not the viewer. Palp side, viewing direction, and whether the organ is resting, expanded, dissected or damaged must be recorded. A two-dimensional projection can change the apparent coil count; coil count is not embolus length.
- Use six external palp segments: coxa, trochanter, femur, patella, tibia, tarsus; there is no separately labelled metatarsus. Historical authors additionally discuss the pretarsus, and Snodgrass treats the walking tarsus as subdivided; do not mix conventions to manufacture a seventh palp segment. [jocque2006; snodgrass1952]
- Historical names remain source-qualified. Bhatnagar and Rempel's 1962 “L. curacaviensis” is treated as probably L. hesperus by later authors, rather than modern South American L. curacaviensis; retain the original label when discussing its preparations. Whitehead and Rempel's 1959 “L. mactans” is not automatically a modern southern-widow voucher. [bhatnagar1962; agnarsson2007; whitehead1959]
- Keep each author's sclerite vocabulary attached to the preparation; do not combine historical and modern labels into one inventory. See Hall 3.
- This case owns palp construction, loading, coupling and damage. Female-tract development and storage physiology belong in body.epigynum; paternity strategy in life.mating-plugs; full mating behaviour in life.courtship and life.cannibalism; search ecology in life.mate-search; silk synthesis in web.male-silk; sensory transduction in senses.contact-chemistry. The halls go deeper on the apparatus at those boundaries.
- Every whole-animal image must show male widows as small and slender, often patterned, with the bulb seated in a cupped cymbium at each palp tip. Widows build three-dimensional tangle cobwebs, never orbs. A hanging female's ventral hourglass faces upward because she is inverted. Redbacks' dorsal stripe is a separate marking. Diagram colours do not assert tissue colours.

### Priority qualifications for the current exhibits

1. Replace the parent's “coiled springs” kicker: the coiled embolus conducts sperm, but a spring-powered injection mechanism has not been demonstrated.
2. Preserve the distinction between making sperm in the abdomen and carrying it in the palps; hemolymph inflation is not an open blood-to-semen connection.
3. Keep “two insertions” as the paired-route requirement in redbacks, not a universal maximum number of insertion attempts or a promise that both stores fill.
4. Do not turn embolus-tip loss into loss of the entire pedipalp, automatic male sterility or guaranteed female monandry.
5. Separate experimentally removing a virgin male's tip, natural tip loss after sperm transfer, and losing most of the embolus; these are different treatments and injuries.
6. Use the 2025 brown-widow coupling study for direct in-copula mechanics; do not transfer every result to redbacks or to all Theridiidae.
7. Correct any general claim that adult redbacks or western widows cannot charge their palps again: repeated adult loading was documented in 2013. [modanu2013]
8. Do not present an unqualified species barcode made from coil counts; distinguish a published typical count from a measured within-species range.

## 0. body.pedipalps — The Palps

**Suggested kicker:** A pair of limbs, a pair of sperm carriers.

**Suggested summary:** Palps begin as working appendages beside the mouth. In males their tips develop into complex copulatory bulbs. Eleven halls follow the apparatus from its segments and development to loading, inflation, genital coupling and the tiny piece that can remain behind.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: principally L. hesperus, L. hasselti, L. mactans, L. variolus, L. geometricus, L. revivensis and L. pallidus; regional taxonomic material identified in the relevant halls. OTHER THERIDIID and OTHER SPIDER comparisons are explicitly labelled.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | palps | Paired carriers | Adult male spider plan; one copulatory organ on each palp | [michalik2011] |
| 6 | external segments per palp | One segment fewer than a walking leg | Museum's arachnological convention; pretarsus not added to this count | [jocque2006] |
| Repeated | adult loading | Carriers can refill | L. hasselti and L. hesperus; demonstrated capability, not a field frequency | [modanu2013] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Palpal organs receive sperm produced in the abdominal reproductive system. **established — GENERAL SPIDER** [michalik2011]
- Adult redbacks and western widows can load their palps repeatedly. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hasselti and L. hesperus** [modanu2013]
- Redback plug effectiveness depends on where the broken tip lodges. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hasselti, mating experiments** [snow2006]

These cards and observations orient the parent; the detailed anatomical distinctions belong in the halls.

### TERMS

- **Pedipalp:** the appendage immediately behind a chelicera, ahead of walking leg I.
- **Cymbium:** modified male palpal tarsus supporting the bulb.
- **Palpal bulb:** sperm-carrying copulatory apparatus at the male palp tip.
- **Embolus:** the sperm-conducting intromittent part of the bulb.
- **Haematodocha / hematodocha:** expandable membrane associated with the bulb; alternative spellings.

### Claims to avoid

- The entire swollen palp is not inserted into the female.
- A coil is not evidence of stored torsional energy or a released metal-spring action.
- No genus-wide total sclerite count, hydraulic pressure, rotation angle or sperm capacity is proposed.
- A model assembled from multiple species must be labelled comparative, rather than named as one fully reconstructed widow.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- **1536×1024 museum plate:** a small, slender, patterned male L. hesperus with one palp enlarged, keeping all eight walking legs separate from the two palps; seat the bulb in its cupped cymbium, with orientation marks and restrained cream linework on near-black.
- **1536×1024 museum plate:** three linked anatomical windows—whole appendage, resting bulb, distal fracture—using species-specific references for each window rather than fusing them into one imaginary specimen.

### STAGE NOTES

- **Construction order:** six external segments (Hall 1); cymbium/alveolus → basal haematodocha/subtegulum → middle haematodocha/tegulum → embolic attachment/embolus. Conductor, median apophysis and TTA project around the tegulum rather than forming a serial chain. Use the 2007 vocabulary. [jocque2006; agnarsson2007]
- **Inflation slider:** membranes expand and permit bulb rotation/tilt; no measured widow rotation sequence was verified. Brown-widow proximal positions vary while distal locks persist. Keep motions schematic, without independently spinning sclerites or an invented degree scale. [huber2004; sentenska2025]
- **Coil presets:** L. mactans ≈3; L. variolus ≈2; type L. hesperus ≈2 with departures; L. geometricus ≈4. These are typical descriptions, not measured species ranges; see Hall 5. HOLD an L. hasselti range and all unsupported minimum–maximum presets. [kaston1970; garb2004]
- **Micrometre inset:** use Hall 8's species-qualified fracture measurements; they cannot supply a pressure or flow-rate calculation.
- **Coupling toggle:** preserve the anatomical sides and contacts documented in Hall 7 when changing camera or mating posture.

## 1. body.pedipalps.appendage — A Leg That Became a Tool

**Suggested kicker:** Beside the mouth, before the legs.

**Suggested summary:** A palp shares the jointed construction of a walking limb but has its own segment plan. Its base participates in feeding, while the distal end differs sharply among females, juveniles and adult males.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: L. geometricus embryos and female characters in the theridiid morphological survey. GENERAL SPIDER: external segmentation and mouthpart relationships. OTHER SPIDER: source-named Eurypelma hentzi for Snodgrass's illustrated female claw and tendons.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2nd | appendage pair | Palps follow chelicerae | Prosomal appendage order; not the second walking-leg pair | [snodgrass1952] |
| 6 / 7 | external segments | Palp / walking leg | Coxa included; palp lacks a separately named metatarsus | [jocque2006] |
| 1 | usual female palpal claw | A different endpoint | General spider description; not three walking-foot claws | [jocque2006] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Palps comprise coxa, trochanter, femur, patella, tibia and tarsus. **established — GENERAL SPIDER, external-segment convention** [jocque2006]
- The palp lacks the walking leg's separate metatarsus. **established — GENERAL SPIDER** [jocque2006]
- Palpal coxal lobes border the passage leading to the mouth. **established — GENERAL SPIDER, comparative anatomy** [snodgrass1952]
- L. geometricus embryos develop a medial endite on the pedipalp. **supported — LATRODECTUS, embryological series** [liu2009]
- Female theridiid palpal claws vary among lineages, with absence recorded in Phoroncidia rather than as a widow character. **supported — OTHER THERIDIID, comparative character survey** [agnarsson2004]
- The illustrated female Eurypelma claw is operated through levator and depressor tendons. **established — OTHER SPIDER, E. hentzi anatomy** [snodgrass1952]

### TERMS

- **Coxa:** basal limb segment attached to the prosoma.
- **Endite / gnathendite:** inwardly directed lobe associated with the palpal coxa in modern descriptions.
- **Maxilla:** traditional name for the palpal feeding lobe, not an additional appendage pair.
- **Pretarsus:** terminal claw-bearing apparatus in historical anatomical terminology.
- **Serial homology:** correspondence among repeated appendages; not proof of a particular evolutionary transformation sequence.

### Claims to avoid

- The title is an anatomical analogy, not evidence that a recent widow walking leg literally became a palp.
- Female and juvenile palps are not defective male bulbs; their feeding and sensory roles remain meaningful.
- Do not label the feeding lobes independently hinged insect jaws or put venom fangs on the palps.
- Female palpal-claw presence is consistent with the general and comparative accounts; HOLD a widow species-by-stage claw-tooth count and detailed claw-force map.
- Snodgrass's claw illustration is a tarantula comparator, not a dissection of Latrodectus.
- Signpost body.body-plan and body.legs for appendage placement, and body.digestion.mouth-and-pump for the complete food-filtering apparatus.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- **1536×1024 museum plate:** ventral female-widow mouthpart locator, two chelicerae, two palpal coxae/endites and two six-segment palps; show the female claw without inventing tooth counts, and distinguish it from the adult male's bulb-in-cymbium inset.
- **1536×1024 museum plate:** parallel palp/leg segment strips, aligned at coxa and tibia, with the walking metatarsus clearly present; a tiny slender patterned male provides scale, not a female-shaped male silhouette.

## 2. body.pedipalps.development — Growing a Bulb

**Suggested kicker:** Swollen does not yet mean functional.

**Suggested summary:** The male palp advertises approaching maturity before it exposes a working adult organ. A direct widow account establishes the external sequence; modern imaging in another theridiid shows why an apparently simple swelling can conceal extensive remodelling.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: Kaston's reared American widows, especially the L. mactans series in Fig. 12; historical “L. curacaviensis” in Bhatnagar and Rempel. OTHER THERIDIID: Parasteatoda tepidariorum, Quade et al.'s imaging series. No common developmental clock is assumed.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | molt remaining | Penultimate male | Stage definition; conspicuous palpal swelling in the widow account | [kaston1970] |
| 2 | molts remaining | Earlier swelling | Antepenultimate males can already be recognizable; not uniform among individuals | [kaston1970] |
| Pre-subadult → subadult → adult | developmental sequence | A comparator's transformation | P. tepidariorum; source terminology, not numbered widow instars | [quade2019] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Kaston's widow males have conspicuously swollen palpal tarsi in the penultimate instar. **established — LATRODECTUS, reared American species** [kaston1970]
- He also recognized swelling in antepenultimate males. **supported — LATRODECTUS, individual developmental series** [kaston1970]
- Earlier swelling was inconsistent among his specimens. **supported — LATRODECTUS, external sexing observations** [kaston1970]
- In Parasteatoda, bulb development extensively reorganizes the distal palp during the subadult stage. **supported — OTHER THERIDIID, P. tepidariorum, three-dimensional developmental imaging** [quade2019]
- The Parasteatoda bulb arises internally before its adult form is exposed at the final molt. **supported — OTHER THERIDIID, P. tepidariorum** [quade2019]
- In that comparator, the claw-associated primordium contributes to bulb formation. **supported — OTHER THERIDIID, P. tepidariorum, developmental interpretation** [quade2019]

### TERMS

- **Instar:** stage between molts; specify the author's numbering convention.
- **Penultimate / subadult:** the stage immediately before the adult molt.
- **Antepenultimate:** the stage two molts before adulthood.
- **Primordium / anlage:** developing tissue from which a structure forms.
- **Histogenesis:** development and reorganization of tissues.

### Claims to avoid

- A smooth swollen juvenile palp is not an exposed adult copulatory bulb or proof of sperm loading.
- Do not promise reliable sexing from every very young widow's palp or assign one universal earliest sexable instar.
- HOLD a complete Latrodectus male histological sequence from the 1962 plates: their existence is verified, but the full developmental plates were not accessible in this pass.
- Parasteatoda's transformation is comparative evidence, not permission to copy its exact tissue movements or timetable into a widow animation.
- Final molt, cuticle hardening, first sperm induction and successful mating are separate events; no fixed “hours after molt” interval was verified for this dossier.
- Signpost life.molting and life.development for sex-dependent instar totals, temperature, food and maturation time; keep this hall focused on the palp.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- **1536×1024 museum plate:** source-based widow palp sequence from earlier juvenile through swollen penultimate to adult; use equal orientation but label magnification changes, and place the adult bulb within its cupped cymbium rather than drawing a glove that splits into fingers.
- **1536×1024 museum plate:** separately boxed P. tepidariorum developmental cutaway labelled OTHER THERIDIID; pair with a small, slender, patterned widow male locator and no invented day scale.

## 3. body.pedipalps.bulb-anatomy — Parts of the Bulb

**Suggested kicker:** A cup, a reservoir and interlocking pieces.

**Suggested summary:** The bulb is an assembly of hard sclerites, softer attachments and an internal sperm pathway. Names help locate those parts only when their author's terminology stays attached; a historical radix is not automatically an extra structure beside a modern TTA.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: historical “L. curacaviensis,” L. geometricus and L. revivensis; Latrodectus material in the 2004 family analysis. OTHER THERIDIID: comparative anatomy used to define sclerite correspondences.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | named sperm-pathway regions | Fundus → reservoir → ejaculatory duct | L. revivensis cleared palp; regions of one system | [berendonck2002] |
| 60 / 29 | species / genera illustrated | The comparative naming problem | Theridiidae, 2007 study; not widow species totals | [agnarsson2007] |
| Cup-shaped | terminal support | Cymbium and alveolus | Modified tarsus and its bulb-bearing depression; identify both, not two additional limb segments | [jocque2006; agnarsson2007] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- The historical widow account describes a spoon-shaped cymbium supporting the male organ. **established — LATRODECTUS, source-identified L. curacaviensis** [bhatnagar1962]
- The subtegulum and tegulum belong to the bulb's basal and principal divisions. **established — GENERAL SPIDER, comparative anatomical framework** [agnarsson2007]
- The 2007 scheme distinguishes conductor, median apophysis and theridiid tegular apophysis. **supported — OTHER THERIDIID, family-wide homology analysis** [agnarsson2007]
- In Latrodectus, a hooked embolus base fits the grooved conductor base. **supported — LATRODECTUS, comparative microscopy** [agnarsson2004]
- The L. revivensis spermophor comprises fundus, reservoir and a narrow outgoing duct. **established — LATRODECTUS, cleared palps** [berendonck2002]
- Its embolus combines a hardened outer truncus with a membranous inner pars pendula. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. revivensis microscopy** [berendonck2002]

### TERMS

- **Cymbium / alveolus:** supporting modified tarsus / depression in it receiving the bulb.
- **Subtegulum / tegulum:** basal / main bulb sclerites; identify membranes between them.
- **Median apophysis:** named tegular-associated process; state the nomenclatural scheme.
- **Conductor / TTA:** distinct structures in the 2007 scheme; TTA means theridiid tegular apophysis.
- **Radix:** historical name applied to an embolic-associated structure; correspondence requires the source plate.
- **Spermophor / spermophore:** internal sperm-containing duct system; fundus is its blind end, reservoir its enlarged storage region.

### Claims to avoid

- Do not equate the external embolus coil with every loop of the internal sperm reservoir.
- No claim that every source recognizes the same number of independent sclerites is defensible.
- “Median” does not mean that the apophysis must occupy the visual centre of every resting view.
- The 1962 list includes radix and terminal apophysis; the 2025 study follows the 2007 vocabulary, while the 2026 atlas uses numbered tegular apophyses. Preserve these author-specific labels until a plate-to-plate correspondence is demonstrated.
- HOLD a one-to-one radix = TTA = TA1 translation, a complete adult male nerve map and a new universal genus diagram assembled from generic orb-weaver parts.
- The conductor's name does not prove that its free tip carries sperm or is itself intromittent.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- **1536×1024 museum plate:** three views of a referenced L. geometricus palp, relaxed, with a cutaway through the cupped cymbium; separate internal spermophor from external sclerites and label the vocabulary “Agnarsson et al. 2007.”
- **1536×1024 museum plate:** historical/modern terminology comparison using separate outlines keyed to their original plates, with unresolved correspondences shown as unresolved; include a slender patterned male locator and no female-shaped “male” body.

## 4. body.pedipalps.inflation — The Hydraulic Unfolding

**Suggested kicker:** Pressure changes the shape of the apparatus.

**Suggested summary:** Expandable membranes allow a compact bulb to move during coupling. Direct widow observations connect inflation with insertion, while comparative anatomy explains the possibilities of folds and pressure without supplying a measured widow pressure curve.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: historical “L. mactans” in Whitehead and Rempel; Kaston's American widows; L. geometricus in the 2025 in-copula study. GENERAL SPIDER / OTHER SPIDER: Huber's histological comparison of genital actuation.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | muscles reported in male palpal pretarsus | A local absence | Historical L. mactans anatomical account; not the whole appendage | [whitehead1959] |
| 107 / 73 | species / families examined | Comparative actuation survey | Huber's serial sections; not 107 widows | [huber2004] |
| Shallow → full | observed inflation sequence | Insertion then fuller inflation | L. geometricus, author's live observations reported with cryofixation methods; not measured pressure | [sentenska2025] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Whitehead and Rempel reported no muscles in the male palpal pretarsus. **supported — LATRODECTUS, historical L. mactans, anatomical survey** [whitehead1959]
- Most entelegyne bulbs in Huber's survey lack bulbal muscles. **supported — GENERAL SPIDER, comparative histological survey with exceptions** [huber2004]
- Haematodochal fold geometry can generate expansion, tilt or rotation. **supported — GENERAL SPIDER, comparative functional interpretation** [huber2004]
- Kaston observed widow haematodochae distending and deflating after insertion. **established — LATRODECTUS, American-widow mating observations** [kaston1970]
- Brown-widow insertion involved shallow inflation before the full inflations used to time fixation. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. geometricus, reported live observations** [sentenska2025]
- The brown-widow account attributes inflation to hemolymph entering the bulb. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. geometricus, author's functional account** [sentenska2025]

### TERMS

- **Haematodocha:** expandable cuticular membrane associated with the bulb.
- **Hemolymph:** circulating body fluid, distinct from semen inside the spermophor.
- **Basal / middle haematodocha:** membranes associated with different bulb divisions in the comparative scheme.
- **Sclerite:** relatively hardened cuticular piece.
- **Hydraulic actuation:** movement produced by pressure acting on a fluid-filled structure.

### Claims to avoid

- “The male palp has no muscles” incorrectly expands a local pretarsal/bulbal result to its proximal segments.
- Do not import body.legs.hydraulics' house-spider pressure measurements into the palp.
- The absence of a muscle within the bulb does not demonstrate absence of nerves or sensory feedback everywhere on the palp.
- HOLD species-specific pressure in kPa, rotation angles, inflation frequency, energy cost and sperm flow per inflation: none was verified.
- Do not infer that a visible inflation equals a counted sperm dose or that blood and sperm flow down one common channel.
- The three membrane categories in the comparative scheme are not three identical balloons in every widow; trace the selected species' preparation before drawing them.
- Signpost body.legs.muscles and body.circulation for broader muscle and fluid systems.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- **1536×1024 museum plate:** resting and expanded palp in matched orientation, bulb supported in the cupped cymbium; show hemolymph space separately from the sperm duct, with unnumbered movement arrows and “schematic” clearly marked.
- **1536×1024 museum plate:** L. geometricus in-copula inset based on the 2025 reconstruction, showing the tiny slender patterned male and the female's epigynal region; distinguish observations of inflation from static micro-CT anatomy.

## 5. body.pedipalps.the-coil — Counting the Turns

**Suggested kicker:** A useful character with a counting problem.

**Suggested summary:** Coil counts helped organize widow taxonomy, but overlap and viewing conventions complicate their use. This hall teaches how to trace an embolus and why a typical value must not become an absolute species rule.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: L. mactans, L. hesperus, L. variolus and L. geometricus in historical American comparisons; regional species groups discussed by Garb et al.; L. lucacha in its original description. L. hasselti is included as an explicit numerical gap.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ≈3 | embolus turns | Southern widow reference | L. mactans, historical typical description; not a population range | [kaston1970] |
| ≈2 / ≈2 | embolus turns | Northern / western reference | L. variolus / type L. hesperus; western departures documented, so not exclusive diagnoses | [kaston1970] |
| ≈4 | embolus turns | Brown widow reference | L. geometricus, historical descriptive category; projection-dependent appearance | [garb2004; kaston1970] |
| ≈3 | full spiral turns | A specified viewing region | L. lucacha, retrolateral part of palp in original diagnosis; not directly interchangeable with every older count | [miller2026b] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Kaston illustrated sibling western-widow palps with misleadingly different apparent counts. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus, reared siblings** [kaston1970]
- His brown-widow comparisons can show three or four coils over different parts of the circumference. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. geometricus, illustration comparison** [kaston1970]
- Garb's molecular results did not recover the historical coil-count species groups as natural groups. **supported — LATRODECTUS, sampled mitochondrial phylogeny** [garb2004]
- The L. lucacha diagnosis explicitly locates its approximately three turns on the retrolateral palp. **established — LATRODECTUS, taxonomic diagnosis** [miller2026b]
- The 2026 atlas questions consistency of loop-count methods among observers. **supported — LATRODECTUS, authors' methodological assessment** [miller2026]

### TERMS

- **Turn:** one complete revolution of a traced structure about a specified reference axis.
- **Partial turn:** fraction of a revolution; report its inclusion rule.
- **Retrolateral / prolateral:** standard rear-facing / front-facing sides of an appendage.
- **Projection:** a two-dimensional view of a three-dimensional structure.
- **Intraspecific variation:** variation among individuals assigned to one species.
- **Homoplasy:** similar character states arising independently or by reversal.

### Claims to avoid

- The displayed values are source-described exemplars, not verified species-wide ranges; do not display “always 2,” “exactly 3” or zero-width ranges.
- HOLD a numerical redback L. hasselti coil range: no sufficiently explicit comparable count was verified here; do not count a convenient photograph and attribute the result to Levi.
- Do not substitute female duct loops for male embolus turns; female paths can include a reversal and are not a simple matching helix.
- Do not pool historical broad “mactans” specimens as modern L. mactans or interpret an old two-coil “curacaviensis group” as today's L. curacaviensis alone.
- Proposed recording convention: state side and view; trace base to intact tip in 3D or complementary views; record total revolutions and endpoint fraction separately; flag missing tips, displaced coils and flattening. This is an editorial protocol, not a claim that all historical authors followed it.
- Coil number alone does not measure embolus length, duct length, genetic distance or mechanical isolation.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- **1536×1024 museum plate:** a single referenced intact palp in three aligned views, tracing the same embolus centreline with numbered complete turns and a separately marked fraction; bulb sits in the cupped cymbium, with a small slender male locator.
- **1536×1024 museum plate:** source-labelled American and brown-widow exemplar outlines, each with view, palp side and “typical description, not species range”; leave the redback numeric tile visibly HOLD rather than inventing a coil.

## 6. body.pedipalps.sperm-induction — Loading the Palps

**Suggested kicker:** Production and delivery happen in different places.

**Suggested summary:** The male must move sperm from his abdominal opening to the organs at the front of his body. Loading, storage and delivery are distinct steps, and encapsulated sperm cells differ from the external coil that carries them.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: L. hasselti and L. hesperus, repeated adult loading; L. revivensis, encapsulated sperm in anatomical sections. GENERAL SPIDER: sperm-web transfer and terminology of sperm packages. OTHER THERIDIID comparisons in the comparative sperm survey are not treated as genus-wide widow observations.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | widow species tested | Repeated adult loading | L. hasselti and L. hesperus; capability, not loading frequency in nature | [modanu2013] |
| 1 | sperm cell per individual capsule | Cleistospermia | Definition of a transfer form, not sperm count per palp | [michalik2014] |
| Sperm web → palp | transfer route | An external loading step | General spider account; not direct internal tubing from testes to palps | [michalik2011; abalos1963] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Spider sperm induction transfers abdominally released semen from silk into the palpal organs. **established — GENERAL SPIDER** [michalik2011; abalos1963]
- Palps are secondary sperm-transfer organs rather than sites of spermatogenesis. **established — GENERAL SPIDER** [michalik2011]
- Redbacks and western widows lack permanent adult sperm depletion in the 2013 study. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hasselti and L. hesperus** [modanu2013]
- Both species transferred sperm into their palps more than once as adults. **supported — LATRODECTUS, adult loading observations** [modanu2013]
- Recently mated L. revivensis females contained encapsulated sperm in the anterior spermathecal lobe. **established — LATRODECTUS, histology and microscopy** [berendonck2005]
- Cleistospermia enclose single cells, whereas coenospermia enclose several individually coiled sperm cells together. **established — GENERAL SPIDER, comparative sperm terminology** [michalik2014]

### TERMS

- **Sperm induction:** filling the palpal organs from externally deposited semen.
- **Spermatogenesis:** production and development of sperm in the primary reproductive organs.
- **Sperm web:** silk supporting semen during indirect loading.
- **Cleistospermium:** one individually encapsulated sperm cell.
- **Coenospermium:** several sperm cells enclosed together within a common secretion sheath.
- **Decapsulation:** loss of the sperm's surrounding secretion capsule; distinct from full uncoiling or demonstrated motility.

### Claims to avoid

- Do not draw freely swimming mammalian-shaped sperm racing down the embolus during insemination.
- Encapsulated sperm in a widow is not evidence for a large multicellular sperm packet; individual encapsulation and coenospermia must stay distinct.
- Direct L. revivensis morphology supports individually encapsulated cells; the comparative classification supports cleistospermia as the appropriate framework, not an ultrastructural survey of every Latrodectus species.
- HOLD exact widow capsule diameter, sperm capacity per palp, universal sperm-web dimensions, first-loading latency after the final molt, and a universal recharge interval.
- Repeated loading as an adult does not by itself establish successful reloading after every kind of embolus damage; a virgin male experimentally lacking a tip is another treatment.
- The mechanisms drawing semen into the duct and releasing it should not be reduced to an unverified simple suction pump; separate established route from unresolved fluid mechanics.
- Signpost web.male-silk for silk-gland expression, life.mate-search for departure from the juvenile web and body.epigynum for storage/activation after delivery.

### TERMS

- **Ipsilateral / contralateral:** same-side / opposite-side insertion, using each animal's anatomy.
- **Self-bracing:** contacts among the male's own genital structures that stabilize coupling.

- **Mechanical isolation:** failure of genital coupling or transfer that contributes to reproductive isolation.
- **Hybridization:** reproduction between individuals assigned to different species.

### Claims to avoid

- The title is a mechanical metaphor, not proof that natural selection produced a species-recognition lock.

- A failed heterospecific courtship, failed insertion, sperm transfer without embryos and sterile offspring are different barriers.
- Laboratory hybrids do not measure wild hybridization, gene flow or disappearance risk; signpost genus.hybrids and genus.katipo for those questions.

**Suggested kicker:** The fracture has a location and a shape.

**Suggested summary:** The distal embolus includes a differentiated terminal piece and a repeatable fracture region. Microscopy can locate that piece within the female tract; experiments are needed to establish whether its position actually blocks a rival.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: L. revivensis, detailed light/SEM anatomy; historical Argentine mactans/curacaviensis, L. geometricus and Australian hasselti in Abalos and Báez; L. hasselti, experimental plug study; L. pallidus, field genital inspections.

- A saddle-like thickening marks its fracture region. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. revivensis** [berendonck2002]
- Its outlet lies near, rather than at, the terminal apex. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. revivensis** [berendonck2002]

### TERMS

- **Apical sclerite / embolus tip:** differentiated distal piece that can detach.
- **Fracture region:** localized area where separation occurs; not necessarily a muscular joint.
- **Truncus:** hardened main portion of the embolus in the cited anatomical descriptions.
- **Pars pendula:** membranous portion along its inner side.
- **Bore / outer diameter:** width of the lumen / full external structure; never interchangeable.
- **Mating plug:** obstruction that limits subsequent insemination; effectiveness must be tested.

### Claims to avoid

- Detachment of the apical piece is not amputation of the entire palp or routine removal of every embolus coil.
- The saddle-like thickening locates a break; it does not alone measure cuticle strength or identify the force causing fracture.
- HOLD a universal fracture-force threshold, a male actively “snapping” a hinge, and female muscular control of breakage as a demonstrated genus mechanism.
- A tip missing from the male is not automatically recovered in the female, and a recovered tip is not automatically an effective plug.
- Do not scale the old approximately 200-µm report to a 300–400-µm L. revivensis specimen or average historically pooled taxa into a genus mean.
- A static retained tip can establish position, not the precise instant of fracture or ejaculation.
- Signpost life.mating-plugs for sperm competition and paternity outcomes; this hall owns the material fragment and where it fits.

- **1536×1024 museum plate:** three tract positions—tip through the spermathecal entrance, tip stranded in duct, more extensive embolus damage—clearly species- and evidence-labelled; use no external cork sealing the entire epigynum.

## 9. body.pedipalps.two-palps — Two Palps, Two Chances

**Suggested kicker:** One route can remain unused.

**Suggested summary:** Paired apparatus makes the order of mating matter. A male may be interrupted before using the other side, yet loss of a terminal tip does not make every later mating mechanically impossible.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: L. hasselti, experimentally constrained insemination and redback behavioural observations; L. mactans, serial laboratory matings; L. pallidus, organ-damage study. Whole-palp self-amputation in other theridiids is only a comparative warning.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | female tract per palp | Separate delivery routes | L. hasselti; experimentally assigning a side by removing the other embolus | [snow2005] |
| 180 | degrees, whole-body turn | The redback somersault | L. hasselti after insertion; NOT bulb rotation angle | [forster1992] |
| 3 | females inseminated per male | More than two lifetime chances | L. mactans, two males offered three successive virgin females each; offspring criterion | [breene1985] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Redback experiments achieved mating with one embolus after the other was removed. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hasselti, experimental manipulation** [snow2005]
- Redback males can survive the first insertion and use the other palp. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. hasselti, behavioural observations** [forster1992]
- Redbacks that lose access after one insertion can leave the other female tract uninseminated. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hasselti, paired-tract experiments** [snow2005]
- Experimentally tipless redbacks that copulated transferred sperm in quantities similar to intact males. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hasselti, conditional on successful mating** [snow2006]
- Two southern-widow males each inseminated three females in succession. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. mactans, small laboratory series** [breene1985]
- Palpal damage did not invariably sterilize white-widow males. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. pallidus, outdoor-enclosure experiment** [segoli2008b]

### TERMS

- **Insertion / intromission:** introducing the embolus; distinguish an attempt from successful coupling.
- **Insemination:** sperm transfer, distinct from embryo production or paternity.
- **Functional sterility:** failure to produce offspring under specified conditions, regardless of sperm production.
- **Palp loss / tip loss:** loss of the appendage / loss of the small distal embolus piece.
- **Residual reproductive value:** potential future reproduction after a current mating opportunity.

### Claims to avoid

- “Two palps = only two lifetime matings” is contradicted by the southern-widow series.
- Do not describe a male with one embolus removed as having no second appendage; the manipulation controls genital use, not necessarily locomotor or sensory palp functions.
- Successful tipless insemination is experimentally supported, but it is not proof of equal mating success, equal sperm loading or equal fertility after severe damage.
- The redback removal experiment found reduced mating success in manipulated males; its comparable sperm counts apply to those that actually mated.
- Distinguish continuing with residual sperm from making new sperm, reloading a damaged organ and successful re-insemination; those require different observations.
- HOLD routine self-amputation of a whole palp in Latrodectus; the famous single-palp specializations of Tidarren are OTHER THERIDIID evidence. [michalik2014]
- The somersault, grooming, renewed courtship and cannibalism sequence belongs in life.courtship and life.cannibalism; it is not identical across the genus.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- **1536×1024 museum plate:** redback male progression, first insertion, withdrawal, second-palp insertion, with two anatomically sided pathway indicators; show the tiny slender patterned male's body turning while the inserted bulb remains supported by its cymbium.
- **1536×1024 museum plate:** intact tip, terminal-tip loss and major embolus loss as separate silhouettes; add a source-labelled southern-widow serial-mating inset, without suggesting that every damaged organ works equally well.

## 10. body.pedipalps.senses-and-tasks — Palps at Work

**Suggested kicker:** The same appendage also touches and handles.

**Suggested summary:** A bulb does not replace every other palp function. Surface hairs, mouth-adjacent contacts and observed tapping belong on the anatomical map, while their sensory mechanisms and the full courtship repertoire remain in neighbouring cases.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: male and female L. hesperus examined by SEM and in behavioural trials; L. hasselti for palp grooming and probing accounts. GENERAL SPIDER: mouthpart and feeding framework. Detailed widow palp-specific web-handling kinematics remain a gap.

### OBSERVATIONS

- Western-widow palps carry curved, blunt, open-tipped hairs on their ventral surfaces. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus, SEM** [ross1979]
- Ross and Smith found these presumed chemoreceptors in both sexes. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [ross1979]
- Their sharp-tipped presumed mechanosensory hairs differ from the open-tipped hairs. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus, anatomical interpretation** [ross1979]

### TERMS

- **Sensillum:** cuticular sensory structure together with its sensory apparatus.
- **Contact chemoreception:** detection of chemicals through direct contact.
- **Mechanoreception:** detection of deformation, movement or mechanical force.
- **Tip pore:** opening at a sensory hair's end; not the entire hair-tip diameter.
- **Grooming:** cleaning movements; their occurrence does not establish every proposed physiological benefit.
- **Bioassay:** test using an organism's response to detect biological activity.

### Claims to avoid

- Hair shape plus courtship on silk supports a sensory interpretation; it does not identify the exact palp neuron responding to a purified pheromone.
- Do not apply walking-tarsus hair measurements to every palp seta or call all palpal hairs taste receptors.
- No full palp sensory-neuron count, receptor-density map, force threshold or genus-wide difference between sexes was verified.
- Whole-palp sensation and innervation of the bulb itself are separate questions; muscle absence does not answer either.
- The palp's feeding base does not make its bulb a chewing organ; do not show teeth masticating prey with the embolus.
- Web contact during courtship is documented, but HOLD a claim that the palp is the primary silk-cutting, wrapping or thread-tensioning tool without a palp-specific ethogram; thread handling by chelicerae and legs must remain correctly attributed.
- Signpost senses.contact-chemistry for pheromone chemistry, life.courtship for signalling sequences, web.male-silk for added silk, and body.digestion for feeding. No measured cleaning advantage or sterilizing effect is asserted.

### TERMS

- **SEM:** scanning electron microscopy, revealing surface detail.

### Claims to avoid

- Neither “a microscope always settles it” nor “all widow genitalia are identical” represents the literature adequately.

- SEM of an isolated tip answers a surface-anatomy question; micro-CT of coupled organs answers a spatial question, not by itself a flow-rate or fracture-force question.
- Do not publish a new genus-wide count of species from this dossier; the recent atlas and catalogue require their own dated treatment in genus.naming and genus.roster.
- Knoflach and van Harten 2002 was bibliographically located but its complete treatment was not retrieved; use it as an explicitly uninspected plate lead rather than silently citing it for a new coil count.

Outdoor mating and retained-tip observations support partial exclusion and incomplete sterility; this is not a redback paternity experiment. [segoli2008b]
- **Somersault:** [Forster 1992](https://doi.org/10.1071/ZO9920001), primary abstract, and [Forster 1995](https://museum.wa.gov.au/sites/default/files/3.%20Forster.pdf), behavioural account, support whole-body turning and use of the second palp. These do not provide a measured bulb-rotation angle. [forster1992; forster1995]
- **Sensory surface:** [Ross and Smith](https://www.americanarachnology.org/journal-joa/joa-all-articles/article/download/JoA_v7_p69.pdf/), pp. 74–75, Fig. 2, readable primary text: measurements, shape and ventral-palpal distribution. Open tips support “presumed chemoreceptors”; there is no purified-pheromone recording from an identified palpal sensillum in this paper. [ross1979]
- **Taxonomic variation:** [Garb et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ympev.2003.10.012), pp. 1135–1136, indexed primary text, discusses historical coil groups and their mismatch with the molecular result. [Lotz](https://the-eis.com/elibrary/sites/default/files/downloads/literature/Revision%20of%20the%20genus%20Latrodectus.pdf), generic character discussion and Fig. 16, indexed primary text; direct complete retrieval timed out. Use its regional illustrations and seta characters, not an invented coil-range table. [garb2004; lotz1994]
- **Yemen treatment:** [WSC bibliographic record](https://wsc.nmbe.ch/reference/9337) verifies Knoflach and van Harten 2002, pp. 321–361. Full text was not retrieved. The [2026 atlas](https://doi.org/10.1093/zoolinnean/zlag145) refers to its retained-tip plates; those references are useful locators, not a claim that this dossier inspected the original plates. [knoflach2002; miller2026]
- **Recent diagnoses and naming:** [L. lucacha original description](https://doi.org/10.3897/zookeys.1281.185973), diagnosis and palp views, indexed primary text; [2026 atlas](https://doi.org/10.1093/zoolinnean/zlag145), loop-count discussion and Fig. 55; [WSC genus catalogue](https://wsc.nmbe.ch/genus-catalog/3681/Latrodectus), checked during this pass. ### Editorial boundaries and remaining measurements

- Preserve all eleven proposed hall ids as suggestions; existing accession BWM-01.12 remains with the parent. Do not allocate child accession numbers in this research hand-off.
- Retain source, species, preparation and confidence when reducing observations to exhibit copy. A labelled comparative diagram is preferable to a falsely complete widow reconstruction.
- Separate three levels of evidence: illustrated anatomy, observed movement, and experimentally demonstrated reproductive consequence. A static contact alone cannot show a force, a sperm dose or an evolutionary cause.
- The source-specific coiled structures, membrane folds and scale bars are sufficient to commission reference-based plates; they are not yet a measured 3D simulation of one male's entire palp.
- Remaining quantitative gaps: comparable coil-count ranges for each requested species (especially L. hasselti), standardized base-to-tip path lengths, micro-CT morphometrics across males, widow pressure and rotation curves, fracture forces, sperm capacity and first-loading/reloading latency, palp-specific sensory-neuron inventories and web-handling kinematics.
- Search gaps are outcomes of this single pass, not assertions that the literature contains no such information. HOLD items may stay out of published content without preventing the source-backed halls from opening.
- Image briefs are commissioning instructions, not generated assets or independent evidence. Use actual referenced specimens and original scale bars; mark interpretive cutaways, avoid tracing an orb-weaver palp into a widow, and preserve the bulb's support within the cymbium.
