# Senses — research dossier

Researched 23 November 2026. Scope: BWM-04, the four existing Senses cases, all eleven proposed topics, and two further exhibits. Existing ids are preserved; other ids, kickers and summaries are suggestions. This is the single-file research hand-off; neither exhibit YAML nor the source registry is edited.

## Reading this dossier

- Bracketed citation keys refer to `content/sources.yaml` or the NEW entries in SOURCES below. The two model dossiers and the registry were read before drafting. A registry entry alone is not verification: retained claims were checked against accessible publication text, author manuscripts, abstracts or explicitly identified primary-source excerpts.
- **established** = directly documented and secure as qualified; **supported** = a particular experiment, limited sample or interpretation; **debated** = an unresolved mechanism or explanation. These labels apply to the qualified sentence, never automatically to the genus.
- **LATRODECTUS** evidence names the species. **OTHER THERIDIID** means a different cobweb-spider genus; **OTHER SPIDER** means a more distant comparator. **GENERAL SPIDER** identifies a general anatomical explanation. Comparative thresholds are not widow thresholds.
- FIGURES are candidate fact cards, not illustrations. They distinguish morphology, neural thresholds, apparatus settings, behaviour, sample sizes and environmental conditions. A stimulus that elicits a response is not necessarily the minimum detectable stimulus. Qualitative cards and fewer observations are retained where numerical evidence is thin.
- Vibrational velocity in mm s⁻¹ is neither thread displacement nor spider speed. Hz describes repetition frequency; dB SPL describes sound pressure, even when the animal probably detects air-particle motion. Micrometres (µm) and nanometres (nm) must not be interchanged.
- Suggested summaries are editorial syntheses with citations. Gaps and unverified candidates appear only in “Claims to avoid” or verification notes, never as affirmative observations or numerical facts.
- Taxonomic names follow the checked [World Spider Catalog genus listing](https://wsc.nmbe.ch/species-list/3681/Latrodectus). The redback is styled *L. hasselti* here; some primary papers print *hasseltii*. Historical specimen names are not silently reassigned. [wsc2026]

### Priority qualifications for the current exhibits

1. Trichobothria detect air motion; slit sensilla detect cuticular deformation. Neither phrase by itself establishes a widow's hearing range. [barth2004]
2. A brown-widow sensory map exists, but it is a small, author-posted undergraduate thesis, not a physiological threshold study. Its counts need that qualification. [parra2009]
3. “Widow courtship has no temporal structure” describes neither the full repertoire nor later courtship sequences. [sivalinghem2021; sivalinghem2021b]
4. Tip-pore hairs were imaged in *L. hesperus*; matching an individual widow hair to a particular pheromone still requires physiology. [ross1979]
5. Male wall-pore olfactory sensilla are experimentally demonstrated in *Argiope bruennichi*, not in *Latrodectus* in the material verified here. [talukder2025]
6. Eyes, light responses and circadian organization deserve a full case; “poor vision” must not become “blind.” [forster1995; mah2020]

## 1. senses.trichobothria — Hairs That Read the Air

**Suggested kicker:** A passing insect moves the air before it touches silk.

**Suggested summary:** Widows have specialized airflow hairs on their legs. Experiments on another spider explain how such hairs turn tiny air movements into neural signals, while the available brown-widow census supplies a cautious anatomical comparison. [parra2009; barth1999]

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: five female *L. geometricus*, morphological thesis; OTHER SPIDER: *Cupiennius salei*, physiological and airflow experiments. [parra2009; barth1999; klopsch2012]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ≈68 | trichobothria across walking legs | A brown widow's airflow hairs | *L. geometricus*; approximate eight-leg inventory, five-female thesis; not a genus census | [parra2009] |
| 200–800 | µm | Hairs tested physiologically | OTHER SPIDER *C. salei*; metatarsal hairs directly stimulated | [barth1999] |
| ≈0.1; occasionally 0.01 | degrees | Hair-deflection thresholds | OTHER SPIDER *C. salei*; neural response near 50–100 Hz under direct coupling | [barth1999] |
| 55 / 15 | cm | Platform changes detection | OTHER SPIDER *C. salei*; tethered-fly neural responses with / without underlying platform; not capture distances | [barth1999] |
| 38.4 ± 5.6 | mm | Detecting an approaching fly | OTHER SPIDER *C. salei*; reported fly distance in free-flight experiment, not a widow threshold | [klopsch2012] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- *L. geometricus* tibial trichobothria occur in two approximately parallel rows. **supported — LATRODECTUS, thesis** [parra2009]
- *L. geometricus* also had a subdistal metatarsal trichobothrium except on leg pair IV. **supported — LATRODECTUS, thesis** [parra2009]
- Trichobothrial anchorage is much more compliant than that of ordinary tactile hairs. **established — GENERAL SPIDER** [barth2004]
- Hair length changes mechanical frequency response through interaction with the surrounding boundary layer. **established — GENERAL SPIDER mechanics** [barth2004]
- Directly driven *C. salei* receptor cells had similar physiological tuning across tested hair lengths. **supported — OTHER SPIDER** [barth1999]
- *C. salei* trichobothrial cells responded to changing stimulation rather than firing spontaneously. **supported — OTHER SPIDER** [barth1999]
- An underlying platform strengthened *C. salei* responses by modifying airflow. **supported — OTHER SPIDER** [barth1999]
- Approaching blowflies generated relatively smooth front flow followed by a fluctuating wake. **supported — OTHER SPIDER experiment** [klopsch2012]
- *C. salei* prey-capture jumps coincided with the wake's arrival above its leg sensors. **supported — OTHER SPIDER** [klopsch2012]

### TERMS

- **Trichobothrium:** a fine, articulated hair responsive to airflow. [barth2004]
- **Boundary layer:** the region of reduced airflow close to a surface. [barth2004]
- **Phasic response:** neural activity concentrated on changes in stimulation. [barth1999]

### Claims to avoid

- No verified widow airflow-velocity threshold or hearing-distance record is supplied.
- Do not give widows *Cupiennius* hair counts, tuning or fly-capture behaviour. [barth1999]
- The thesis's approximate total and schematic row descriptions do not reconcile into an exact census; retain “approximately.” [parra2009]
- An airflow hair is not automatically a pressure-sensitive ear. [barth2004]

## 2. senses.slit-sensilla — Feeling Strain in the Skeleton

**Suggested kicker:** The skeleton is part of the sensor.

**Suggested summary:** Slit sensilla register deformation of the cuticle, including deformation caused by vibration. Arrays of slits distribute mechanical sensitivity; their best measured nanometre-scale performance comes from *Cupiennius*, not widows. [politi2021; schaber2012]

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: female *L. geometricus* anatomical mapping; OTHER SPIDER: *C. salei* slit mechanics and electrophysiology. [parra2009; schaber2012; barth1982]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 88 | lyriform organs across eight legs | A mapped widow inventory | *L. geometricus*; thesis abstract; organ count, not individual slits or neurons | [parra2009] |
| ≈8–200 / 1–2 | µm | Slit length / width | GENERAL/OTHER SPIDER morphology summarized in strain-detector section; not widow measurements | [politi2021] |
| 1.4–≈30 | nm | Compression at neural threshold | OTHER SPIDER *C. salei*, proprioceptive tibial HS-8; varies among slits | [schaber2012] |
| 0.01 | mN | Small stimulus force | OTHER SPIDER *C. salei*, HS-8 threshold-related force; not whole-web force | [schaber2012] |
| 10⁻⁷ | cm | Small high-frequency displacement | OTHER SPIDER *C. salei*, metatarsal organ at 1 kHz; equals 1 nm, not slit compression | [barth1982] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- *L. geometricus* lyriform organs were mapped on several leg segments. **supported — LATRODECTUS, thesis** [parra2009]
- A dorsal metatarsus–tarsus organ was identified in the brown-widow map. **supported — LATRODECTUS, thesis** [parra2009]
- Lyriform organs are arrays of slit sensilla. **established — GENERAL SPIDER** [barth2004]
- Slits compress most readily under loading perpendicular to their long axes. **established — OTHER SPIDER mechanics** [politi2021]
- Individual slits in an array can differ in mechanical sensitivity. **supported — OTHER SPIDER** [schaber2012]
- *C. salei* HS-8 loading curves were approximately linear. **supported — OTHER SPIDER** [schaber2012]
- *C. salei* vibration-sensitive HS-10 loading curves were exponential. **supported — OTHER SPIDER** [schaber2012]
- A cuticular pad beside HS-10 mechanically filters inputs and limits overcompression. **supported — OTHER SPIDER** [politi2021]
- *C. salei* metatarsal slit thresholds decreased steeply above roughly 40 Hz. **supported — OTHER SPIDER** [barth1982]
- Metatarsal organs also respond to slow tarsal deflection, supporting a proprioceptive role. **supported — OTHER SPIDER** [barth1982]

### TERMS

- **Slit sensillum:** an embedded cuticular strain sensor. [barth2004]
- **Lyriform organ:** a group of closely associated sensory slits. [schaber2012]
- **High-pass filter:** a system transmitting higher-frequency inputs more effectively than lower-frequency inputs. [barth1982]

### Claims to avoid

- “A slit is an open crack”: membranes cover the sensory structure. [politi2021]
- Do not equate tarsal displacement, slit compression and receptor-current threshold. [schaber2012; barth1982]
- No *Latrodectus* nanometre threshold was verified.
- Slits are not olfactory pores, and the tarsal organ is a different structure. [barth2004; anton1994]

## 3. senses.vibration — Reading the Web

**Suggested kicker:** Silk carries motion to the waiting spider.

**Suggested summary:** A widow's web is a mechanical transmission network. Recordings show effective courtship transmission alongside large local variation, so the web cannot be treated as a wire that preserves every frequency equally. [vibert2016; sivalinghem2021]

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: *L. hesperus*; OTHER SPIDER comparator: *Eratigena agrestis*, called *Tegenaria agrestis* in earlier work. [vibert2016; sivalinghem2021]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | male signal types | A measured repertoire | *L. hesperus*: abdominal tremulation, bounce and web pluck | [sivalinghem2021] |
| 16 | male–female interactions | Timing sample | *L. hesperus*; phase-duration sample in Results excerpt | [sivalinghem2021] |
| 40.21 | min | Mean distal phase | *L. hesperus*; placement on web to first female contact; N=16, study mean | [sivalinghem2021] |
| 99.16 | min | Mean proximal phase | Same sample; first female contact to first palp insertion; not a required courtship duration | [sivalinghem2021] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- *L. hesperus* webs transmit vibrations generated by male courtship movements. **established** [sivalinghem2021]
- Abdominal tremulation, bouncing and plucking use different production mechanisms. **supported — L. hesperus** [sivalinghem2021]
- Bounce and pluck signal power correlated with male size. **supported — L. hesperus** [sivalinghem2021]
- Transfer-function analysis found efficient transmission of male-generated signals. **supported — L. hesperus** [sivalinghem2021]
- Frequency-sweep experiments measured transmission at several locations on widow webs. **established — L. hesperus** [vibert2016]
- Transmission varied more within individual webs than among webs. **supported — L. hesperus and OTHER SPIDER comparator** [vibert2016]
- Longitudinal and transverse transmission efficiencies differed little in that comparison. **supported — L. hesperus and OTHER SPIDER comparator** [vibert2016]
- Inconsistent frequency transmission makes a frequency-only recognition code unlikely. **supported — interpretation, L. hesperus** [vibert2016]

### TERMS

- **Substrate-borne vibration:** mechanical motion carried through a structure, here silk. [vibert2016]
- **Transfer function:** the measured relation between an input movement and transmitted vibration. [sivalinghem2021]
- **Attenuation:** reduction in signal amplitude during transmission. [vibert2016]

### Claims to avoid

- Signal power correlated with size does not prove females use it to choose larger males. [sivalinghem2021]
- No universal widow localization accuracy, propagation speed or detection threshold was verified.
- Efficient courtship transmission and variable frequency transmission concern different experiments and inputs. [sivalinghem2021; vibert2016]
- Reserve prey-versus-mate decisions for `senses.prey-or-mate`; keep scaffold construction in the Silk & Web vault.

## 4. senses.vision — What Eight Eyes Are For

**Suggested kicker:** Limited detail does not mean no light.

**Suggested summary:** Widow eyes provide a route for light information even though prey capture is strongly guided by the web. Their anatomy, developmental comparisons and observed light responses support a careful account of vision; widow colour discrimination and acuity remain unquantified here. [jocque2006; forster1995; gainett2020]

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: *L. occidentalis* morphology, *L. hasselti* spiderling behaviour; OTHER THERIDIID: *Parasteatoda tepidariorum* eye development and LED responses; GENERAL SPIDER eye comparison. [valdezmondragon2023; forster1995; gainett2020; kamano2022; morehouse2017]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | eyes | Four named pairs | Theridiid anatomical plan; see widow eye illustrations in the species description | [jocque2006; valdezmondragon2023] |
| 0.25 | mm | Principal-eye diameter | *L. occidentalis* female holotype CNAN-T01589; AME, p. 35; one specimen, not genus mean | [valdezmondragon2023] |
| 0.20 / 0.22 / 0.23 | mm | Secondary-eye diameters | Same holotype; ALE / PME / PLE respectively | [valdezmondragon2023] |
| ≈48 | h after hatching | Eyes become recognizable | OTHER THERIDIID *P. tepidariorum*, 26°C; pigment, lenses and lateral tapeta develop before first-instar moult | [gainett2020] |
| Positive | phototaxis | Light can attract a cobweb spider | OTHER THERIDIID *P. tepidariorum*; LED attraction and ERG study, accessible primary abstract | [kamano2022] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Theridiid eyes are generally arranged in two rows. **established — GENERAL THERIDIID anatomy** [jocque2006]
- The anterior median pair comprises the principal eyes. **established — GENERAL SPIDER** [morehouse2017]
- ALE, PME and PLE are the three secondary-eye pairs. **established — GENERAL SPIDER** [morehouse2017]
- Principal and secondary eyes differ in retinal orientation. **established — GENERAL SPIDER** [morehouse2017]
- Principal eyes lack a tapetum; secondary eyes possess one in many spider groups. **established — GENERAL SPIDER** [morehouse2017]
- *P. tepidariorum* secondary eyes develop canoe-shaped tapeta with a central division. **established — OTHER THERIDIID** [gainett2020]
- *L. hasselti* young climb toward light before dispersal. **supported — LATRODECTUS** [forster1995]
- Positive phototaxis becomes less pronounced in later redback stages. **supported — LATRODECTUS** [forster1995]
- *P. tepidariorum* showed positive phototaxis to white-LED illumination in the reported assays. **supported — OTHER THERIDIID** [kamano2022]
- RNA interference against *sine oculis A* disrupted both principal and secondary eyes in house-spider offspring. **supported — OTHER THERIDIID** [gainett2020]

### TERMS

- **AME / ALE / PME / PLE:** anterior median / anterior lateral / posterior median / posterior lateral eyes. [valdezmondragon2023]
- **Tapetum:** a reflecting layer associated with light-sensitive cells; it gives light another passage through the receptors. **GENERAL SPIDER** [morehouse2017]
- **ERG:** electroretinogram, a summed electrical response to light, distinct from a behavioural colour-discrimination test. [kamano2022]
- **Phototaxis:** directed movement in relation to light. [forster1995]

### Claims to avoid

- No accessible *Latrodectus* spectral-sensitivity curve, acuity in degrees, absolute light threshold or colour-discrimination experiment was verified. Do not assign UV/green peaks, red blindness, monochromacy or salticid acuity to widows.
- The directly checked canoe-tapetum observations are house-spider evidence, not widow histology. [gainett2020]
- Neither eye diameter nor tapetum presence alone specifies visual sensitivity. [morehouse2017]
- A cobweb life makes reliance on vibration plausible; it does not demonstrate that every widow eye function was lost through disuse. See `senses.brain` for comparative neural investment.
- The Kamano study exists in Japanese (2022) and English translation (2023); these are not independent replications. Exact spectral values were not retained from inaccessible full text.

## 5. senses.contact-chemistry — Tasting a Thread

**Suggested kicker:** A chemical message begins at a hair's open tip.

**Suggested summary:** Male widows respond to chemicals on female silk even when the female is absent. Western-widow micrographs show plausible contact receptors on the appendages that touch silk; direct identification of the receptor cells for individual widow pheromones remains open. [ross1979; scott2015b]

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: male/female *L. hesperus*, male *L. hasselti*; OTHER SPIDER: *Argiope bruennichi* and *A. blanda*, receptor ultrastructure. [ross1979; stoltz2007; muller2020]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50–60 | µm | Presumed taste-hair length | *L. hesperus*, SEM, p. 74 and Fig. 2; function inferred anatomically | [ross1979] |
| ≈5 / 2 | µm | Base / tip diameter | Same presumed chemoreceptive hairs; tip diameter is not pore diameter | [ross1979] |
| 96 / 80 | % males courting | Silk / silk extract | *L. hesperus*, T-rod filter-paper assays; separate treatments | [scott2015b] |
| 40 / 39 | % longer contact | Silk / extract retention | Same respective treatments versus blank / solvent paper | [scott2015b] |
| 2–22 | chemoreceptive cells per sensillum | A comparative cellular range | OTHER SPIDER *Argiope* tarsal tip-pore sensilla; not a widow cell count | [muller2020] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- *L. hesperus* presumed chemoreceptive hairs have blunt, open tips and curved shafts. **supported — morphology, LATRODECTUS** [ross1979]
- These hairs occur on tarsi and ventral pedipalps in both widow sexes. **supported — LATRODECTUS** [ross1979]
- Male western widows courted on unoccupied female webs. **supported — LATRODECTUS** [ross1979]
- Western-widow silk extract induced courtship on filter paper. **supported — LATRODECTUS** [scott2015b]
- Synthetic MB-MP-S alone induced weaker responses than natural silk or extract. **supported — L. hesperus** [scott2015b]
- Male redbacks discriminated virgin-female web extracts from juvenile-female extracts. **supported — L. hasselti** [stoltz2007]
- Extracts from webs built immediately after redback mating elicited no more interest than solvent controls. **supported — L. hasselti** [stoltz2007]
- *Argiope* tip-pore sensilla house both chemoreceptive and mechanoreceptive cells. **established — OTHER SPIDER** [muller2020]
- Their long chemosensory dendrites extend toward the terminal pore. **established — OTHER SPIDER** [muller2020]
- A two-component synthetic blend subsequently elicited western-widow male courtship. **supported — L. hesperus** [fischer2025]

### TERMS

- **Contact chemoreception / gustation:** chemical detection through contact with a substrate. [stoltz2007; muller2020]
- **Tip-pore sensillum:** a sensory structure with a terminal opening. [muller2020]
- **MB-MP-S:** N-3-methylbutanoyl-O-methylpropanoyl-L-serine methyl ester, a western-widow contact-pheromone component. [scott2015b]

### Claims to avoid

- The widow SEM study called these receptors “presumed”; it did not record their response to a purified pheromone. [ross1979]
- Do not equate tasting silk with detecting the female by airborne scent.
- Chemical discrimination immediately after mating is not proof that every mated widow permanently stops signalling. [stoltz2007]
- “Spiders possess only tip-pore chemosensilla” is outdated as a generalization. [talukder2025]

## 6. senses.olfaction — Following a Scent Through Air

**Suggested kicker:** A web can advertise beyond its threads.

**Suggested summary:** Airborne chemicals guide male widow mate searching. Wall-pore hairs provide a verified receptor mechanism in another spider, while western-widow and redback studies supply direct behavioural and chemical evidence. [kasumovic2004; fischer2025; twidle2026; talukder2025]

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: *L. hesperus* field searching and *L. hasselti* chemical assays; OTHER SPIDER: *A. bruennichi* single-sensillum physiology and comparative family survey. [kasumovic2004; twidle2026; talukder2025]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 / 0 | male trap captures | Synthetic scent attracted widows | *L. hesperus*, isobutyric-acid / control traps, one field night; Fig. 4c | [fischer2025] |
| 840 | ng loaded per lure | Tested acid dose | Same experiment; bait loading, not emission rate or detection threshold | [fischer2025] |
| 4 | compounds in tested blend | A redback field lure | *L. hasselti*; three candidate volatiles plus a candidate short-range component | [twidle2026] |
| ≈7,000 | wall-pore sensilla per male | A comparative olfactory array | OTHER SPIDER *A. bruennichi*; all walking legs combined | [talukder2025] |
| 19 / 16 | species / families | Comparative sampling | OTHER SPIDER survey; morphology, not physiology in every taxon | [talukder2025] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Field male western widows were attracted to unoccupied female webs by airborne cues. **supported — L. hesperus** [kasumovic2004]
- These males distinguished conspecific webs from redback webs using airborne cues. **supported — L. hesperus** [kasumovic2004]
- Synthetic isobutyric acid attracted male western widows in a field trial. **supported — L. hesperus** [fischer2025]
- A redback blend containing three candidate volatiles and an acylated serine acid trapped males in New Zealand vineyards. **supported — L. hasselti** [twidle2026]
- The candidate redback volatiles were 2-methylpropanoic acid, (S)-2-methylbutanoic acid and 2-pyrrolidone. **supported — L. hasselti** [twidle2026]
- *A. bruennichi* wall-pore neurons responded to female pheromone in a concentration-dependent manner. **established — OTHER SPIDER** [talukder2025]
- Wall-pore sensilla were absent from the examined female and subadult male wasp spiders. **supported — OTHER SPIDER** [talukder2025]
- Mate-searching western widows can also exploit rivals' silk trails. **supported — L. hesperus, silk contact rather than airborne receptor identification** [scott2019]

### TERMS

- **Olfaction:** detection of airborne chemical information. [talukder2025]
- **Wall-pore sensillum:** a sensory hair with multiple shaft-wall openings providing chemical access. [talukder2025]
- **Headspace:** the air surrounding an emitting sample, collected for volatile analysis. [twidle2026]

### Claims to avoid

- No verified widow wall-pore count or single-sensillum pheromone threshold is supplied.
- The wasp-spider mechanism does not identify a widow's receptor, and absence in wasp-spider females does not mean female spiders cannot smell. [talukder2025]
- A trap dose is not a natural signalling dose; six male captures are not a detection-distance measurement. [fischer2025]
- Do not reduce all widow scent to one compound or treat all redback chemical components as individually field-validated attractants. [twidle2026]
- The 2026 redback results and older butyric-acid work require assay- and population-specific comparison; they do not justify one universal redback blend.

## 7. senses.tarsal-organ — A Pocket for Humidity and Warmth

**Suggested kicker:** A tiny capsule samples the surrounding air.

**Suggested summary:** The tarsal organ contains protected sensory endings. Direct recordings in *Cupiennius* demonstrate humidity and temperature sensitivity, but finding the corresponding structure in a widow does not transfer those performance values. [ehn1994; anton1994; parra2009]

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: female *L. geometricus* morphology only; OTHER SPIDER: *C. salei* tarsal-organ physiology and innervation. [parra2009; ehn1994; anton1994]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | tarsal organs across walking legs | Brown-widow map | *L. geometricus*; one per tarsus in the thesis; palps outside this inventory | [parra2009] |
| 7 | sensilla per capsule | Inside the organ | OTHER SPIDER *C. salei*; six sensilla have three cells, one has two | [anton1994] |
| 11 / 10 | percentage points RH | Humidity-step discrimination | OTHER SPIDER *C. salei* moist / dry cells; differences between successive upward / downward steps, respectively | [ehn1994] |
| 0.4 | °C | Warm-cell step resolution | OTHER SPIDER *C. salei*, average differential sensitivity; single-cell result | [ehn1994] |
| 3 | central target regions | Parallel sensory projections | OTHER SPIDER *C. salei*; tracer-filled afferents, anatomical count | [anton1994] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- A dorsal tarsal organ was mapped on each brown-widow walking leg. **supported — LATRODECTUS, thesis** [parra2009]
- The *C. salei* organ is an air-filled cuticular capsule with an external aperture. **established — OTHER SPIDER** [anton1994]
- Its internal sensilla have terminal pores. **established — OTHER SPIDER** [anton1994]
- Moist and dry cells responded antagonistically to sudden humidity changes. **supported — OTHER SPIDER** [ehn1994]
- Warm-sensitive cells occurred alongside the humidity-sensitive cells. **supported — OTHER SPIDER** [ehn1994]
- Both humidity-sensitive cell types were excited by some volatile polar chemicals. **supported — OTHER SPIDER** [ehn1994]
- Tarsal-organ afferents projected to appendage neuropil and other suboesophageal regions. **supported — OTHER SPIDER** [anton1994]
- Multiple central projections suggest parallel processing of environmental information. **supported — OTHER SPIDER interpretation** [anton1994]

### TERMS

- **Hygroreception:** humidity detection. [ehn1994]
- **Thermoreception:** temperature detection. [ehn1994]
- **Blumenthal organ:** the tarsal organ, distinct from the metatarsal lyriform strain-sensing organ. [parra2009; anton1994]

### Claims to avoid

- No widow-specific temperature or humidity threshold was verified.
- Responses to pungent vapours do not establish the organ as the widow's mate-pheromone receptor. [ehn1994]
- Step discrimination is not a preferred environmental humidity or a tolerance limit.
- The organ's name specifies its location, not one exclusive sensory modality. [anton1994; ehn1994]

## 8. senses.proprioception — Knowing Where the Legs Are

**Suggested kicker:** Feedback from every bend and load.

**Suggested summary:** Joint hairs, internal receptors and slit organs provide several forms of movement feedback. Comparative experiments also test gravity-related posture, so gravity belongs here rather than in an unsupported separate “widow balance organ” case. [seyfarth1985; seyfarth1990]

**Taxa studied:** OTHER SPIDER: *C. salei*, joint anatomy, hair-plate recordings and ablations; LATRODECTUS: *L. hesperus* behavioural navigation, without receptor identification. [seyfarth1985; seyfarth1990; sergi2021]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | leg joints examined | Distributed feedback | OTHER SPIDER *C. salei*; each joint supplied by several receptor types | [seyfarth1985] |
| 25–70 | hairs per plate | A coxal hair field | OTHER SPIDER *C. salei*; ventrolateral coxa near prosomal joint | [seyfarth1990] |
| 0.5–1 | degrees | Hair-plate response threshold | OTHER SPIDER *C. salei*; hair deflection, not whole-leg angle | [seyfarth1990] |
| 7/10 | tested animals | Height changed after ablation | OTHER SPIDER *C. salei*; unilateral hair-plate removal increased mean body height significantly | [seyfarth1990] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Each examined *C. salei* joint had internal receptors, slits and long cuticular hairs. **established — OTHER SPIDER** [seyfarth1985]
- Joint-membrane movement deflected coxal hair plates. **established — OTHER SPIDER** [seyfarth1985]
- Each coxal plate hair was supplied by one sensory cell. **established — OTHER SPIDER** [seyfarth1990]
- Only distalward hair deflection excited the recorded plate receptors. **supported — OTHER SPIDER** [seyfarth1990]
- Maintained deflection caused a slowly declining spike rate. **supported — OTHER SPIDER** [seyfarth1990]
- Plate afferents projected within the corresponding same-side neuromere. **established — OTHER SPIDER** [seyfarth1990]
- Hair-plate ablation changed body height in some animals without changing body-axis orientation to gravity. **supported — OTHER SPIDER** [seyfarth1990]
- Hair-plate physiology suggests graviception, but ablations gave limited behavioural effects. **debated — OTHER SPIDER mechanism** [seyfarth1990]
- Western-widow displacement experiments support internally updated navigation. **supported — LATRODECTUS, receptor pathway unresolved** [sergi2021]

### TERMS

- **Proprioception:** sensing the position, movement and loading of the animal's own body. [seyfarth1985]
- **Hair plate:** a localized group of hairs deflected by joint movement. [seyfarth1990]
- **Internal joint receptor:** sensory cells beneath the cuticle near an articulation. [seyfarth1985]
- **Graviception:** sensing gravity-related direction or loading. [seyfarth1990]

### Claims to avoid

- Hanging inverted is a posture, not identification of a gravity receptor.
- No widow statocyst, dedicated gravity organ or hair-plate ablation experiment was verified.

- A widow's path-integration behaviour does not identify which proprioceptor provides its input. [sergi2021]

## 9. senses.prey-or-mate — A Meal, a Mate, or a Rival?

**Suggested kicker:** The response depends on how the web moves.

**Suggested summary:** Low-amplitude early male signals were less likely to provoke predation in western-widow playback tests. Later courtship adds structured sequences, while redback competition experiments show that female responses also depend on accumulated courtship. [vibert2014; sivalinghem2021b; stoltz2010]

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: *L. hesperus* males/females with fly and cricket stimuli; *L. hasselti* females with competing males. [vibert2014; sivalinghem2021b; stoltz2010]

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 52.34 ± 25.28 / 31.82 ± 16.29 / 28.57 ± 17.41 | Hz | Male / cricket / fly frequency | *L. hesperus* webs, Table 1; mean ± SD, n=16/source; overlapping distributions | [vibert2014] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Male, cricket and fly vibrations overlapped in waveform. **supported — L. hesperus** [vibert2014]
- Low-amplitude playbacks seldom elicited predatory approaches regardless of male or prey origin. **supported — L. hesperus** [vibert2014]

### TERMS

- **RMS amplitude:** root-mean-square vibrational velocity in the cited recordings. [vibert2014]
- **Structured signalling:** combining distinct components in a stereotyped sequence. [sivalinghem2021b]

### Claims to avoid

- “A high frequency means male; a low frequency means prey.” [vibert2014; vibert2016]
- The classifier used by researchers is not a measurement of the female's classification accuracy. [vibert2014]
- No three-way widow neural code for prey, mate and rival was verified. Competition studies demonstrate responses, not a unique rival frequency. [stoltz2010]
- A courtship-duration threshold is not a universal minimum or proof of individual male recognition. [stoltz2010]

### Claims to avoid

- No verified widow temperature-gradient preference or humidity-choice optimum was found; omit such cards.

- The *Cupiennius* warm-cell result does not prove a 0.4°C widow behavioural discrimination. [ehn1994]
- The retained exhibit covers thermal behaviour and drying physiology; the proposed humidity-sensing mechanism is covered, comparatively, in `senses.tarsal-organ`.
