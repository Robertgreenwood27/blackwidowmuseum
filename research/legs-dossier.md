## Reading this dossier

- Bracketed citation keys refer to content/sources.yaml or the NEW entries in SOURCES below. The dossier follows research/digestion-dossier.md, with research/silk-glands-dossier.md as the second model. The current legs case, source registry, all requested neighbouring cases and all twelve digestion halls were consulted before drafting. Existing exhibit text is an editorial boundary, not independent confirmation of its claims.
- **established** = securely documented as qualified; **supported** = a particular experiment, limited sample or interpretation; **debated** = an explanation that remains unresolved. Confidence belongs to the qualified sentence, not automatically to the genus.
- Evidence classes are **LATRODECTUS**, **OTHER THERIDIID**, **OTHER SPIDER**, and **GENERAL SPIDER**. A textbook diagram is not a widow dissection. A primary account's observations, comparative inference and hypothesis are distinguished.
- FIGURES are candidate fact cards, including qualitative cards where measurements are unavailable. A missing measurement is not zero. Sample, species, sex, stage, measurement definition and conditions travel with each number. Cards marked HOLD are research leads, not launch copy.
- Roman numerals I–IV identify leg pairs from front to rear; left and right are the spider's. A leg formula ranks the four pairs by length; it is neither a walking sequence nor a segment sequence. Whole-leg length, summed segment length, patella–tibia length and posture-dependent leg span must remain separate.
- Use the seven external segments of the museum's arachnological convention: coxa, trochanter, femur, patella, tibia, metatarsus and tarsus. Historical authors also discuss a claw-bearing pretarsus and use basitarsus/telotarsus; translate their terminology before counting structures. [foelix2011; snodgrass1952]
- Historical identifications remain historical: Whitehead and Rempel's 1959 animals are source-identified L. mactans, not automatically modern southern-widow vouchers. Clarke's L. mactans hasselti is the redback, here styled L. hasselti. Vollrath's treatment of Randall under L. mactans does not change Randall's experimental species, L. variolus. [whitehead1959; clarke1986; randall1981; vollrath1990]
- This case owns limb construction, actuation, contact mechanics, use and repair. Circulatory anatomy stays in body.circulation; cuticle chemistry in body.exoskeleton; overall size in body.size; sensory transduction in senses.*; full silk and reproductive sequences in web.* and life.*.
- Every widow scene uses a three-dimensional tangle cobweb, with substrate-attached gumfoot lines when relevant, never an orb. A hanging female's hourglass is ventral and faces upward because she is inverted. Redbacks also have a distinct dorsal stripe; do not substitute it for the ventral marking. Diagrammatic colours must not imply observed muscle or hemolymph colours.

### Priority qualifications for the current exhibit

1. Preserve the distinction between basal autotomy and distal amputation: the former did not regenerate in Randall's northern-widow experiments, whereas sufficiently distal cuts could. [randall1981]
2. Keep “two main joints lack extensors”; do not turn it into “spider legs contain only flexor muscles.” The foot has antagonistic claw muscles. [foelix2011]
3. Label Parry and Brown's pressure measurements as house-spider experiments, never as measured widow blood pressure. [parry1959]
4. Reuse the existing leg-formula claim with its African-revision scope; a universal species-by-sex table was not verified. [lotz1994]
5. Add Clarke's redback anatomy as direct widow evidence, while keeping its small comparative study distinct from a genus-wide census. [clarke1986]
6. Replace “regrows at the next molt to full size” with the site-dependent outcome and the explicit performance gaps in Hall 9.
7. Treat long-legged male appearance, size-adjusted leg allometry and demonstrated search advantage as different propositions.
8. Do not describe an upside-down widow as hanging on suction pads, a proved passive locking mechanism or a measured zero-energy grip.

## 0. body.legs — Seven Segments

**Suggested kicker:** Jointed limbs that walk, work and listen.

**Suggested summary:** A widow's eight walking legs combine muscles, fluid pressure and thread-gripping feet. Eleven halls follow their joints and movements, their work on silk and in courtship, and the sharply different outcomes of losing a leg at its base or farther along it.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: source-identified L. mactans, L. hasselti, L. variolus, L. hesperus, L. geometricus and L. elegans; African taxa in Lotz's revision. Named comparative spiders appear only in their relevant halls.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | external segments per walking leg | Body to foot | Arachnological convention; claw-bearing pretarsus not an eighth labelled external segment | [foelix2011] |
| 8 | walking legs | Four paired limbs | Spider body plan; palps and chelicerae excluded | [foelix2011] |
| Coxa–trochanter | detachment site | A basal loss is different | L. variolus, experimentally induced autotomy | [randall1981] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Lotz ranks the legs of the African widows in his revision I, IV, II, III from longest to shortest. **established — LATRODECTUS, revision-level description** [lotz1994]
- Redbacks have been directly described climbing a scaffolding of single silk threads gripped at the leg tips. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hasselti** [clarke1986]
- Brown- and southern-widow leg proportions followed different developmental patterns in a recent comparative study. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. geometricus and L. mactans** [barrantes2026]

These cards and observations orient the parent; the detailed samples and qualifications belong in the halls.

### TERMS

- **Podomere:** a segment of an arthropod limb.
- **Articulation:** contact between skeletal structures within a joint.
- **Flexion / extension:** reducing / increasing the angle at a specified joint.
- **Autotomy:** self-detachment of an appendage; distinguish it from an experimental cut.
- **Regeneration:** production of missing tissue, distinct from healing a wound or changing posture.

### Claims to avoid

- “Eight identical tools”: pair, sex, stage and task matter.
- “Two knees” is an analogy; identify femur–patella and tibia–metatarsus explicitly.
- Do not imply that any injured adult widow will replace a leg.
- The proposed halls are research scopes, not eleven new claims of equal evidential completeness.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- An inverted, species-identified female in a tangle with a leg I enlarged into seven labelled external segments; keep the ventral hourglass upward and palps visually separate from the eight legs.
- A three-part locator plate: joint cutaway, foot on one thread, and distal versus basal injury diagrams; use the same anatomical orientation throughout.

## 1. body.legs.segments-and-joints — Seven Pieces, Many Axes

**Suggested kicker:** A joint is more than a bend.

**Suggested summary:** The familiar segment names describe a limb whose joints have different axes and constraints. A measured specimen anchors leg length, while redback dissections show why one generic ball-and-socket drawing cannot represent every connection.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: L. hasselti in Clarke's comparative dissections; African widows in Lotz; two collected L. elegans females from Manipur, with the measurement table belonging to voucher WILD-11-ARA-1113. OTHER SPIDER: Heteropoda venatoria for the explicitly comparative axis explanation.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| 21.47 | mm, summed segments | First leg of one specimen | L. elegans female WILD-11-ARA-1113, Manipur; femur through tarsus, coxa and trochanter excluded | [kananbala2012] |
| 19.18 / 14.52 | mm, summed segments | Fourth / second leg | Same voucher and measurement convention; individual values, not population means | [kananbala2012] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- The external sequence is coxa, trochanter, femur, patella, tibia, metatarsus and tarsus. **established — GENERAL SPIDER** [foelix2011]
- In Heteropoda, coxa–body and coxa–trochanter movements contribute to forward and backward leg placement. **established — OTHER SPIDER, H. venatoria, functional anatomy** [clarke1984]
- In that comparator, trochanter–femur, femur–patella and tibia–metatarsus movements alter the body-to-foot distance. **established — OTHER SPIDER, H. venatoria** [clarke1984]
- Its femur–patella and tibia–metatarsus joints have paired articulations along a dorsal hinge line. **established — OTHER SPIDER, H. venatoria** [clarke1984]
- Redback patella–tibia articulation differs between anterior and posterior leg pairs in Clarke's comparative account. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hasselti** [clarke1986]
- The described L. elegans specimen has leg I longer than IV, then II and III. **established — LATRODECTUS, single voucher** [kananbala2012]

### TERMS

- **Coxa:** basal segment adjoining the body.
- **Trochanter:** short segment between coxa and femur.
- **Patella:** short segment between femur and tibia.
- **Metatarsus / basitarsus:** segment immediately proximal to the tarsus in the convention used here.
- **Bicondylic / monocondylic:** having two / one principal articulating contacts; not a complete specification of every permitted motion.
- **Arthrodial membrane:** flexible cuticle between harder limb pieces.
- **Prolateral / retrolateral:** sides facing toward the front / rear of the spider in the standard extended-leg reference position.

### Claims to avoid

- Do not call the coxa–trochanter joint a freely rotating spherical joint or assign every joint one identical axis.
- Do not import Heteropoda's rotational range at the metatarsus–tarsus joint into a widow.
- The L. elegans table's published leg III total, 9.97 mm, conflicts with its listed segment sum, 11.39 mm; HOLD that total pending correction rather than silently repairing the source.
- No genus-wide minimum/maximum leg length, walking joint-angle envelope or universal male/female leg formula was verified.
- A printed total of femur through tarsus is not all seven segments and is not a leg span.
- Signpost body.body-plan for appendage placement, body.exoskeleton for membrane material, and body.pedipalps for the differently segmented palps.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- A seven-segment museum plate with pair numbers on the whole animal and a separate axis inset labelled Heteropoda comparator; use arrows rather than invented degree values.
- A same-scale tracing of legs I, II and IV of the cited L. elegans voucher based on the reported lengths; label “one preserved specimen, femur to tarsus,” not “typical widow.”

## 2. body.legs.muscles — The Muscles Inside

**Suggested kicker:** Pulling from inside a rigid tube.

**Suggested summary:** Widow musculature has a substantial historical anatomical literature. The secure picture combines a whole-animal muscle survey with newer redback comparisons, while the detailed joint-action map keeps its comparative sources visible.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: adult females and males identified as L. mactans by Whitehead and Rempel; L. hasselti in Clarke. OTHER SPIDER: source-named Eurypelma hentzi in Snodgrass's plates. Do not retroactively assign the 1959 specimens to modern species without voucher evidence.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 537 / 533 | individual muscles | Female / male inventory | Whitehead and Rempel's adult, whole-animal serial-section/dissection account; NOT muscles per leg | [whitehead1959] |
| 3 / 2 | patella–tibia muscles | Front / rear leg pairs | Clarke's comparative description includes redback; anatomical subdivisions, not force measurements | [clarke1986] |
| Flexor + pressure | opposing actions | The two major hinge joints | General spider framework; not absence of musculature throughout the limb | [parry1959; foelix2011] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Whitehead and Rempel examined widow musculature by serial sections and dissection. **established — LATRODECTUS, historical L. mactans** [whitehead1959]
- Their only substantial sex difference was absence of muscles in the male palpal pretarsus. **supported — LATRODECTUS, historical L. mactans, author's anatomical comparison** [whitehead1959]
- In the tarantula plate, extrinsic muscles run from carapace and endosternum to the leg base. **established — OTHER SPIDER, Eurypelma hentzi** [snodgrass1952]
- Coxal muscles operate the trochanter, and levator and depressor groups move the femur in that preparation. **established — OTHER SPIDER, E. hentzi** [snodgrass1952]
- Its femur–patella and tibia–metatarsus joints have depressor musculature without opposing joint extensors. **established — OTHER SPIDER, E. hentzi** [snodgrass1952]
- Its patellar muscles move the tibia transversely, while the metatarsus–tarsus connection lacks a dedicated intersegmental muscle. **established — OTHER SPIDER, E. hentzi** [snodgrass1952]
- Redbacks have a smaller, simpler trochanter–patella complex than the grasping hunters in Clarke's comparison. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hasselti, comparative dissection** [clarke1986]
- Clarke described a particularly enlarged posterior tibia–metatarsus muscle in the redback. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hasselti** [clarke1986]
- Spider claw-bearing pretarsi are raised and lowered through antagonistic muscles and tendons. **established — GENERAL SPIDER** [foelix2011]

### TERMS

- **Intrinsic / extrinsic muscle:** contained within the appendage / arising outside it and acting on its base.
- **Origin / insertion:** anatomical attachment sites used to identify a muscle; not sufficient alone to measure its action.
- **Apodeme:** inward cuticular support for muscle attachment.
- **Tendon:** a force-transmitting connection; distinguish its cuticular portion from living muscle.
- **Levator / depressor:** raises / lowers a specified part in the stated coordinate system.
- **Muscle complex:** adjacent or fused muscle subdivisions whose counting may differ among authors.

### Claims to avoid

- The 537/533 inventory is not a replicated distribution, a fibre count or a modern muscle atlas for every Latrodectus.
- Do not divide 537 by eight to obtain a “muscles per leg” card.
- The four-muscle sex difference concerns pedipalps; it does not show that males lack walking-leg muscles.
- Whitehead and Rempel's complete origin/insertion plates were not retrievable in this pass; do not invent their numbered leg-muscle list. Clarke and the labelled tarantula map supply the accessible detail.
- Muscle size is not measured force, power, fibre type or stamina. The comparative association with capture mode is not proof of adaptive causation.
- Signpost body.pedipalps for palpal function and body.digestion.mouth-and-pump for foregut muscles.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- A transparent leg showing internal muscle bundles and tendons, with hydraulic joints highlighted; label any detailed tarantula-derived architecture as comparative and check redback variants against Clarke's plates.
- A joint-action plate using compact “muscle flexion / pressure extension / claw tendons” symbols; keep whole-animal muscle totals outside the leg diagram.

## 3. body.legs.hydraulics — Straightened by Pressure

**Suggested kicker:** Fluid pressure supplies the opposing push.

**Suggested summary:** Two major joints extend when pressure acts on their flexible membranes. The classic measurements explain the mechanism, but they belong to house spiders; widow pressure curves and a complete allocation of muscular versus hydraulic work remain unmeasured here.

**Taxa studied:** OTHER SPIDER: Tegenaria atrica, source identification, in Parry and Brown; wolf-spider cadavers in Yap et al. GENERAL SPIDER: hydraulic extension framework. No direct widow pressure assay was verified.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| About 5 | cm Hg | Maintained leg pressure | T. atrica, restrained experimental preparations; abstract's approximate value, not a universal resting pressure | [parry1959] |
| Up to 40 | cm Hg | Transient pressure | Same paper's abstract; stimulated/struggling spiders, not normal widow walking | [parry1959] |
| ≈6.7 / ≈53 | kPa | The same two pressures | Unit conversions from 5/40 cm Hg; rounded, not additional measurements | [parry1959] |
| Not verified | kPa | Widow pressure | No Latrodectus measurement adopted | — |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Parry and Brown measured pressure inside house-spider legs and related it to extension torque. **established — OTHER SPIDER, T. atrica** [parry1959]
- The transient pressures were sufficient to explain the torques produced while accelerating an attached mass. **supported — OTHER SPIDER, T. atrica, experimental mechanical comparison** [parry1959]
- Spider prosomal compression can increase pressure in the legs. **established — GENERAL SPIDER, anatomical framework** [foelix2011]
- In the cadaver experiment, externally applied pressure extended the legs and pressure release allowed them to close. **established — OTHER SPIDER, wolf-spider preparations, pneumatic manipulation after death** [yap2022]
- Loss of hydraulic extension provides a mechanical explanation for the inward leg curl after death. **supported — GENERAL SPIDER, consistent with the cadaver manipulation rather than a widow mortality assay** [yap2022]

### TERMS

- **Hemolymph:** circulating body fluid in the spider's open circulatory system.
- **Hemocoel:** communicating internal spaces containing hemolymph.
- **Pressure:** force per unit area, distinct from volume flow.
- **Torque:** turning effect about a joint axis.
- **Antagonism:** opposing actions, here including a muscle acting against fluid pressure.
- **Gauge pressure:** pressure relative to the surrounding atmosphere; preserve the experimental reference when available.

### Claims to avoid

- “The heart straightens each leg” confuses circulation with the active pressure-generating mechanism; signpost body.circulation for heart and vessels.
- Do not equate a cadaver actuated with air to a living widow's normal hydraulic system.
- Do not say a dead spider actively contracts all flexors forever or that every dead spider must exhibit the same pose.
- No widow pressure threshold for movement, individual-joint valve map, stroke volume, pressure at autotomy or share of total running power was verified.
- The older pressure-source discussion and later models are not a settled identification of one universal pump muscle.
- Temperature-dependent sprint kinematics in Aphonopelma hentzi belong to a tarantula comparator, not a widow thermal-performance curve. [booster2015]

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- A conceptual flexed/extended hydraulic joint with a ventral membrane and dorsal pivot; use separate arrows for muscle pull and internal pressure, not a piston installed inside the leg.
- A labelled house-spider measurement inset beside a widow schematic, with the 5/40 cm Hg figures confined to the former; a small curled-cadaver silhouette may illustrate loss of extension without depicting injury as a visitor procedure.

## 4. body.legs.feet-and-grip — A Foot Around a Thread

**Suggested kicker:** Holding silk is a contact problem.

**Suggested summary:** A three-clawed foot grips a thread by a mechanism different from a hunting spider's adhesive pads. This hall combines feet and inverted support because anatomy is well supported, while widow hanging endurance, grip force and smooth-surface performance remain separate research gaps.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: L. hasselti for single-thread support and restricted distal-joint anatomy. GENERAL SPIDER: three-clawed web-spider foot mechanics. OTHER SPIDER: named hunters only where adhesive structures are compared. Theridiid family morphology is a framework applicable to widows, not a survey of all their feet.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | tarsal claws | Paired claws and a median hook | Theridiid family diagnosis; do not count accessory bristles as extra claws | [jocque2006] |
| Hook + opposing bristles | contact arrangement | Gripping one thread | GENERAL SPIDER web-foot mechanism; not a widow force measurement | [foelix2011] |
| Not verified | hours / mN | Hanging endurance / force | No time-to-fatigue or force-to-detachment experiment on a widow adopted | — |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Theridiid walking feet bear three tarsal claws. **established — GENERAL SPIDER, family diagnosis encompassing Latrodectus** [jocque2006]
- In the described web-spider mechanism, the median hook pushes silk against opposing serrated bristles. **established — GENERAL SPIDER** [foelix2011]
- Lifting that hook releases the thread from the bristles. **established — GENERAL SPIDER** [foelix2011]
- Redbacks grip individual scaffold threads with the claws at their leg tips. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hasselti** [clarke1986]
- Clarke associated their restricted metatarsus–tarsus mobility with support on single threads. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hasselti, functional interpretation** [clarke1986]
- Scopulae and claw tufts are adhesive hair fields anatomically distinct from a thread clasp. **established — GENERAL SPIDER, comparative anatomy** [wolff2013]

### TERMS

- **Pretarsus:** terminal claw-bearing apparatus; not an additional long external segment in the exhibit's seven-segment convention.
- **Pectinate claw:** a claw with comb-like teeth.
- **Accessory / auxiliary bristle:** a bristle opposing or assisting the claws, not necessarily a separate true claw.
- **Scopula:** a field of specialized hairs; its precise location and adhesive morphology must be specified.
- **Claw tuft:** an adhesive hair pad immediately beneath the claw region.
- **Inverted support:** support with the venter uppermost; posture alone does not reveal energy expenditure.

### Claims to avoid

- Do not conflate three structures: teeth on the paired claws, bristles opposing the median hook, and the tarsal IV wrapping comb. Signpost body.comb-foot for the last.
- Avoid “all three claws clamp the same thread” or “each claw has its own muscle.”
- No widow-specific count of claw teeth or opposing bristles, thread-diameter matching curve, friction coefficient or maximal attachment load was verified.
- The expected absence of hunting-spider scopulae/claw tufts must not become an invented Latrodectus SEM result: this pass did not verify a species-by-sex negative inventory. Commission source-checked feet without importing hunter pads; withhold a universal absence card.
- No controlled widow smooth-glass climbing assay was located. Clarke's explicit glass difficulties refer to Araneus females, not a quantified redback result; pet-keeping anecdotes do not establish incapacity.
- Do not infer suction cups, glue secreted by widow feet, a passive tendon lock, “zero muscular effort” or an hours-long endurance value from an inverted photograph.
- Signpost senses.proprioception for posture feedback and web.navigation for route finding.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- A taxon-labelled three-clawed web-spider mechanism showing one thread between median hook and opposing bristles, alongside a separately labelled hunter claw-tuft inset; no scopula pasted onto the widow.
- An inverted redback supported on several irregular scaffold threads, plus a modest distal-joint inset; omit clocks and force multipliers and keep the ventral hourglass facing upward.

## 5. body.legs.walking-and-running — Across Silk and Ground

**Suggested kicker:** A route changes what a long leg can do.

**Suggested summary:** Males move through vegetation, over ground and along silk while searching for females. Field travel rates and laboratory running or climbing tests answer different questions; a comparative gait diagram can explain coordination without pretending that a widow gait atlas exists.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: adult male L. hesperus in field releases and laboratory climbing; male L. pallidus in field tracking. OTHER SPIDER: juvenile Davus pentaloris in gait experiments. No widow-specific stride-phase map was verified.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| >1.25 / <0.25 | m/min | Contrasting field travel rates | Maximum average rates reported for recaptured L. hesperus males in 2016 / 2017 release experiments; contrasting wind, distance divided by elapsed search time, NOT sprint maxima | [scott2019] |
| >200 | m | Some interweb movements | L. hesperus, 2016 tracking; most recaptured movements were within 60 m, not continuous measured paths | [scott2019] |
| Two alternating sets of four | model gait | A coordination reference | OTHER SPIDER: Davus tarantula study; real footfall timing varied around this model | [kane2025] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Western-widow males were observed travelling faster along silk than across ground or vegetation. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus, field observations** [scott2019]
- Laboratory vertical climbing speed did not correlate with the size measures tested in adult male western widows. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [brandt2007]
- Horizontal speed increased with size in the same experiment. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [brandt2007]
- Male white-widow leg length did not significantly correlate with total distance walked in the field study. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. pallidus, observational correlation** [segoli2006]
- The tarantula gait experiment found coordination patterns that departed from perfectly synchronized tetrapods. **supported — OTHER SPIDER, D. pentaloris** [kane2025]

### TERMS

- **Stance / swing:** portions of a step with / without foot contact.
- **Duty factor:** fraction of a stride spent in stance.
- **Tetrapod gait:** coordination described in terms of two sets of four legs, not four-legged anatomy.
- **Stride length / frequency:** distance per cycle / cycles per unit time.
- **Search rate:** displacement per elapsed time including pauses and route choice; distinguish from short-burst running speed.
- **Allometry:** relationship between a trait's size and an overall size measure.

### Claims to avoid

- Do not convert the field averages into a widow's maximum running speed or a web-versus-ground speed ratio.
- Neither correlation with size nor a null result isolates leg length as the causal trait; no controlled widow leg-elongation experiment was verified.
- Do not use Argiope mate-search selection as a widow result or generalize “longer legs always find more mates.”
- The gravity hypothesis is debated across spiders; one within-sex western-widow test neither proves it nor resolves the origin of every species' size dimorphism.
- No widow stride length, stride frequency, duty factor, turning radius or standardized ground/web sprint comparison is adopted.
- Signpost life.mate-search for pheromones, mortality and competitive context; web.male-silk for the male's silk; web.navigation for homing; life.ballooning for silk-borne spiderling dispersal, not powered flight by legs.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- A male western widow moving between rough ground, vegetation and a silk bridge; visually separate a field displacement arrow from an inset illustrating a short laboratory speed trial.
- A clearly labelled Davus comparator footfall diagram with alternating tetrapods and variability, beside an unnumbered widow on irregular silk; do not animate an asserted universal widow gait.

## 6. body.legs.tools — Eight Working Limbs

**Suggested kicker:** Pull, hold, reel and clean.

**Suggested summary:** Legs manipulate silk as well as carry weight. Brown-widow construction observations identify several pair-specific jobs, while redback observations document prey transport, probing and cleaning; the complete web and feeding sequences remain in their neighbouring exhibits.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: L. geometricus from Costa Rica, direct observations plus 40 minutes of construction video from three spiders; L. hasselti natural-history observations; female L. mactans and L. hesperus in the existing comb study. OTHER THERIDIID: Achaearanea digitus, source identification, for a separately labelled grooming ethogram.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I / II–III / IV | leg-pair roles | Reel / manage loose silk / hold dragline | Observed L. geometricus gumfoot-building sequence; roles depend on phase, not fixed exclusive occupations | [eberhard2008] |
| 35 | % of wrapping strokes | Both hind legs together | L. geometricus, video counts of 329 strokes in four prey encounters; strokes are not independent spiders | [barrantes2007] |
| 13–19 / 13–22 | comb bristles | Southern / western female combs | Existing neighbouring case: 30 adult females per species, both sides counted; details stay in body.comb-foot | [hanp2000] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Brown widows held a descending dragline with one leg IV before anchoring it to the floor. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. geometricus** [eberhard2008]
- During ascent, legs I reeled silk that accumulated on legs II and was transferred toward legs III. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. geometricus** [eberhard2008]
- A hind leg extended shortly before the spinnerets made the substrate attachment. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. geometricus, observed movement; tension effect unmeasured** [eberhard2008]
- Brown widows used both alternating and simultaneous fourth-leg wrapping strokes. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. geometricus** [barrantes2007]
- Redbacks probed prey with a foreleg before turning to apply silk. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hasselti, natural-history observation** [forster1995]
- Wrapped redback prey was supported by silk and held between the hind legs during transport. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hasselti** [forster1995]
- Juvenile redbacks escaping sticky silk wiped their legs through the mouthparts before using them to clear the abdomen. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hasselti, observer's account** [forster1995]
- The comparator's grooming repertoire included passing tarsi between the chelicerae. **supported — OTHER THERIDIID, A. digitus, ethogram** [japyassu2008]

### TERMS

- **Reeling:** gathering an existing thread rather than simply producing more silk.
- **Dragline:** trailing silk connection; a fourth leg can manipulate it without making the silk itself.
- **Gumfoot:** capture line with an adhesive portion attached to the substrate below the web.
- **Swathing / wrapping:** applying silk around a target; distinguish attack wrapping from later packaging.
- **Grooming:** cleaning actions; mouthpart contact alone does not demonstrate eating the appendage or recovering nutrients.

### Claims to avoid

- Do not call the forelegs sticky-spiral measuring legs or draw the brown widow constructing an orb.
- Do not infer a leg IV motor pulls every fibre in every context, or that left and right hind legs always alternate.
- Bringing leg II to the mouth during construction was interpreted as breaking a held line; do not relabel that frame as verified grooming. [eberhard2008]
- Probing prey is observed; determining its identity, venom risk or palatability by that action remains interpretation.
- Do not generalize the redback's transport grip into continuous leg grasping during all feeding or into the capture method of a wolf spider.
- Keep comb microstructure in body.comb-foot, architecture in web.construction, attack sequences in web.wrapping, fluid processing in body.digestion.extra-oral and meal duration in body.digestion.meal-clock.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- A source-based brown-widow construction sequence using Fig. 11 of Eberhard et al.: dragline held by IV, I reeling, II–III managing fluff, spinnerets making attachments; irregular tangle above gumfoot lines.
- A juvenile redback clearing silk from one leg at the mouthparts, with a separate wrapped-prey transport inset; no oral silk gland or secretion emerging from the foot.

## 7. body.legs.courtship-and-contest — Signals, Touch and Leverage

**Suggested summary:** Courting males use legs to pluck threads, initiate body bounces and make direct contact. Redback observations also show leg bracing during withdrawal after the somersault; the hall explains limb actions without retelling the whole mating system.

- **Tactile courtship:** direct contact between animals.
- **Somersault:** copulatory repositioning placing the redback male's abdomen near the female's mouthparts.

### Claims to avoid

- Bracing to withdraw is not proof that all three/four-legged males succeed, nor that fewer legs make withdrawal impossible.
- The redback somersault is a species-specific context, not the mating posture of all widows.
- Do not portray ritual leg wrestling as an inevitable duel; the accessible competition result emphasizes limited fighting and continued courtship.

## 8. body.legs.leg-loss — The Break at the Base

**Suggested kicker:** Shedding and cutting are different injuries.

**Suggested summary:** Northern widows can detach a leg at its basal breaking plane. The immediate mechanical benefit and the longer-term cost must be separated: direct widow field frequencies and controlled prey-capture or mating costs remain gaps, while comparative studies show that costs need not appear in every assay.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: L. variolus in Randall's ligation/amputation experiment; adult L. hesperus in the existing defensive-stimulation study. OTHER SPIDER: Zygiella x-notata, adult females in a field survey and laboratory tests.

| Not verified | % of wild widows | A missing prevalence estimate | No denominator-based Latrodectus field leg-loss survey adopted | — |
| 9.5–13 | % of adult females | A comparator's field frequency | Z. x-notata, NOT widows; original abstract does not supply full survey denominators/locality—HOLD as numerical display | [pasquet2011] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Ligation at or proximal to mid-tibia elicited northern-widow autotomy at the coxa–trochanter joint. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. variolus** [randall1981]
- More proximal ligation elicited autotomy more frequently. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. variolus, experiment's qualitative gradient** [randall1981]
- The western-widow defensive pinching experiment reported no leg autotomy in its second experiment. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus, treatment-specific negative observation** [nelsen2014]

### TERMS

- **Breakage plane:** a structurally localized site of detachment.
- **Amputation:** experimental or traumatic removal by cutting; not necessarily self-detachment.
- **Ligation:** constriction by a tie used as an experimental treatment.
- **Autospasy / autotilly:** historical terms for externally resisted separation / removal using the animal's mouthparts; definitions vary, so report the original procedure. [randall1982]
- **Prevalence:** proportion missing a leg when surveyed, not the rate at which new losses occur.
- **Compensation:** altered use of remaining structures; not evidence of regrowth.

### Claims to avoid

- Do not call the basal plane a femur break or draw the coxa detached with an autotomized leg.
- A leg-deficient photograph cannot identify predator escape, a failed molt, courtship damage or the frequency of the event.
- The cited western-widow non-result does not contradict induced autotomy in another species or establish that pinching never elicits it.
- No widow estimates for leg-loss frequency by pair/sex, survival benefit during predator seizure, capture cost, altered gumfoot production or mating/paternity penalty were verified.
- No statistical difference in a comparator assay is not proof of no cost in nature; avoid the generic “spare legs” claim.
- Spider appendotomy can restrict fluid loss, but a source-checked widow-specific tissue/valve map was not retrieved; do not invent one.
- Failed extraction at molt belongs in life.molting; absence of a leg after maturity belongs here without a promise of replacement.

- An evidence comparison with an unnumbered widow silhouette and a separately named Zygiella result panel; any orb appears only in the comparator panel, never around the widow.

## 9. body.legs.regeneration — A Conditional Return

**Suggested kicker:** The location of loss changes the outcome.

**Suggested summary:** Randall's result is unusually instructive: northern widows could replace sufficiently distal losses but did not replace autotomized legs. Later coxal-removal experiments reveal another response—relocation of a neighbouring leg base—while size and functional recovery of a regenerated widow leg remain open measurement tasks.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: L. variolus, Randall 1981; fourth-instar females in the 1982 coxal-removal study. OTHER SPIDER: Araneus, Zygiella and source-named Nephila in Vollrath's regeneration experiments; juvenile Davus pentaloris for modern recovery kinematics. These are separate datasets.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mid-femur | most proximal successful cut level | A positional limit | L. variolus, Randall's amputation experiment; no universal percentage success implied | [randall1981] |
| 5 of 20 | surgical subjects surviving to later instars | Whole coxa removal | Fourth-instar L. variolus; leg I removed at body–coxa connection, 27 °C, 80% RH, flightless Drosophila diet; NOT basal-autotomy survival | [randall1982] |
| ≤1 | day | A comparator's locomotor recovery | D. pentaloris after induced loss of two legs; running performance, not widow tissue regrowth | [kane2025] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Northern-widow cuts proximal to mid-femur did not produce leg regeneration in Randall's experiment. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. variolus** [randall1981]
- Autotomized legs did not regenerate in that experiment. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. variolus** [randall1981]
- Randall did not observe autotomy following his amputations. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. variolus, procedure-specific result** [randall1981]
- Removing a whole coxa left a healed scar without a replacement limb in the surviving northern widows. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. variolus, separate 1982 operation** [randall1982]
- After later molts, an adjacent coxa moved into the vacated area in those survivors. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. variolus** [randall1982]
- Comparable coxal relocation was not observed after autotomy that retained the missing leg's coxa. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. variolus, n = 53 in the comparison** [randall1982]
- Regenerated limbs emerged at molts and differed in initial completeness among Vollrath's comparator spiders. **supported — OTHER SPIDER, named web-building taxa** [vollrath1990]
- Tarantulas in the modern experiment resumed their prior running performance after leg regeneration. **supported — OTHER SPIDER, D. pentaloris** [kane2025]

### TERMS

- **Proximal / distal:** nearer the body / nearer the foot along the limb.
- **Regenerate:** newly produced replacement tissue or appendage.
- **Intermolt:** interval between molts.
- **Developmental gradient:** the positional regeneration framework tested by Randall; not a demonstrated molecular pathway.
- **Coxal relocation:** movement of an existing leg base, distinct from formation of a missing leg.
- **Functional recovery:** recovery in a specified assay, not merely visible presence of a limb.

### Claims to avoid

- Do not label 25% surgical survival a regeneration probability, autotomy survival or wild leg-loss frequency.
- The 1982 printed early-mortality statement is internally difficult to reconcile with later survival; use the explicit 5/20 endpoint and do not construct a daily survival curve.
- No verified Randall 1981 sample-by-cut-level table, molt-latency distribution, relative regenerate length, sensory complement, comb restoration or locomotor/capture-performance assay was accessible; these cannot be supplied from the abstract.
- Do not borrow the tarantula's regeneration after basal autotomy as the widow outcome, or Nephila's initially clawless stumps as a measured widow phenotype.
- No universal “half-size first, full-size second molt,” number of molts to normality or maximum regenerative age is justified for Latrodectus here.
- Adults cannot replace a visible cuticular limb without a further molt; this is not equivalent to “no adult female widow ever molts.” Post-maturity molts have been reported in L. tredecimguttatus, but regeneration after them was not tested in that report. [nadolny2019]
- Signpost life.molting for ecdysis, and Hall 8 for the injury event.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- A northern-widow decision plate contrasting basal autotomy, a cut proximal to mid-femur, and a sufficiently distal cut; show the documented outcome category and an unscaled post-molt regenerate, without invented recovery dates or dimensions.
- A source-based ventral coxal-relocation series after complete coxa I removal; label the much more severe surgery, distinguish it from ordinary autotomy, and show coxa II moving rather than a ninth leg appearing.

**Suggested kicker:** Maturity changes the comparison.

### OBSERVATIONS

### TERMS

- **Instar:** developmental stage between molts; authors differ in numbering hatchlings and pre-emergence stages.
- **Isometry / allometry:** proportional scaling / size-dependent change in proportions, under a defined measurement relationship.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** difference between sexes; state whether the comparison is mass, body length or particular leg segments.
- **Quadratic trajectory:** a fitted curved relationship; not proof that growth proceeds smoothly through rigid cuticle between molts.
- **Gompertz trajectory:** a growth model with changing rate; used here only for the named comparator.
- **Structural size proxy:** a rigid-body measure used to estimate size; not literally the whole animal's length.

### Claims to avoid

- Do not shorten “front patella–tibia” to “whole leg” when using the 2024 ratios, even though the discussion uses informal leg-length language.
- A male/female ratio of 3/4 is not a male leg/body ratio, nor evidence that males have longer absolute legs.
- No table of segment lengths at every widow instar, universal post-molt percentage increment, or sex-specific allometric coefficient was verified.
- Do not infer developmental canalization as established causation from similar fitted accelerations; the 2026 authors propose that explanation.
- Food and abdomen distension can change mass and apparent body proportions without establishing longer sclerotized legs; keep condition distinct from structural size.
- Signpost body.size for whole-body dimensions and masses, life.molting for stage counts and ecdysis, and life.mate-search for reproductive consequences.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- A same-scale male/female western-widow pair with only front patella and tibia highlighted, plus a ratio annotation explicitly naming those segments; retain the male's slender, patterned appearance.
- A conceptual species-separated growth plot for brown and southern widows with unnumbered axes unless primary coefficients are obtained; mark molt steps and label the smooth curves as fitted summaries, not continuous cuticle elongation.

### Claims to avoid

- No widow receptor-neuron total, limb-wide innervation census, sensory regeneration atlas or dedicated gravity organ was verified.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- A brown-widow leg map with distal locations for tibial trichobothria, metatarsal organ and dorsal tarsal organ; leave the disputed proximal organ placement unresolved until checked, and mark all counts with the five-female scope.
- A western-widow tarsal SEM-style inset distinguishing a blunt open-tipped presumed chemical hair from an ordinary pointed hair; no invented pore dimension, neuron count or airflow threshold.

[randall1981]
- **Randall 1982:** [Original four-page follow-up](https://www.americanarachnology.org/journal-joa/joa-all-volumes/detail/article/download/JoA_v10_p247.pdf/?no_cache=1), pp. 248–249, full text. Five of twenty survived to later instars, but a nearby claim of 90% mortality in four days is inconsistent if applied to the same denominator. Do not reconstruct a survival schedule or imply the experiment measured locomotion. [randall1982]
- **Comparative costs and regeneration:** [Pasquet et al., primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21533554/) gives the Zygiella field interval and assay outcomes without full sample metadata; [Vollrath, Tables 2–4 and regeneration discussion](https://britishspiders.org.uk/system/files/library/080603.pdf) was accessible in full. Neither fills the widow performance gaps. [pasquet2011; vollrath1990]
- **Modern comparator gait:** [Kane et al., Methods, Figs 1 and 4–7](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12211592/), full text. Five juvenile Davus were followed across treatments, with repeated trials; data points/strides are not independent animals. Cold-anesthesia controls matter when interpreting early slowing. This is a comparative illustration, not a widow gait specification. [kane2025]
- **Growth:** [Barrantes et al., 2026 primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42117401/) verifies species, sex and trajectory statements; full methods, coefficients and per-instar measurements were unavailable. ### Editorial boundaries and remaining measurements

- Preserve the source/class/confidence distinction in every published observation; do not drop taxon names when reducing copy for cards.
- Keep body.circulation's fluid/heart coverage, body.exoskeleton's material layers, body.size's body measurements, body.body-plan's appendage map and body.pedipalps' genital anatomy in their own cases.
- Use body.digestion.midgut for gut branches near leg bases, body.digestion.mouth-and-pump for the alimentary pump, body.digestion.water for water balance, body.digestion.fasting for starvation-related loss of locomotion, and body.digestion.first-meals for maternal leg sweeps. None warrants an extra legs hall.
- Construction, wrapping, navigation and male silk are applications linked to web.construction, web.wrapping, web.navigation and web.male-silk; sensory mechanisms link to the seven requested senses cases; development, search, courtship and aerial dispersal link to life.molting, life.mate-search, life.courtship and life.ballooning.
- Search gaps are outcomes of this pass, not claims that no publication exists: widow pressure/torque curves; complete widow muscle attachments from the 1959 plates; claw-tooth and auxiliary-bristle counts; formal pad-absence/glass assays; grip force, latch anatomy and inverted endurance; controlled ground/web gait and sprint data; field leg-loss denominators; injury-specific mating, capture and building costs; Randall's full 1981 treatment table; regenerated widow leg dimensions, strength, sensory equipment and function; instar-by-instar segment tables and sex-specific allometric coefficients.
- Image briefs are commissioning instructions, not finished illustrations or independent evidence. Anatomical artists should check original plates for each named taxon before rendering; unresolved structures should remain unlabelled rather than be filled from a convenient tarantula or orb-weaver image.
