- Evidence classes are **LATRODECTUS**, **OTHER THERIDIID**, **OTHER SPIDER**, **GENERAL SPIDER**, and **OTHER ARTHROPOD**. No comparative mechanical measurement becomes a widow measurement. No OTHER THERIDIID result is needed merely to fill a category.

- Historical names remain attached to historical preparations. Hagstrum's title says “L. mactans,” whereas Kaston discusses that work as L. hesperus; Whitehead and Rempel's historical “L. mactans” is not a newly verified modern southern-widow voucher. Retain the source name and identify the naming uncertainty. [hagstrum1968; kaston1970; whitehead1959]

### Priority qualifications for the current exhibit

1. **Retain the “3 sublayers of the leg epicuticle” card, with its original anatomical scope.** Hadley's primary abstract explicitly names outer membrane, outer epicuticle and dense homogeneous layer in L. hesperus walking legs; it also reports no observed cuticulin layer. The proposed concern is resolved in favour of the card, not evidence that all widow surfaces share that subdivision. [hadley1981]
2. **Retain the three principal regions as a useful section label.** Epicuticle, exocuticle and endocuticle were present in Hadley's examined regions; epidermis belongs below them, not inside that three-count. Do not import a universal insect cement/wax/cuticulin stack. [hadley1981]
3. **Keep the barrier claim, but identify the perturbation.** The approximately 200-fold result concerns solvent-scrubbed versus control/sham spiders, not naturally waxless widows; 45–51 °C is the interval of fastest permeability increase, not one measured wax melting point or a lethal-temperature threshold. [hadley1989]
4. **Qualify the sclerotization sentence.** “The new cuticle matures and stiffens after emergence” is safer than implying one verified reaction confined to the exocuticle; the insect quinone/laccase mechanism must be marked comparative and the precise widow pathway held. [politi2021; arakane2005; asano2019; campli2024]
5. **Retain soft abdomen versus stiff carapace/legs as a regional distinction.** It is not evidence for a hairless, wholly unsclerotized or uniformly elastic abdomen; local plates such as the epigynum are exceptions, and unfolding versus material stretch is not quantified for a feeding widow here. [australianmuseum2018; berendonck2002; barth1973]
6. **Keep the lipid-film observation on the carapace.** Do not move the measured preparation to the abdomen or turn film removal into an optical experiment demonstrating the cause of gloss. [hadley1981]
7. **Keep “characteristically shiny black” as appearance, not explanation.** HOLD claims that abdominal hair/scales are absent, that a lipid film alone produces the shine, or that widow gloss has a measured refractive index/reflectance curve; the colour studies test pigments in specified samples. [vetter2017; hsiung2015; hsiung2017]
8. **Qualify the hair observation by hair type.** Socketed mechanosensory setae transmit deflection, but a tarsal-comb bristle, an airflow hair and a tip-pore sensillum are not interchangeable devices; morphology-based taste assignments need that qualification. [barth2004; ross1979; parra2009]
9. **Do not add “all lost legs regrow at the next molt.”** Northern-widow autotomy and distal amputation gave different outcomes, while wound closure and replacement of a whole limb are different events. [randall1981; randall1982]
10. **Keep the existing exuvium illustration schematic and review pending.** Hagstrum locates the opening laterally above the coxae; do not invent a central dorsal zipper, depict the exuvium as a dead intact spider, or equate withdrawal duration with completion of material hardening. [hagstrum1968]

## 0. body.exoskeleton — The Exoskeleton

**Suggested kicker:** One covering, many material jobs.

**Suggested summary:** The widow's outer skeleton changes from a stiff supporting shell to pliable membranes, sensory interfaces and a water barrier. Twelve halls follow its construction, surface, renewal and repair, keeping direct widow evidence separate from the spiders and insects used for comparison.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: principally L. hesperus, L. geometricus and L. variolus, with L. tredecimguttatus and historical “L. mactans” where identified. OTHER SPIDER: principally Cupiennius salei for architecture and mechanics, Tegenaria atrica for the historical molt-cycle study, and Pardosa astrigera for wound repair. OTHER ARTHROPOD: Tribolium castaneum for experimentally tested tanning chemistry. GENERAL SPIDER: standard anatomical distinctions.

### OBSERVATIONS

- The western-widow cuticle contains a connected pore-and-wax-canal system. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus, electron microscopy** [hadley1981]
- Different cuticular regions perform different mechanical jobs. **established — GENERAL SPIDER, comparative materials synthesis** [politi2021]
- Injury closure does not necessarily replace a lost widow limb. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. variolus, surgery and regeneration experiments** [randall1981; randall1982]

These cards and observations orient the parent; the detailed anatomical distinctions belong in the halls.

### TERMS

- **Cuticle:** extracellular covering made by the epidermis.
- **Exoskeleton:** mechanically supporting outer covering and its specialisations.
- **Epidermis:** living cell layer beneath the cuticle.
- **Sclerotization:** cuticle hardening through changes in its protein matrix.
- **Exuvium:** shed cuticular covering; exuvia is also used, and exuviae is plural.

### Claims to avoid

- No verified micrometre thickness series for western-widow carapace, abdomen and leg is supplied in this pass.
- A waterproof barrier is not an impermeable seal; hardness, stiffness, toughness and shininess are different properties.
- Do not paint zinc enrichment across the entire widow or present a comparator's test curve as her performance.
- A membrane is still cuticle; the endosternite is a different, internal connective-tissue support.

## 1. body.exoskeleton.layers — Built in Layers

**Suggested kicker:** A surface, a laminate, a living maker.

**Suggested summary:** Start with the actual western-widow sections, then enlarge a comparative chitin–protein laminate. Counting the major regions is useful; understanding their arrangement is more useful than treating the covering as three identical sheets.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: L. hesperus cephalothorax, abdomen and walking-leg EM preparations. OTHER SPIDER: Cupiennius-based cuticular fibre research. GENERAL SPIDER: epidermis/cuticle distinction.

### OBSERVATIONS

- Hadley examined surface and transverse preparations from three western-widow body regions. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus, SEM/TEM** [hadley1981]
- The leg epicuticle subdivisions were outer membrane, outer epicuticle and dense homogeneous layer. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [hadley1981]
- No cuticulin layer was observed in those preparations. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus, method-qualified absence** [hadley1981]
- The procuticle contains chitin–protein fibres beneath a fibreless epicuticle. **established — OTHER SPIDER, C. salei material sections** [tadayon2020]
- Successive fibre directions can rotate through a laminate. **established — OTHER SPIDER, comparative electron microscopy** [barth1973]
- Epidermal cells secrete the extracellular cuticle. **established — GENERAL SPIDER** [politi2021]

### TERMS

- **Epicuticle:** outer cuticular region without the procuticle's chitin fibres.
- **Procuticle:** chitin-containing region beneath the epicuticle.
- **Exocuticle / endocuticle:** outer / inner named procuticular regions.
- **Lamella:** structural layer; specify how its repeat is defined.
- **Bouligand architecture:** helicoidal or rotating-plywood arrangement of fibre directions.
- **Assembly zone:** region at the epidermal surface where secreted components assemble.

### Claims to avoid

- HOLD an absolute L. hesperus thickness card: the accessible 1981 primary abstract supplies no thickness values or specimen denominator.
- Do not label epidermis as chitin or count it as a fourth cuticle region.
- A 180° repeat does not mean neighbouring sheets turn by 180°, nor does helicoidal mean alternating 90° plywood.
- Do not give every region identical thickness, cross-linking, pitch or fibre arrangement.
- Signpost body.size for body dimensions and body.chelicerae.fang-materials for the specialised fang section.

## 2. body.exoskeleton.waterproofing — The Waxed Shield

**Suggested kicker:** A thin barrier with a large effect.

**Suggested summary:** Surface lipids make the western widow's cuticle a much better water barrier. Temperature and experimental preparation change the measured permeability, so a body-surface flux is not interchangeable with whole-animal water loss.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: L. hesperus abdominal-cuticle experiments. OTHER ARTHROPOD: broader lipid-mixture terminology only; no imported beetle or scorpion performance values.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 45–51 | °C | Fastest permeability rise | L. hesperus temperature/transpiration summary | [hadley1989] |
| ≈200× | relative water-loss rate | Solvent treatment | Chloroform:methanol scrubbing versus control/sham | [hadley1989] |
| 30 / 0 | °C / % RH | A defined assay environment | Summary's whole-body/in-situ comparison conditions | [hadley1989] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Water permeability rose with temperature in the widow assay. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [hadley1989]
- Solvent scrubbing greatly increased measured water loss. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [hadley1989]
- Whole-animal and local-capsule rates differed without statistical significance. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [hadley1989]
- Excised cuticle was leakier than cuticle measured in place. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus** [hadley1989]
- The epidermis was not supported as an additional active efflux barrier. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus, authors' interpretation** [hadley1989]

### TERMS

- **Cuticular permeability:** ease of water passage through cuticle under defined conditions.
- **Surface lipid:** lipid at the external interface, not necessarily pure wax ester.
- **Hydrocarbon:** compound made only of carbon and hydrogen.
- **Wax canal / pore canal:** fine epicuticular route / larger cuticular canal system; distinguish from a gland duct.
- **In situ / in vitro:** measured in place / in an isolated preparation.
- **Relative humidity:** water-vapour saturation percentage at the stated temperature.

### Claims to avoid

- No absolute widow water-loss rate with area, time and vapour-pressure units was recovered; do not generate one from a fold change.
- No solvent ratio, specimen n, collection locality or exact lipid chain-length distribution is inferred from the accessible summary.
- “Critical/transition temperature” must not become a single measured lipid melting point, sensory threshold or survival maximum.
- HOLD claims that desert populations are more waterproof than urban/coastal populations and that widows outperform all insects or other arthropods.
- The 1978 primary chapter on widow permeability/lipid composition is a relevant inaccessible lead; its reference alone does not verify a lipid inventory.
- Signpost senses.heat-and-water, senses.tarsal-organ, body.digestion.water, web.site and genus.western for sensing, budgets and microclimate.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- **1536×1024 museum plate:** near-black background, cream linework; abdominal cuticle with intact versus experimentally disrupted lipid barrier, water-vapour arrows and a source-labelled treatment-ratio tile; no literal 200 water droplets or wax melting at one temperature.
- **1536×1024 museum plate:** local capsule, whole spider and isolated cuticle as three separate measurement setups; temperature strip marks 45–51 °C, without fabricated ordinate values or a desert-versus-city ranking.

### TERMS

- **Sclerite:** locally hardened cuticular plate.

- **Pleura:** lateral body-wall region between dorsal and ventral plates.
- **Compliance:** deformation produced per applied load in a specified geometry.
- **Pedicel:** narrow connection between prosoma and abdomen.
- **Strain:** relative deformation, distinct from force or displacement.

## 4. body.exoskeleton.sclerotization — Tanning the Armour

**Suggested kicker:** Hardness and blackness need separate evidence.

**Suggested summary:** Protein cross-links can change a cuticle's material properties, and pigments can change its appearance. The best-tested catechol tanning pathway comes from insects; widow pigment detection and comparative spider metal incorporation answer different questions.

**Taxa studied:** LATRODECTUS: L. hesperus black-hair Raman spectra and successive-molt colour descriptions. OTHER SPIDER: C. salei fang maturation. OTHER ARTHROPOD: T. castaneum RNA-interference experiments.

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14 / 6 | spider species / families | Melanin survey breadth | Includes L. hesperus black hairs; not 14 widow species | [hsiung2015] |
| ≈1380 / 1580 | cm⁻¹ | Eumelanin spectral bands | Raman interpretation; not emitted colours | [hsiung2015] |
| 2 | laccase-2 isoforms | An insect mechanism tested | T. castaneum; alternative splice products | [arakane2005] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Western-widow black hairs produced spectra consistent with eumelanin. **supported — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus, Raman spectroscopy** [hsiung2015]
- Female western widows darken across successive molts. **established — LATRODECTUS, L. hesperus, descriptive account** [vetter2017]
- Oxidised catechol derivatives can cross-link insect cuticular proteins. **established — OTHER ARTHROPOD, insect tanning chemistry** [arakane2005]
- Beetles with laccase-2 transcripts depleted failed to tan normally. **supported — OTHER ARTHROPOD, T. castaneum, RNA interference** [arakane2005]
- Histidine-rich protein deposition precedes zinc incorporation in comparative spider fangs. **supported — OTHER SPIDER, C. salei, post-ecdysis analyses** [politi2017]

### TERMS

- **Sclerotization:** chemical stiffening/hardening of the cuticular matrix.
- **Cross-link:** bond connecting molecular chains or binding sites.
- **Catechol:** aromatic molecule with adjacent hydroxyl groups.
- **Quinone:** oxidised reactive compound involved in insect tanning reactions.
- **Melanisation:** production or deposition of melanin pigments.
- **Eumelanin:** broad-band-absorbing dark pigment class.

### Claims to avoid

- HOLD a widow-specific sequence “tyrosine → dopamine → named quinone → hardened black abdominal wall.”
- Do not treat blackness as a hardness meter or pigment identification as a complete cuticle cross-link assay.
- Comparative reviews disagree in emphasis: Politi describes catechol hardening, whereas Asano and Campli stress incomplete chelicerate pathway evidence; retain that qualification rather than announcing a resolved widow mechanism. [politi2021; asano2019; campli2024]
- Suggested disulphide or alternative enzymatic cross-links are hypotheses here, not demonstrated substitutes in Latrodectus.
- Successive-instar darkening is not a measured minute-by-minute postmolt colour clock.
- Signpost body.colour/body.hourglass.pigment for pattern and chemical detail, and body.chelicerae.fang-materials for Zn–histidine coordination.

### IMAGE BRIEFS

- **1536×1024 museum plate:** near-black background, cream linework; separate boxes for widow black-hair pigment evidence, insect protein cross-linking and Cupiennius metal incorporation; label the taxon over every box and keep inferred pathways dashed.
- **1536×1024 museum plate:** matched cuticle network diagrams illustrating cross-link density separately from pigment amount; no artwork equating a darker panel with a measured stiffer widow.

### Claims to avoid
