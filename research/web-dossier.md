# Silk & Web — research dossier

Researched 22 November 2026. Scope: the thirteen proposed exhibits, four existing exhibits to enrich, and five further topic suggestions. Only this dossier is changed; the YAML below is a proposal, not an edit to the source registry.

## Reading this dossier

- Citation keys in brackets refer to `content/sources.yaml` or to the new entries at the end. DOI links in SOURCES resolve to the underlying publications; section-specific links provide useful full texts or table locators.
- **established** = directly documented, with convergent evidence where available; **supported** = a particular experiment, limited observation, or a well-supported interpretation; **debated** = a hypothesis or unresolved evolutionary/mechanistic explanation. Confidence applies to the sentence as qualified, not automatically to every widow species.
- **OTHER THERIDIID**, **OTHER SPIDER**, and **GENERAL/COMPARATIVE** explicitly mark evidence outside *Latrodectus*. A brown widow or redback is a widow, but neither is interchangeable with a western black widow.
- FIGURES are candidate museum fact cards, not illustrations. Each row separates value, unit, label, conditions, and citations. Qualitative values are used where the literature supports a description but no defensible numerical rate. Experimental sample sizes are labelled as such.
- Mechanical **stress** is force per cross-sectional area; **breaking force** depends on thread thickness. **Toughness** is energy per volume, not strength. Values from differently collected or conditioned silk are not universal species constants.

### Priority qualifications for the current exhibits

1. Widows build three-dimensional gumfoot cobwebs, never orbs. [blackledge2005; barrantes2010]
2. The spring energy in the *L. hesperus* capture system was attributed experimentally to the scaffold, while gumfoot threads deformed plastically. Replace an exclusive claim that the gumfoot's own elasticity powers the hoist. [argintean2006]
3. **Table 1** reports engineering values: decimal extensibilities are fractional strains; toughness units should be MJ m⁻³. The existing 996 ± 274 MPa engineering-stress label is correct. [blackledge2005, Table 1 and methods]
4. Anchor micrographs/counts principally concern *Parasteatoda tepidariorum* (formerly *Achaearanea*); widow scaffold discs were also sampled. [sahni2012, Figs 1–5 and methods]
5. Weather resistance requires humidity, substrate and loading qualifications. [sahni2011; ayoub2021]
6. TuSp1/ECP-1/ECP-2 are demonstrated constituents, not an exhaustive egg-case recipe: ECP3 and additional components were subsequently detected. [hu2006; shanafelt2021]

## 1. web.scaffold — The Scaffold

**Taxa studied:** adult/penultimate female *L. hesperus*; OTHER THERIDIID *P. tepidariorum* for comparative attachment work. [blackledge2005; blackledge2007; sahni2012]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1,069 ± 204 | MPa | Scaffold breaking stress | Female *L. hesperus*; engineering stress, mean ± SD, n=54 fibres; Table 1 | [blackledge2005] |
| 957 ± 194 | MPa | Dry gumfoot stress | Same females/conditions; n=47 fibres | [blackledge2005] |
| 90 | % | Scaffold resilience | *L. hesperus*; energy recovery at strains below 3%, not at rupture | [argintean2006] |
| 3 | dimensions | A spatial trap | *L. hesperus* cobweb architecture; not a planar capture spiral | [zevenbergen2008] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Dry supporting threads suspend sheets and gumfeet. **established** [blackledge2005]
- Similar scaffold/gumfoot tensile properties support a shared major ampullate origin. **supported** [blackledge2005]
- The scaffold stores recoverable energy that powers the gumfoot capture system in *L. hesperus*. **supported** [argintean2006]
- Scaffold threads can entangle flying prey. **supported** [sahni2012]
- Fed western widows can build dense tangles without distinct hunting sheets. **supported** [zevenbergen2008]
- Denser tangles may impede predators or announce their approach. **debated** [zevenbergen2008]
- Some adult widow webs contain localized sticky segments outside the gumfeet. **supported** [barrantes2010; ayoub2021]
- Feeding changes architecture and thickness without necessarily changing material strength. **supported** [blackledge2007]

### TERMS

- **Scaffold:** load-bearing supporting threads that suspend the cobweb. [blackledge2005]
- **Sheet:** approximately horizontal threads used during hunting. [zevenbergen2008]
- **Resilience:** the fraction of deformation energy recovered when a material unloads. [argintean2006]

### Claims to avoid

- Material strength, breaking force and anchor adhesion are distinct. [blackledge2005]
- “All threads above the gumfeet are always dry”: too absolute across stages and species. [barrantes2010]
- No widow aerial-interception efficiency was verified here.
- Predator protection remains a hypothesis in the feeding experiments. [blackledge2007; zevenbergen2008]

## 2. web.gumfoot.trip-lines — The Web as an Alarm

**Taxa studied:** female/male *L. hesperus*, *Acheta domesticus*, *Musca domestica*. OTHER SPIDER comparator: *Tegenaria agrestis* (Agelenidae). [vibert2014]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 31.82 ± 16.29 | Hz | Cricket vibration frequency | Dominant frequency on *L. hesperus* webs, measured at the female's usual position; Table 1, mean ± SD | [vibert2014] |
| 28.57 ± 17.41 | Hz | Fly vibration frequency | Same receiver-position measurements; distributions overlap | [vibert2014] |
| 3.27 ± 2.59 | mm s⁻¹ | Fly vibration amplitude | RMS vibrational velocity, not prey speed or a sensory threshold; Table 1 | [vibert2014] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Prey struggles generate vibrations that propagate through a widow's cobweb to the resident's position. **established** [vibert2014]
- Gumfoot contact both restrains prey and provides vibrational information about its presence. **supported** [zevenbergen2008]
- Females attacked high-amplitude vibration playbacks more readily than low-amplitude playbacks. **supported** [vibert2014]
- Low-amplitude playback suppressed predation regardless of prey/male source. **supported** [vibert2014]
- Redbacks approach disturbances, touch prey with a front leg, then orient to apply silk. **supported** [forster1995]
- Prey movement influences the amount and sequence of redback silk application. **supported** [forster1995]
- Spider slit sensilla detect cuticular strain associated with mechanical stimulation. **established — GENERAL SPIDER anatomy** [barth2004]
- Widow navigation also uses remembered movement information, so web-borne sensing does not explain every journey. **supported** [sergi2021]

### TERMS

- **Substrate-borne vibration:** mechanical motion carried through a solid structure, here silk. [vibert2014]
- **RMS amplitude:** root-mean-square magnitude of a vibration measurement; the study reports vibrational velocity. [vibert2014]
- **Slit sensillum:** a cuticular slit-shaped mechanoreceptor sensitive to deformation. **GENERAL SPIDER** [barth2004]

### Claims to avoid

- A single “prey frequency” or universal attack threshold: frequency distributions overlap, and amplitude matters. [vibert2014]
- Calling web vibrations airborne hearing, or assigning *Cupiennius* receptor thresholds to widows. [barth2004; vibert2014]
- “Every contact causes an immediate rush”: one captive redback often waited before approaching. [vanhelsdingen2019]

## 3. web.egg-sac-silk — A Composite Nursery

**Taxa studied:** female *L. hesperus* for proteins, fibre classes, and water transport; *L. mactans* and *L. variolus* for comparative sac production; *L. geometricus*/*L. pallidus* for defensive comparisons. OTHER SPIDER: *Argiope aurantia* in diffusion experiments. [hu2006; vasanthavada2007; karkosiak2025; kaston1970; arabesky2025]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 184 | amino acids | TuSp1 repeat length | *L. hesperus* sequence characterized in the original protein study | [hu2005] |
| 2 | fibre diameter classes | A composite covering | *L. hesperus* large core fibres and finer fibres; not “exactly two anatomical layers” | [vasanthavada2007] |
| 11.8 | kDa | Predicted ECP3 mass | *L. hesperus* translated precursor; protein detected in egg-case material | [shanafelt2021] |
| 21 | sacs per female | Recorded production maximum | *L. hesperus* record summarized from Kaston; not a typical clutch count or universal maximum | [kaston1970; ebeling1975] |
| 10 / 6 | sacs per female | Other American widow records | Reported maxima for *L. mactans* / *L. variolus*, respectively; historical series | [kaston1970; ebeling1975] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Large-diameter western widow egg-case fibres contain TuSp1, ECP-1, and ECP-2. **established** [hu2006]
- ECP-1 and ECP-2 have cysteine-rich regions and differ from canonical repeat-rich spidroins. **established** [hu2006; shanafelt2021]
- Aciniform spidroin occurs in the sac's fine fibres and in prey-wrapping silk. **established** [vasanthavada2007]
- ECP3 is an additional constituent, predominantly expressed in tubuliform glands. **supported** [shanafelt2021]
- TuSp1, ECP-1, and ECP-2 transcription increases before egg-case production at different developmental times. **supported** [casem2010]
- Western widow sacs enclose an egg mass, an intervening air space, and an outer silk membrane. **supported** [karkosiak2025]
- Porous silk can conserve water by shielding internal stagnant air from wind rather than acting as an impermeable film. **supported** [karkosiak2025]
- Recombinant TuSp1 fibre extensibility increased when the conserved C-terminal domain was retained. **supported — SYNTHETIC FIBRES** [gnesa2012]
- Increased drawing strengthened synthetic tubuliform fibres while reducing extensibility. **supported — SYNTHETIC FIBRES** [gnesa2012]
- Brown widow sac projections hindered parasitoid oviposition in a removal experiment. **supported — L. geometricus only** [arabesky2025]

### TERMS

- **Tubuliform silk:** female reproductive silk associated with egg-case construction; also called cylindrical silk. [hu2005; casem2010]
- **ECP:** egg-case protein; ECP-1, ECP-2, and ECP3 are identified components, not synonyms for TuSp1. [hu2006; shanafelt2021]
- **Porosity:** the fraction of a membrane occupied by open space. [karkosiak2025]
- **Postspin drawing:** stretching a newly made synthetic fibre to modify molecular alignment and mechanics. [gnesa2012]

### Claims to avoid / quantitative gap

- Do not equate fibre diameter classes, microscopic coatings, sac wall layers, and the air gap; these describe different scales. [vasanthavada2007; karkosiak2025]
- No defensible native *Latrodectus* whole-sac strength/modulus/toughness figure was verified for publication here. The [2001 undergraduate abstract](https://scholarlycommons.pacific.edu/purcc/2001/events/12/) has suspect stress units; do not silently repair them or promote them to established values.
- Recombinant TuSp1/ECP-2 measurements are not native fibre or whole-sac measurements. [gnesa2012]
- Avoid “waterproof,” “sterile,” “UV-proof,” and “parasite-proof”; the diffusion experiment and observed egg-sac enemies do not establish those protections. [karkosiak2025; cowles2025]
- ECPs as particular molecular crosslinkers remain a mechanistic interpretation; co-detection does not establish a fixed 1:1:1 stoichiometry. [hu2006]

## 4. web.retreat — Where She Waits and Feeds

**Taxa studied:** *L. hesperus*, *L. hasselti*, *L. geometricus*, *L. revivensis*; shrikes (*Lanius excubitor*) tested with mealworm surrogates in widow nests. [zevenbergen2008; forster1995; benjamin2003; konigswald1990]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ~⅔ | shrub height | Retreat position | *L. revivensis* field nests; general tendency, with seasonal variation | [lubin1991] |
| ~10 / ~90 | % debris cover | Light and dense cover | *L. revivensis* nest treatments in captive shrike trials | [konigswald1990] |
| 20/20 / 1/17 | successful trials | Cover reduced prey retrieval | Lightly / densely covered nests; mealworms substituted for spiders; not widow survival percentages | [konigswald1990] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Western widow hunting sheets commonly emerge from retreats in crevices. **supported** [zevenbergen2008]
- Brown widows construct silk-lined peripheral retreats. **established** [benjamin2003]
- Desert widow retreats can be exposed cones covered with debris rather than hidden holes. **established** [konigswald1990]
- *L. revivensis* adds debris and dense silk to its nest over successive nights. **supported** [lubin1991]
- Debris-covered nests reduced shrike access to surrogate prey in captive experiments. **supported** [konigswald1990]
- Redbacks cut wrapped prey free and carry it back to their lair to feed. **supported** [forster1995]
- A captive redback stored consumed prey remains around its hiding place. **supported** [vanhelsdingen2019]
- The earliest *L. mirabilis* nymphal webs lacked a peripheral retreat and provided a central resting site. **supported** [barrantes2010]

### TERMS

- **Retreat:** a protected resting site connected to the capture web. [benjamin2003]
- **Crypsis:** concealment that reduces detection by an observer or predator. [konigswald1990]
- **Prey transport:** moving a subdued prey package from capture location to feeding location. [forster1995]

### Claims to avoid

- Universal central resting, tubular retreats or feeding at capture points. [benjamin2003; barrantes2010; forster1995]
- Treating the shrike experiment as a direct live-widow mortality test or as proof against wasps. [konigswald1990]
- Claims of a particular retreat silk protein mixture or insulation rating were not verified here; conical shape alone does not identify gland origin.

## 5. web.dragline — The Safety Line

**Taxa studied:** female *L. hesperus*; broader spider comparisons. Steel/Kevlar values are compiled benchmarks. [blackledge2005; madurga2016; boutry2010; gosline1999]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 996 ± 274 | MPa | Dragline breaking stress | Female *L. hesperus*, forced MA; engineering, mean ± SD, n=52 | [blackledge2005] |
| 0.33 ± 0.06 | mm mm⁻¹ | Breaking extension | Same fibres; engineering strain, equivalent to 33 ± 6% | [blackledge2005] |
| 194 ± 58 | MJ m⁻³ | Dragline toughness | Same fibres; energy per initial volume | [blackledge2005] |
| 1.5 | GPa | High-tensile steel reference | Approximate literature value compiled for relative scale; unspecified grade, not a matched experiment | [gosline1999] |
| 3.6 | GPa | Kevlar 49 reference | Approximate tensile-strength benchmark in the same comparative table | [gosline1999] |
| 50 / 6 | MJ m⁻³ | Kevlar 49 / steel toughness | Illustrative literature benchmarks, not present-day specifications or weight-normalized values | [gosline1999] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Major ampullate silk supplies lifelines and structural threads. **established** [blackledge2005]
- Western widow tensile performance overlaps published orb-weaver measurements. **supported** [blackledge2005]
- MaSp1 and MaSp2 are major dragline constituents, but the fibre contains additional proteins. **established** [ayoub2007; larracas2016]
- Proteomics also detected AcSp1 and several small proteins in western widow dragline material. **supported** [larracas2016]
- Water treatment followed by drying produces a maximum-supercontracted reference state in western widow dragline experiments. **supported** [madurga2016]
- Unrestrained wet silk can shorten, whereas restrained silk develops contraction stress. **established — GENERAL/COMPARATIVE SPIDER evidence** [boutry2010]
- Supercontraction varies among spider lineages and is not restricted to orb-web builders. **supported — GENERAL/COMPARATIVE** [boutry2010]
- High extensibility allows silk to absorb considerable energy even when a comparison material has higher tensile strength. **established — GENERAL MATERIALS principle** [gosline1999]

### TERMS

- **Engineering strain:** extension divided by original length. [blackledge2005]
- **Toughness:** work required to break a material per unit volume. [gosline1999]
- **Supercontraction:** water-induced shortening or, if shortening is prevented, generation of tension. [boutry2010]
- **Major ampullate / MA:** the silk-gland class that produces the principal dragline material. [chaw2018]

### Claims to avoid

- “Five times stronger than steel” without density, steel grade, silk condition, and comparison basis; the numbers above are stress and energy density. [gosline1999]
- Universal superiority to Kevlar contradicts these benchmarks. [gosline1999]
- A universal “50% widow shrinkage” or humidity threshold: no species-specific numerical shrinkage card is proposed from the comparative abstract. [boutry2010]
- Universal superiority to orb-weaver silk; hydration and processing matter. [madurga2016]

## 6. web.wrapping — Silk Before the Bite

**Taxa studied:** female *L. hesperus* for AcSp/MiSp chemistry; *L. geometricus* for comparative wrapping kinematics; *L. hasselti* for attack observations, including one captive adult in van Helsdingen's report. [vasanthavada2007; lamattina2008; barrantes2007; vanhelsdingen2019]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IV | leg pair | The silk-throwing comb | Theridiid tarsal comb; widow application, not an extra appendage | [miller2007; hanp2000] |
| ~300 | kDa | Aciniform protein band | Apparent mass of *L. hesperus* AcSp1-like material; gel estimate | [vasanthavada2007] |
| 1–2 | cm | Wrapping distance | One captive female redback's general attack pattern; not a population mean | [vanhelsdingen2019] |
| 35 | % movements | Simultaneous hind legs | *L. geometricus*: Table II, 329 movements across four prey sequences; alternate movements also occurred | [barrantes2007] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Serrated tarsal bristles on leg IV help theridiids cast sticky silk onto prey. **established** [miller2007]
- Western widow prey-wrap material contains aciniform spidroin. **established** [vasanthavada2007]
- Minor ampullate fibroin is also a demonstrated constituent of western widow prey wrapping. **established** [lamattina2008]
- Brown widows use both simultaneous and alternating movements of the fourth legs during wrapping. **supported** [barrantes2007]
- Redbacks can alternate biting with further applications of sticky and dry wrapping silk. **supported** [forster1995]
- Torpid prey may be bitten before movement triggers wrapping, so attack order is flexible. **supported** [forster1995]
- One captive redback repeatedly immobilized offered *Vespula vulgaris* wasps with silk before biting. **supported** [vanhelsdingen2019]
- That redback transported wrapped prey toward its hiding place after cutting surrounding threads. **supported** [vanhelsdingen2019]

### TERMS

- **Attack wrapping:** applying silk to restrain prey during its capture. [barrantes2007]
- **AcSp1:** aciniform spidroin identified in widow wrapping and fine egg-sac fibres. [vasanthavada2007]
- **MiSp:** minor ampullate spidroin, including a protein experimentally identified in widow prey-wrap silk. [lamattina2008]
- **Swathing:** covering prey with successive silk applications. [lamattina2008]

### Claims to avoid

- “All wrapping is pure AcSp1,” “wrapping silk is just venomous glue,” or an unverified fixed AcSp:MiSp ratio. [vasanthavada2007; lamattina2008]
- Molecular detection in collected wrap material does not identify every strand's composition at every moment of an attack. [lamattina2008]
- “Widows always bite first” and “widows never bite first” both erase prey-dependent sequences. [forster1995]
- Do not transfer the extreme compressive killing measurements from uloborid spiders to widows; the wasp observations establish capability, not a comparative injury-risk reduction. [barrantes2007; vanhelsdingen2019]

## 7. web.construction — Building an Accumulating Trap

**Taxa studied:** *L. geometricus* construction; female *L. hesperus* fibres/investment; *L. revivensis* and *L. hasselti* maintenance. [benjamin2003; blackledge2005b; lubin1991; forster1989]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | fibres per gumfoot | Down and back | *L. hesperus*: paired fibres laid during descent and ascent | [blackledge2005b] |
| Night | activity period | Maintenance after dark | Field *L. revivensis*: repairs, sticky-thread renewal and relocation | [lubin1991] |
| Incremental | construction mode | Add and repair | *L. geometricus* included; no complete-replacement cycle observed | [benjamin2003] |
| 12 | h between censuses | Measuring a growing web | *L. hesperus* feeding experiment; observation interval, explicitly not a natural rebuilding interval | [toupin2022] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Brown widows reinforce structural threads and use existing silk during expansion. **supported** [benjamin2003]
- A brown widow descends, anchors a gumfoot, then returns upward. **supported** [benjamin2003]
- Brown widows connect gumfoot-building bouts by traversing structural threads. **supported** [benjamin2003]
- Brown widow construction includes building a silk-lined retreat. **supported** [benjamin2003]
- In western widows, down-and-up construction produces paired fibres of different diameters. **supported** [blackledge2005b]
- Desert widows renew sticky capture threads and repair their webs at night. **supported** [lubin1991]
- Starving redbacks can eventually lose the ability to spin, although existing webs still provide capture structure. **supported** [forster1989]
- A resource experiment found positive covariation between trap and structural thread investment, rather than obligatory substitution. **supported** [toupin2022]

### TERMS

- **Construction bout:** a continuous period of building activity. [benjamin2003]
- **Frame thread:** silk connecting the web to supports. [benjamin2003]
- **Maintenance:** adding or replacing web elements while retaining existing structure. [lubin1991; forster1989]

### Claims to avoid / energetics gap

- Daily complete rebuilding was not demonstrated. [benjamin2003; forster1989]
- House-spider construction rates are not brown widow measurements. [benjamin2003]
- No widow web-energy budget or recycling efficiency was verified; thread counts are not calorimetry. [toupin2022]
- Orb-spiral construction sequences do not describe widow building. [benjamin2003]

## 8. web.hunger — Dinner or Defence?

**Taxa studied:** female *L. hesperus*; 2008 juveniles were approximately two moults before maturity. [blackledge2007; zevenbergen2008; toupin2022]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ~2 | fold diameter | Thicker threads when fed | *L. hesperus*, fed versus fasted treatments; not a doubling of intrinsic silk strength | [blackledge2007] |
| 225 | % increase | Predicted breaking force | Author's inference from measured thread dimensions; not a whole-web predator-resistance measurement | [blackledge2007] |
| 112 | juvenile females | Testing hunting performance | Total *L. hesperus* used in the 2008 experiment; study sample size | [zevenbergen2008] |
| 3 | prey-abundance levels | A later test | *L. hesperus* resource experiment testing structural/trap allocation; methods context | [toupin2022] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Fed western widows produced more total web silk than fasted females in the 2007 experiment. **supported** [blackledge2007]
- Fed females allocated relatively more silk to support threads and less to sheets and gumfeet. **supported** [blackledge2007]
- Thread diameter changed more clearly than the silk material's tensile properties under those feeding treatments. **supported** [blackledge2007]
- A defensive benefit of greater support investment was proposed rather than directly measured against predators. **debated** [blackledge2007]
- Transferring spiders between webs separated the web builder's feeding history from the hunter's current motivation. **supported** [zevenbergen2008]
- Both fed and starved hunters performed better on webs built by starved spiders. **supported** [zevenbergen2008]
- In a later study, spiders producing more structural threads also produced more trap threads. **supported** [toupin2022]
- Consequently, an obligatory physiological trade-off between these two thread categories was not supported by that later experiment. **supported** [toupin2022]
- Protein-deprived western widows produced stickier capture threads under a separate dietary manipulation. **supported** [blamires2014]

### TERMS

- **Body condition:** resource state assessed relative to structural size, distinct from body size alone. [blackledge2007]
- **Behavioural plasticity:** a change in an individual's expressed behaviour across conditions. [zevenbergen2008]
- **Allocation trade-off:** increasing one investment at a cost to another under limiting resources. [toupin2022]

### Claims to avoid

- “Hungry spiders simply make bigger webs,” or “well-fed spiders stop spinning”; investment has multiple dimensions. [blackledge2007]
- Proven predator exclusion or obligatory support-versus-gumfoot substitution. [zevenbergen2008; toupin2022]
- Treating total fasting, protein deprivation, and low body condition as identical manipulations. [blackledge2007; blamires2014]

## 9. web.messages — Silk Carries More Than Prey

**Taxa studied:** *L. hesperus* and *L. hasselti*, with species-specific chemical and behavioural results. Pheromone chemistry does not establish identical cues across the genus. [scott2015b; fischer2025; stoltz2007]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ~3 | fold reduction | Fewer rival males | Field *L. hesperus*: male-reduced versus intact female webs; authors report “three times fewer” | [scott2015] |
| 58 | % males | Web reducers | *L. hesperus* courtship sample; proportion performing web reduction, not a genus-wide rate | [scott2012] |
| 3 | vibration signal types | A courtship repertoire | *L. hesperus*: abdominal tremulation, bounces, web plucks; 2021 study | [sivalinghem2021] |
| 2 | contact components | An expanded chemical message | *L. hesperus*: methyl ester plus corresponding acid tested in the 2025 study; not necessarily the complete blend | [fischer2025] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Female western widow silk bears a chemically identified acylated serine methyl ester that contributes to male courtship stimulation. **established** [scott2015b]
- A second contact component and a courtship-active synthetic blend were demonstrated subsequently. **supported** [fischer2025]
- Synthetic isobutyric acid attracted male western widows in a field experiment. **supported** [fischer2025]
- Western widow web pheromone abundance peaked during summer in a year-long field sampling study. **supported** [fischer2025]
- Courting western widow males cut and bundle female silk while adding their own silk. **established** [scott2015]
- Male-reduced webs attracted fewer rivals, whereas comparable experimental silk removal did not reproduce the effect. **supported** [scott2015]
- Adding male silk alone did not reduce intact webs' attractiveness in that field experiment. **supported** [scott2015]
- Web-reducing males elicited fewer aggressive responses and more rapid female quiescence in a courtship study. **supported** [scott2012]
- Redback males distinguished virgin-female silk extracts from extracts produced immediately after mating. **supported** [stoltz2007]
- New silk produced by just-mated redbacks elicited no greater male interest than solvent controls in those assays. **supported** [stoltz2007]
- Western widow males combine vibration elements into more stereotyped sequences during later courtship stages. **supported** [sivalinghem2021]
- Low-amplitude male vibrations are less likely to elicit a female predatory response during early courtship. **supported** [vibert2014]

### TERMS

- **Contact pheromone:** a chemical encountered on silk that elicits a conspecific behavioural response after contact. [scott2015b]
- **Airborne sex attractant:** a volatile chemical that guides potential mates toward its source. [fischer2025]
- **Web reduction:** courtship cutting, bundling and silk addition that reduces a female's exposed web. [scott2015]
- **Tremulation:** body oscillation that generates substrate-borne vibrations. [sivalinghem2021]

### Claims to avoid

- “Males detect virginity by sight” or “silk carries only structural information.” [stoltz2007; fischer2025]
- “Web reduction works merely by removing half the web” or “male silk alone repels rivals.” [scott2015]
- “Every male reduces webs” is contradicted by the observed proportion. [scott2012]
- “Mated females of all widow species permanently cease signalling” exceeds the immediate-postmating redback experiment. [stoltz2007]
- “Widow courtship vibrations lack structure”: that characterization of early signals does not describe the later sequences recorded in 2021. [vibert2014; sivalinghem2021]

## 10. web.evolution — A Cobweb's Orb-Weaving Heritage

**Taxa studied:** *L. geometricus*, *L. mirabilis*, putative *L. hesperus* (identification uncertain); broader Theridiidae/Araneoidea comparisons. [barrantes2010; blackledge2009; fernandez2018; coddington2019]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | major web types | Theridiid diversity | Benjamin–Zschokke comparative classification; OTHER THERIDIIDS included, not four widow web types | [benjamin2003] |
| 159 | spider species | Phylogenomic sampling | Fernández et al. comparative dataset; not 159 widow species | [fernandez2018] |
| ~2,500 | genes | Testing the family tree | Same comparative analysis; sampling depth, not silk-gene count | [fernandez2018] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Theridiidae belongs within Araneoidea, alongside lineages that construct orb webs. **established — GENERAL/COMPARATIVE** [blackledge2009; fernandez2018]
- Cobweb architecture is interpreted as derived from orb-weaving ancestry in theridiid evolutionary studies. **supported — EVOLUTIONARY INFERENCE** [blackledge2009; opell2024]
- Gumfoot traps capture crawling prey using a localized adhesive region and a releasing substrate anchor. **established** [opell2024]
- Aggregate adhesive production links widow capture chemistry to the silk toolkit of other araneoids. **supported — COMPARATIVE** [ayoub2021]
- Young widow webs can have central discs and more orderly radial organization than adult webs. **supported** [barrantes2010]
- Juvenile organization suggests architectural homology, without being an orb. **supported — EVOLUTIONARY INFERENCE** [barrantes2010]
- The number and timing of orb-web origins across all spiders remain sensitive to phylogeny and character coding. **debated — GENERAL/COMPARATIVE** [fernandez2018; coddington2019]
- Recent comparative measurements associate cobweb capture biomechanics with softer, less tough glue proteins than orb-web glues. **supported — GENERAL THERIDIID COMPARISON** [opell2024]

### TERMS

- **Araneoidea:** the spider superfamily containing theridiids and several orb-weaving lineages. [blackledge2009]
- **Homology:** similarity attributed to shared ancestry rather than independent functional resemblance. [coddington2019]
- **Ancestral-state reconstruction:** inference of past traits by mapping present traits onto a phylogeny. [coddington2019]
- **Derived trait:** a character changed from its ancestral condition. [barrantes2010]

### Claims to avoid

- “Cobwebs are primitive chaos that evolved into orbs”: it reverses the inferred theridiid direction. [blackledge2009]
- Baby-widow orbs or literal evolutionary replay during development. [barrantes2010]
- A settled date for the first gumfoot, a single undisputed number of orb origins, or a proven one-to-one transformation of every orb element. [fernandez2018; coddington2019]
- “All cobweb glues lost proteins or hygroscopic salts”; western widow chemistry retains both. [jain2015]

### OBSERVATIONS

- Western widows preferentially established webs at rocks bearing cricket chemical cues in outdoor enclosures. **supported** [johnson2011]

- Desert widows moved to larger shrubs as they grew and passed through moults. **supported** [lubin1993]
- Relocation was associated with increased mortality in the marked desert widow population. **supported** [lubin1993]

### TERMS

- **Microhabitat:** local physical and biological conditions at the scale of an individual web site. [johnson2011]
- **Refugium:** a sheltered location used as the resident's retreat. [vetter2016]
- **Site fidelity:** continued occupation of a site after initial selection. [vetter2016]
- **Kairomone:** an organism's chemical cue exploited by another species, such as prey residues used by a predator. [johnson2011]

### Claims to avoid

- “Widows always live indoors,” “all widows choose darkness,” or “all webs are at ground level”: species and site contexts differ. [vetter2016; lubin1991; ebeling1975]
- “Females never relocate” and a universal residence duration are both unsupported. [lubin1993]
- Do not turn preference among offered cardboard refuges into a habitat requirement or assume brown widow choices equal western widow choices. [vetter2016]

**Taxa studied:** *L. hesperus* with *Argyrodes pluto*, *Neospintharus baboquivari*, and *Mimetus*; *L. mactans* in mud-dauber prey samples; *L. hasselti* in a documented skink record. OTHER THERIDIID host: *Tidarren sisyphoides*. [cowles2025; dean1988; oshea2017]

### OBSERVATIONS

- Western widows consumed ground-active arthropods from several orders in a year-long coastal study. **established** [salomon2011]
- *Argyrodes pluto* stole western widow egg sacs and fed on eggs and spiderlings. **supported** [cowles2025]

- Egg-sac thieves exploited intervals when the host was occupied with prey wrapping. **supported** [cowles2025]
- *Mimetus* occurred in western widow webs, but the study did not observe these intruders killing their hosts. **supported** [cowles2025]

- The Arizona investigators did not document *A. pluto* killing adult western widows. **supported** [cowles2025]

### TERMS

- **Kleptoparasitism:** taking resources acquired or maintained by another animal. [cowles2025]
- **Araneophagy:** eating spiders. [cowles2025]
- **Parasitoid:** an organism whose developing offspring exploit and ultimately kill their host. [arabesky2025]
- **Provisioning:** stocking a nest with food for offspring, as mud-daubers do with immobilized spiders. [dean1988]

### Claims to avoid

- “All visitors are prey” or “all kleptoparasites eat only scraps.” [cowles2025]
- Do not present *Mimetus* occurrence as an observed widow kill or assume every visitor is a harmless commensal. [cowles2025]
- “Blue mud-daubers are the principal predator everywhere” exceeds a local prey-content survey. [dean1988]
- A photographed vertebrate entangled in silk does not alone establish capture, killing, consumption, or the spider's identification; use documented case reports. [oshea2017; nyffeler2021]
- Large vertebrate prey records do not establish a routine dependence on vertebrates. [salomon2011; nyffeler2021]

## 14a. web.gumfoot — Enriching the Spring-Loaded Trap

**Taxa studied:** female *L. hesperus*; OTHER THERIDIID *P. tepidariorum* for much anchor microstructure. [blackledge2005b; argintean2006; sahni2012]

### OBSERVATIONS

- Gumfoot lines couple a supporting web to a surface traversed by prey. **established** [blackledge2005b]
- The basal adhesive catches prey while the line above provides a mechanical connection. **established** [blackledge2005b]
- Release of the weak attachment converts the taut arrangement into a lifting trap. **supported** [sahni2012]
- In western widow experiments, recoverable scaffold deformation supplied the lifting energy. **supported** [argintean2006]
- Glue-coated portions of a western widow gumfoot are less stiff and more extensible than dry portions. **supported** [blackledge2005b]
- Gumfoot number and arrangement change during widow development. **supported** [barrantes2010]

### TERMS

- **Pre-tension:** tensile loading already present before prey makes contact. [argintean2006]

### Claims to avoid

- “A gumfoot always lifts any prey completely off the ground”; outcome depends on the interacting prey and trap. [argintean2006; sahni2012]
- “The sticky base is a separate, orb-like capture silk”: glue changes an underlying strong fibre's behaviour without making it an orb spiral. [blackledge2005b]
- Universal gumfoot adhesive lengths. [zevenbergen2008; barrantes2010]

## 14c. web.gumfoot.glue — More Than a Droplet of Glue

**Taxa studied:** *L. hesperus*, with OTHER THERIDIID *P. tepidariorum* and OTHER SPIDER orb-weaver comparisons. [sahni2011; jain2015; ayoub2021]

### FIGURES

| Value | Unit | Short label | Qualifier / locator | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15–90 | % RH | Humidity range tested | Western widow glue's elasticity and adhesion changed little in these experiments; not all weather | [sahni2011] |

### OBSERVATIONS

- Western widow gumfoot glue contains water-insoluble glycoproteins and hygroscopic organic salts. **established** [jain2015]
- Organic salts contribute to adhesion even though widow glue changes less with humidity than tested orb glues. **supported** [jain2015]
- The 2011 droplet experiments described western widow glue as behaving like a viscoelastic liquid. **supported** [sahni2011]
- Later proteomics detected aggregate spidroins and additional protein families in western widow gumfoot material. **supported** [ayoub2021]
- AgSp1 showed abundant glycosylation in both widow and house-spider samples. **supported** [ayoub2021]
- AgSF2 was abundant in western widow gumfoot samples and is distinct from AgSp2. **supported** [ayoub2021]
- Protein deprivation increased western widow thread stickiness without increasing its glue droplet size. **supported** [blamires2014]
- The chemical mechanism producing that diet effect was proposed, not directly identified. **debated** [blamires2014]

### TERMS

- **Glycoprotein:** a protein bearing attached carbohydrate groups. [jain2015; ayoub2021]
- **Hygroscopic:** tending to absorb water from the atmosphere. [jain2015]
- **Viscoelastic:** exhibiting both time-dependent flow and recoverable elastic response. [sahni2011]
- **AgSp / AgSF:** aggregate spidroin / aggregate silk factor; different protein names, not interchangeable abbreviations. [ayoub2021]

### Claims to avoid

- “Widow glue contains no glycoproteins or salts”: later composition work contradicts that inference from early droplet appearances. [jain2015]
- “Humidity-independent” should mean relatively invariant over tested conditions, not immunity to rain, temperature or aging. [sahni2011]
- Do not assign all proteins in a glue-plus-fibre sample to the adhesive itself. [ayoub2021]
- “One uniform protein droplet” or “pure liquid with no elasticity” overstates the rheological description. [sahni2011; ayoub2021]

## 14d. web.silk-proteins — A Sequence Is Not the Whole Recipe

### OBSERVATIONS

- Western widow MaSp1 and MaSp2 have large repetitive interiors bounded by nonrepetitive terminal regions. **established** [ayoub2007]
- MaSp1 contains glycine-rich motifs interspersed with polyalanine runs. **established** [ayoub2007]
- The western widow MaSp2 sequence includes additional proline-containing and glutamine-rich motifs. **established** [ayoub2007]
- Polyalanine-rich crystalline regions contribute to strength, while less ordered regions permit extension. **supported — GENERAL STRUCTURE–FUNCTION interpretation** [gosline1999]

### TERMS

- **Motif:** a recurring short amino-acid pattern within a protein. [ayoub2007]
- **Poly-A:** consecutive alanines, distinct from RNA poly(A). [ayoub2007]
- **Ensemble repeat:** a larger repeating unit assembled from shorter sequence motifs. [ayoub2007]
- **Proteomics:** identification and measurement of proteins in a biological sample. [larracas2016]

### Claims to avoid

- “Exactly two proteins make all widow silk” or “two dragline proteins are the entire fibre.” [larracas2016]
- “Each gene is an exclusive gland barcode,” or “RNA expression alone proves incorporation into a finished thread.” [clarke2017; larracas2016]
- Generic orb-weaver motifs do not describe every widow sequence. [ayoub2007]
- “More MaSp2 automatically means tougher silk” needs matched compositional and mechanical evidence; motif models are not universal conversion formulas. [gosline1999; clarke2017]

## 15. Further exhibit topics — five evidence-backed options

These are topic proposals, not fully commissioned exhibit entries; the evidence is sufficient to justify developing them separately.

1. **web.navigation — A remembered route home.** *L. hesperus* made directional errors after web manipulations and took shortcuts, supporting path integration rather than simple silk-trail retracing. **supported** [sergi2021]
2. **web.juvenile-architecture — Growing up.** *L. mirabilis* and putative *L. hesperus*: juvenile discs/radials differ from adult architecture. **supported** [barrantes2010]
3. **web.nursery-defence — The defended egg sac.** Brown widow spikes impeded parasitoids, and females increased spike density after parasitoid exposure; this is *L. geometricus*, not all black widows. **supported** [arabesky2025]
4. **web.male-silk — A wandering male's silk toolkit.** Mature *L. hesperus* and *L. geometricus* males expressed minor ampullate spidroin genes most strongly; courtship-silk function remains an interpretation. **supported** [correagarhwal2017]
5. **web.glue-diet — Feeding changes the adhesive.** Protein deprivation increased *L. hesperus* capture-thread stickiness without enlarging droplets; a useful chemical counterpart to architectural plasticity. **supported** [blamires2014]
